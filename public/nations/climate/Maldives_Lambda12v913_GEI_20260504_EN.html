§01

National Overview

The Maldives is a nation consisting of 26 atolls and 1,192 coral islands located near the equator in the Indian Ocean. Although it spans 820 km from north to south, its land area is only 300 km². A population of 520,000 lives scattered across 187 inhabited islands. The capital, Malé, is one of the most densely populated cities in the world.

The country with the lowest average elevation on Earth. More than 80% of the entire territory is located less than 1m above sea level. The world's highest point is 2.4m on Villingili Island. This is the highest point in the entire country.

§2 History and Culture
§02

History and Culture — What is Disappearing

The history of the Maldives dates back to before 500 BC. Dravidian peoples from Sri Lanka and South India first settled here, and later, Arab, African, and Malay merchants interacted to form a unique civilization. Crossroads of the Indian OceanThe Maldives was a key hub of the medieval East-West trade routes.

Originally a Buddhist nation, the Maldives converted to Islam in 1153. The Sultanate system lasted for 800 years, and the coral stone carving architecture, lacquerware craft, and traditional weaving techniques formed during this period are still passed down today. Malé's Hukuru Miskiy (Friday Mosque)is a 17th-century masterpiece built of coral stone.

Dhivehi — language spoken only in the Maldives.
A mixture of Sanskrit, Arabic, and Sri Lankan. It possesses its own unique script, Thaana.
Thaana is a right-to-left script derived from Arabic numerals.
If this language disappears, centuries of seafaring knowledge, fishing traditions, and folklore recorded in this script will disappear with it.

§4 Hulhumalé Project
§04

Hulhumalé — The City of Hope, Its Limits

Started in 1997, Hulhumalé is an artificial island built by dredging seabed sand 8km away from Malé. Standing 2m high — 1.3 times the Maldivian average. Target capacity is 240,000 people. It is evaluated as one of the most ambitious climate adaptation projects in the world.
