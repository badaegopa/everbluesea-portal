§01

Country Overview

The Marshall Islands is a country consisting of 1,200 islands and atolls located in the Central Pacific. Population 42,000. Maximum altitude of the territory is less than 2m. It is one of the lowest countries on Earth. And it is also the country that has suffered the most nuclear bomb detonations in history.

From 1946 to 1958, the United States conducted 67 nuclear tests on these atolls. The Castle Bravo test, with a yield 1,000 times that of the Hiroshima atomic bomb, detonated on this land. Before being submerged by climate change, nuclear bombs changed these islands first.

History of Nuclear Testing — "For the good of mankind"

"for the good of mankind" — Words spoken by a US Navy Admiral to 167 residents of Bikini in 1946
They left. Even after 20 years, even after 30 years, they could not return.
As of 2025, Bikini Atoll remains uninhabited.
March 1946
A US Navy Admiral requested the relocation of 167 Bikini residents. "Nuclear testing for peace". The residents left "with no choice".
1946–1958
67 nuclear tests. Total yield of 108 Megatons — equivalent to detonating one Hiroshima bomb every day for 20 years. 3 islands evaporated. Large underwater craters formed.
March 1, 1954
Castle Bravo — 1,000 times that of Hiroshima. 2.5 times stronger than expected. Radioactive fallout blanketed the residents of Rongelap and Utirik Atolls. They testified that "white snow resembling ashes" fell. Cancer rates surged.
1977–1980
Nuclear waste landfilling on Enewetak Atoll. Runit Dome construction — nuclear waste sealed with concrete. At the time of design, sea level rise was not considered at all.
Present, 2025–2026
Runit Dome cracking in progress. Sea level rise due to climate change — risk of dome base submersion. Scenario of nuclear waste leaking into the ocean becomes visible. US DOE report: "It is safe" — rebutted by the Marshall Islands government.

§3 Runit Dome
§03

Runit Dome — The Intersection of Nuclear and Climate

Runit Dome is a nuclear waste disposal facility constructed on Runit Island of Enewetak Atoll between 1977 and 1980. Hundreds of thousands of cubic meters of nuclear waste are stored under a concrete dome with a diameter of 115m. There is no waterproof lining under the dome. It is in direct contact with the soil.

§4 Culture and History
§04

Culture and History — Extinction of Matrilineal Society

Prior to nuclear testing, the Marshall Islands was a strong matrilineal society. Land was inherited through maternal lineages, and women held substantial power. The forced relocation of Bikini residents shook this matrilineal land ownership system to its core.

Testimony of a Marshallese:
"My grandmother was a clan leader of Bikini Atoll. Land comes down through grandmother. But grandmother was driven out as a child in 1946 and never got to return. I have never seen my land."

§6 Relationship with the United States
§06

Relationship with the United States — Incomplete Responsibility

The Marshall Islands was a US UN Trust Territory from 1947 to 1986. During that period, nuclear testing took place. After independence, the Marshall Islands and the United States signed the Compact of Free Association (COFA). However, nuclear compensation has always been controversial.
