Executive Summary

Kazakhstan ranks 1st in Central Asia with a series η of 0.5128, and its BSLI is +0.0913 (Grade E · 47th out of 139 countries), placing the grassroots base above the basic living line. The dimensions within the 2024 critical band (P ≥ 0.4458) are A5 and A7, and the maximum cross-sectional pressure is A5 · P=0.610 (measured: R&D/GDP). There are no missing dimensions.

The pressure on A5 (measured: R&D/GDP) is a 'static deficit' that has barely moved over a long period, whereas A7 Social Structure (measured: aging and population growth) is a 'highly active deficit' that fluctuates significantly, such as +0.1277 in 2021, -0.0660 in 2022, and -0.0549 in 2024. The two pressures differ in character.

On the political axis, following the January 2022 bloodshed (238 officially announced dead), the country continuously redesigned its institutions through the 2022 constitutional amendment, the 2024 nuclear power referendum, and the March 2026 new constitution (unicameral Kurultai, reinstatement of the vice presidency). The gap between official figures (high approval and turnout rates) and independent monitoring (OSCE: lack of political alternatives) illustrates the character of this redesign.

§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1986-12
Jeltoqsan (December Events) — When Soviet Communist Party General Secretary Gorbachev replaced the First Secretary of the Kazakh Republic with an ethnic Russian, youth protests broke out in Almaty and were suppressed. It remains the first memory in Kazakh political history of 'street protests' ending in violent suppression.
1991-12
Independence — Independence was declared during the dissolution of the Soviet Union. Nazarbayev ruled for the next 28 years as the first president, building a state structure based on resource revenues (oil, gas, and uranium).
2011-12
Zhanaozen Events — A prolonged strike by oil workers in the western Mangystau Region was suppressed, resulting in multiple deaths. The western oil region has since served as an ignition point for protests (the January 2022 protests also began in Zhanaozen).
2019-03
Power Transition — Nazarbayev resigned and Tokayev succeeded to the presidency. Nazarbayev maintained influence as Chairman of the Security Council and other roles (dual power).
2022-01
January Events (Qandy Qantar) — Protests against a surge in liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) prices in Zhanaozen spread nationwide. The government declared a state of emergency and requested CSTO troops. According to the final list from the Prosecutor General's Office, 238 people died. Former National Security Committee Chairman Massimov was arrested and sentenced to 18 years in prison in April 2023 on charges of high treason and attempted violent seizure of power (closed trial).
2022-06
Constitutional Referendum — According to preliminary tallies from the Central Referendum Commission, 77.18% voted in favor with a turnout of 68.06%, amending one-third of the provisions of the 1995 Constitution.
2022-11
Snap Presidential Election — Tokayev was elected with 81.31% (turnout 69.44%). The presidential term was changed to a single seven-year term.
2024-10
Nuclear Power Referendum — The proposal to build a nuclear power plant was approved with 71.12% in favor (turnout 63%) (Series Briefing).
2026-03~08
New Constitution and Unicameral Parliament — A new constitution amending 84% of the 1995 Constitution was adopted through a referendum on March 15 (turnout 73.12%, approval 87.15%) and came into effect on July 1. In the first 145-seat unicameral Kurultai election on August 23, the pro-presidential party Adilet won 110 seats, and on August 31, Erlan Karin was appointed to the reinstated post of Vice President (Series Briefing).
§ 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

A Continuous Institutional Redesign. According to the Country Analysis Series briefing (as of 2026-10-07), the 2026 new constitution replaced the bicameral legislature with a 145-seat unicameral Kurultai (party-list proportional representation, barring independent candidacies) and reinstated the office of Vice President. The president described this as a transition from a 'super-presidential system to a presidential system with an influential parliament,' but appointment powers for several high-level positions were transferred to the president, and a new 164-member People's Council appointed by the president was created. Provisions regarding the supremacy of international treaties over domestic law and the right to strike were eliminated (CEVRO University CAPS, Caspian Policy Center).

Tax System and Inflation. Under the new tax code on January 1, 2026, the value-added tax increased from 12% to 16% (adjusted from an initial draft of 20%, Thomson Reuters Regulatory Intelligence 2026-02). The Bureau of National Statistics announced annual inflation for November 2025 at 12.4%, and the National Bank cited regulated price increases (such as fuel) and VAT as inflation risks. In a DEMOSCOPE survey prior to implementation (late 2025), 61.4% of respondents replied that the VAT hike would degrade quality of life, and 41.6% replied that they did not trust government spending (reported by Orda.kz). Actual inflation following the tax increase could not be verified within the scope of this study.

Growth. The World Bank's Monthly Economic Update (2025-12) estimated real GDP growth for January–September 2025 at 6.3% year-on-year, and the National Bank's 2025 forecast range was 6.0–6.5%. The growth rate itself converges between official figures and international organization estimates — Kazakhstan's gap lies in political and human rights records rather than economic statistics.

Public Trends. The series briefing noted no reports of large-scale collective action within the coverage of referendums and parliamentary elections between 2024 and 2026. According to ACLED tallies, protests increased from 506 in 2019 → 814 in 2020 → 988 in 2021, and in 2022, 915 protests were recorded alongside 149 political violence events and 226 fatalities (concentrated around 97 events in January) (vault acled_link compilation).

High dimensions. A1 Infrastructure (measured: electricity and internet 0.9646), A12 Mobility (measured: FDI 0.8847), A3 (measured: Gini coefficient 0.8470), and A9 (measured: GPI and military expenditure 0.8283) pull η upward. While A3 is designated 'Civil Liberties,' the series engine's measured input is the Gini coefficient, meaning this high score signifies that 'the published income distribution indicator leans toward equality' rather than 'civil liberties are expansive' (interpreting the designation–measurement gap toward the measured side).

Low dimensions. A5 Education Access (measured: R&D/GDP 0.0247) is near the bottom of the empirical anchors across 197 countries, and A10 (measured: tourist arrivals 0.0173) and A4 Economic Stability (measured: GDP and manufacturing 0.2000) are also low. Typical of a resource-exporting country — the structure where manufacturing and research investment shares are small and the economy is tethered to commodity prices is directly reflected in the measured dimensions.

Absorber binary. A2 Institutional Trust (0.4067) and A8 Governance (0.4717) show intermediate values. In empirical pattern analysis, A2×A8 is the most tightly bound pair, moving in the same direction in the same year at a rate of 0.851. In 2024, the ΔP for both dimensions was -0.0123 and -0.0206, both moving toward departure (reduced pressure).

Consolidated fragment (integrating prior literature by dimension). ① A4·A5 (resource dependence · low R&D) → rentier state theory (Beblawi & Luciani, The Rentier State, 1987) and Ross (2001, "Does Oil Hinder Democracy?", World Politics 53(3)): Resource rents weaken the taxation–representation linkage and crowd out manufacturing and research investment — the applicability condition is the state finance's dependence on rents, and Kazakhstan is a case where the premise of the theory is shifting as it attempts to broaden its tax base, such as through the 2026 VAT increase. ② A2·A8 (Institutional Trust · Governance) → Schatz (2009, "The Soft Authoritarian Tool Kit", Comparative Politics 41(2)): 'Soft authoritarianism' maintained by agenda-setting and persuasion rather than coercion — January 2022 was the moment when this tool failed and shifted to coercion (request for CSTO), and the subsequent constitutional revision and referendum are read as a restoration of soft tools. Connection point: since the rent of ① is the funding source for the soft tools of ② (subsidies and price controls), the moment price controls are lifted (January 2022 LPG), both pieces are shaken simultaneously.

§ 09

Collapse Path Classification — WD / SN / BH

Path Classification Criteria (Canonical UI, Stellar Evolution Metaphor)
WD: E_A2 < 0.3, C_internal < 0.4 · SN: E_A2 0.3~0.5, C_internal > 0.5 · BH: E_A2 < 0.2, C_internal > 0.7
White Dwarf (WD) = suppression and latency · Supernova (SN) = collapse and regeneration · Black Hole (BH) = implosion and irreversibility
The decisive variable distinguishing SN from BH is C_internal (elite internal conflict energy). It is a metaphor and does not assert an astrophysical correspondence.

January 2022 is close to a case that reached the threshold of SN (collapse and regeneration) before retreating. Although C_internal (elite internal conflict) was temporarily high, the Presidential Administration absorbed the conflict through external troops (CSTO) and judicial proceedings (indictment of Massimov and others), consolidating power. The subsequent 2022 and 2026 constitutional revisions are interpreted as a process of institutionalizing this consolidation.

Current observations indicate that C_internal has declined and E_A2 (institutional-trust effect) is at an intermediate level, meaning the short-term path is closer to the conditions of WD (suppression and latency). However, A5 and A7 within the band and the thin BSLI margin leave a structure capable of rapid ignition like 2022 if price shocks press upon the grassroots base again. This is not a forecast, but a listing of observed conditions.

◐
WD — Managed Stability
Conditions: C_internal kept low, price shocks buffered
A path in which power centered on the Presidential Administration is maintained under the new constitutional system, and inflationary/tax pressures are absorbed through fiscal subsidies. Pressures from A5 and A7 within the band remain as they are.
✸
SN — Reorganization After Shock
Conditions: Price shock + recombination of elite fissures
A path where a coupling similar to January 2022 occurs again, leading to a short-term explosion followed by power realignment. There is a precedent from 2022.
●
BH — Implosion
Conditions: E_A2 < 0.2 + C_internal > 0.7
With the currently observed levels of institutional trust and governance and the degree of power centralization, no signals corresponding to this condition are visible.
