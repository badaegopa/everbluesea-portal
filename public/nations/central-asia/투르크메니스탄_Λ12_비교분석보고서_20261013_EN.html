§ 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

Signs of Cracks in Father-Son Co-Rule. Ibragimova (2026-10-07) of the Carnegie Russia Eurasia Center summarized the 2026 personnel reshuffles. Minister of National Security Atagarayev was dismissed citing corruption, succeeded by Meredov, former head of President Serdar's security detail. The head of the State Border Service, a figure from the Gurbanguly era, was also replaced, and Deputy Prime Minister for Oil and Gas Amanov's post was taken by Agajanov, head of the state oil company and a close confidant of Serdar. The leadership of Mary, Balkan, and Lebap provinces, two deputy prime ministers, and the ministers of health and culture were also replaced. While viewing the rivalry as discreet and unlikely to spill into open conflict, the analysis noted that escalating tensions pose the risk of both men losing power.

Official Narrative and Foreign Activities. Official government and embassy media continue to designate Gurbanguly as the 'National Leader, Chairman of the Halk Maslahaty', reporting on his visit schedule to Turkmenbashi, Balkan Province, on October 6, 2026. President Serdar met with President Trump in the 'Central Asia + US' format and paid a state visit to Azerbaijan in June 2026. The Carnegie analysis reported that Gurbanguly sought to attend the Trump-led 'World Summit' but was not invited — since this is a single analytical source, it is kept solely as 'reported content'.

Economy. Following its 2025 Article IV consultation mission (2025-06), the IMF stated that growth slowed from 4.5% in 2023 to 3.0% in 2024 due to sluggish hydrocarbon exports, recommending improvements in the availability, quality, and reliability of economic statistics. The official growth rate for the same period was 6.3% (cited from World Bank overview). The official growth rate for 2025 could not be confirmed in this survey.

Regime Assessment. BTI 2026 classifies Turkmenistan as a 'consolidated authoritarian state', noting that all changes in state apparatuses, including the judiciary and prosecution, as well as major projects across all societal sectors, require presidential decrees or supreme-level approval. In public trends, ACLED recorded 8 protests in 2020, 0 in 2021, and 2 each in 2022 and 2023, where the absence of records does not imply the absence of events (a data capture rate issue in information-controlled states).
