§ 01

Executive Summary

Bhutan's 12-dimensional state vector is uniformly high across the board. External Shocks (A11 0.9135), Inequality (A10 0.8951, Gini 28.5, measured 2022), Infrastructure (A1 0.7857), and Economic Stability (A4 0.7760) shore up the upper tier, while the two axes of the absorber—Institutional Trust (A2 0.6434) and Governance (A8 0.6986)—are also the highest in South Asia. Axiom A1 (Invariance of Alignment) posits that any pressure ultimately targets A2 and A8. Bhutan is a country where that target point holds firm with substantial thickness.

The weak spots are Environmental Pressure (A9 0.3886, ND-GAIN climate vulnerability/readiness) and Educational Access (A5 0.4615, adult literacy rate 64.9%, measured 2022). Social Structure (A7 0.6118) was determined solely by the voter turnout of 65.6% (2024 general election) due to the absence of a WVS trust survey.

The core observation of this report lies outside the numbers. The State of the Nation Report (2026) counted more than 71,000 citizens living or working abroad, roughly 10% of the population. While the official unemployment rate is low in the 3% range, youth unemployment is around 17%, and a significant portion of young people who could not find jobs have left for countries such as Australia. Part of Bhutan's low pressure figures can be read as the outcome of people under pressure having slipped outside the national borders. Under canonical determination, there is no uprising signal, and collapse path determination is withheld due to the absence of SE and C_internal.

§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Economy. The National Statistics Bureau (NSB) National Accounts 2025 announced real GDP growth of 7.5% for 2024 (driven by hotels/restaurants, public administration, and electricity — via BBS reports). The A4 input values in Canonical v2.3 (growth 7.5%, inflation 2.8%, unemployment 3.3%) are also international organization records for the same year. Gelephu Mindfulness City (GMC) is a special administrative region initiative on the border with India announced in 2023, followed by a $100 million bond issuance in 2024 and an announcement of a $1 billion crypto allocation in December 2025. The ADB presents this as a private-sector-led green investment and trade transformation initiative.

Labor and Migration. The NSB Labor Force Survey 2025 announced that the unemployment rate fell from 5.9% in 2022 to 3.4% in 2025. Quarterly indicators from the same agency show youth unemployment at 17.1% in Q1 2025, 17.8% in Q3, urban youth unemployment at 21.1%, and female youth unemployment in Q4 at approximately 26%. The State of the Nation report (2026) counted over 71,000 citizens living and working abroad (roughly 10% of the population), with AFP reporting that about 39,000 (55%) of them are in Australia. Remittance inflows in 2025 were approximately $343 million. The number of births over the past decade has dropped by more than a quarter, prompting the government to introduce allowances for third and subsequent children.

Politics. The January 2024 general election proceeded peacefully, with the People's Democratic Party (PDP) forming the government with 30 seats (Election Commission). ACLED records 1–2 incidents of political violence per year and 0 fatalities from 2020 to 2024. The issue of the southern Nepali-speaking population is rarely addressed on the domestic political agenda. Human Rights Watch counted at least 37 political prisoners (32 Lhotshampa) in 2023 (primary source not cross-checked — via search summary, marked as unverified).

§ 03-B

Official Figures vs. Independent Sources — Examining the Gaps

Most figures published by Bhutan match those recorded by international organizations. The 2024 growth rate of 7.5% announced by the NSB matches the canonical input value (7.50%), and the voter turnout of 65.6% is identical to the Election Commission's announcement. There are no visible traces of the numbers themselves being inflated. The gap arises from who the numbers count. The unemployment rate counts only the labor force remaining in the country, voter turnout counts only registered voters with access to voting methods, and civil liberties assessments examine only residents inside the borders. Those who have left and those who were pushed out are not included in any of the indicators.

Viewed through canonical axioms, this gap serves as a channel through which pressure vents out across the border before reaching the absorbers (A2 and A8). Axiom A1 posits that pressure ultimately targets A2 and A8, but when people under pressure leave, the line of sight itself is shortened. Axiom A11 (Water Anomaly) warns that surface stability and internal pressure can coexist. In Bhutan's case, however, internal pressure is exiting rather than accumulating, which differs from the pre-uprising scenario described by Axiom A11. Rather than conclusively diagnosing this as a 'hidden crisis,' this report merely records the fact that pressure has moved outside the measurement window.

Piece 1 — A7 Social Structure · A12 Mobility: Hirschman's Exit, Voice, and Loyalty. Hirschman (Hirschman 1970, Exit, Voice, and Loyalty) posited that when an organization deteriorates, members either leave (Exit), speak up (Voice), or stay and endure (Loyalty). When exit is easy, voice decreases. Bhutan is an example where the opening of migration channels to Australia significantly lowered exit costs. A7 (voter turnout 65.6%) and A12 (Internet 91.3% · Mobile 99.8) simultaneously exhibit high connectivity and diminished participation. Application limits: Hirschman's unit of analysis is the firm or organization; applying it to national-level migration requires separate examination of migration costs and nationality retention institutions.

Piece 2 — A5 Education Access: Brain Drain and Human Capital. Docquier & Rapoport (Docquier & Rapoport 2012, Journal of Economic Literature) summarized that while high-skilled migration does not solely impose losses on sending countries but can also incentivize education, the net loss is larger for smaller-population countries. Bhutan's A5 score of 0.4615 is driven down by adult literacy (64.9%) despite high secondary enrollment (86.2%) and education expenditure (5.8% of GDP). This is a generational effect of belatedly initiated mass education. Connection: It links to Piece 1 because educated youth form the main driving force of migration.

Piece 3 — A9 Environmental Pressure: Climate Vulnerability. The ND-GAIN Vulnerability of 0.528 and Readiness of 0.414 reflect exposure to Himalayan glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs) and landslides. Although Bhutan is known as a country where carbon sequestration exceeds emissions, canonical A9 measures vulnerability rather than emissions (v2.3 harmonization). Consequently, A9 emerges as a depressed axis for this country.

Piece 4 — A2·A8 Absorber: Democratization from Above. Bhutan's democratization was led by the King (2008). Viewed through Axiom A2 (Elastic Duality), vertical resilience (royal authority) was established prior to horizontal checks (political parties, press). Within A8, Political Stability (1.35) and Control of Corruption (1.47) are high, while Regulatory Quality (−0.09) falls below zero. This indicates that while governance is clean, market regulation is burdensome. Consolidated check: Pieces 1 and 2 operate on an individual-level, multi-year scale, whereas Piece 4 operates on an institutional-level, multi-decade scale; thus, they are not bundled into the same shift.
