Executive Summary

The heaviest fact in Afghanistan's state vector is that both absorber axes are touching the floor together: Institutional Trust A2 = 0.0512 (V-Dem Liberal Democracy 0.018, Corruption Perceptions Index CPI 17) and Governance A8 = 0.0950 (all 6 WGI indicators below −1.3). Civil Liberties A3 0.1404, Social Structure A7 0.0858, and Mobility A12 0.0867 are also at the bottom tier. It is a country starting from a state where the final target points A2 and A8, as dictated by Axiom A1 (aim invariance), have already collapsed.

Conversely, Economic Stability A4 = 0.6594 and External Shocks A11 = 0.9546 appear high. This does not mean the economy is stable. A4 assigns a higher value the lower the inflation rate is, and consumer prices in 2024 declined by −6.6%. The World Bank interprets this deflation as a sign of weak domestic demand. The exchange rate volatility input for A11 is an empirical measurement from 2020, predating the collapse of the republic. The canonical edition leaves this value unadjusted (DECISIONS 17), and this report reveals that gap in §03-B.

The upper bound of E_A2 (= A2) is 0.0512, which is below Axiom A9's brittleness threshold of 0.2. In August 2021, the republican government did indeed collapse. The current Taliban government is a de facto government unrecognized by most countries, devoid of electoral channels, and banning secondary and higher education for women. As C_internal is not measured, the collapse path is not finalized.

§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Economy. According to the World Bank Afghanistan Economic Monitor, the 2024 growth rate is estimated at 2.5%, remaining positive for two consecutive years, but consumer prices continued to decline at −7.7% in FY2023 and −4.2% in FY2024, which the World Bank interprets as reflecting weak domestic demand. In August 2025, driven by demand from returnees, inflation turned positive to 3.1%. Following the closure of the Pakistan border, trade was rerouted via Iran and Central Asia, and the trade deficit in November 2025 widened by 6% year-on-year (sources from World Bank monthly reports — cross-checked against search summaries, marked as 'cross-checked against summaries' due to failed text extraction from original PDF). The Taliban's National Statistics and Information Authority (NSIA) announced a growth rate of 2.7% for the solar year 1402 (March 2023–March 2024).

Population Movements. The UNHCR·IOM dashboard recorded 2,917,500 returns in the year 2025 alone. 1,879,200 people (64%) returned from Iran and 995,700 (34%) from Pakistan; among them, 1,448,250 were deportations/forced repatriations, 87.1% of which originated from Iran. While Bhutan is a country sending its citizens abroad, Afghanistan during the same period is a country where citizens outside are being forcibly returned.

Society and Diplomacy. On August 31, 2025, the Kunar earthquake struck mountainous areas with many mud-brick houses. In October, the worst border clash with Pakistan since the Taliban's return to power broke out before a ceasefire was reached through mediation by Qatar and Türkiye. In August 2026, UN Women stated that the Taliban had issued over 100 edicts restricting women's rights since retaking power, while the UN humanitarian appeal of $1.7 billion was only 25% funded. In a joint statement at the UN, 56 countries urged the repeal of restrictions on women and girls. The Taliban maintains that it respects women's rights in accordance with Islamic law and Afghan culture.

§ 03-B

Official Figures vs. Independent Sources — Examining the Gaps

Afghanistan's official figures consist of two layers: announcements by the de facto authorities (Taliban government) and the last empirical measurements left by institutions of the republican era. Canonical Edition v2.3 utilizes both layers as values within the empirical window (2016–2025). In this country, the gap arises not from data fabrication, but from the disappearance of measurement apparatus alongside the regime. Since 2021, elections, income distribution surveys, and foreign exchange market observations have ceased, and the remaining figures are either vestiges of the collapsed republic or remote estimates by international organizations.

Read through canonical axioms, Axioms A1 and A12 (aim and fuse invariance) hold that any shock ultimately determines its collapse path at the A2 and A8 thresholds. Because Afghanistan's A2 and A8 are already at the bottom, dimensions that appear 'high' like A4 and A11 do not provide strength supporting the absorbers. Deflation is not price stability but the surface of collapsed demand (Axiom A11 — coexistence of surface stability and internal pressure), and voter turnout from the republican era does not represent present participation. By retaining the numbers without erasing them, what has ceased to be measured becomes visible alongside them.

Piece 1 — A2·A8 Absorber: The Institutional Paradox of Aid-Dependent States. Moss, Pettersson & van de Walle (2006, CGD Working Paper 74) posited that when external aid accounts for a large share of public revenues, governments become accountable to donors rather than citizens, thereby weakening institutional development. Afghanistan during the Republic era was an extreme case; when aid was cut off in 2021, the administration supported by A8 collapsed along with it. Currently, the six indicators of A8 0.0950 (Government Effectiveness −1.93, Rule of Law −1.94, Political Stability −2.21) rank among the lowest in the world. Application limitations: The theory addresses countries where aid is maintained, and de facto governments after aid has been suspended fall outside its scope.

Piece 2 — A4 Economic Stability · A6 Health Level: Sen's Entitlement Approach. Sen (1981, Poverty and Famines) posited that famine occurs not from a lack of food, but because the entitlements (income and exchange entitlement) that allow people to purchase food collapse. Afghanistan's deflation, where poverty deepens even as prices fall, fits this framework. For A6 0.4099, health expenditure is measured high at 15.0% of GDP (international aid, small GDP), yet the infant mortality rate of 48.3 (per 1,000 live births) is high. A high expenditure ratio does not equate to good health conditions. Nexus: The aid cutoff in Piece 1 leads to the demand collapse in Piece 2.

Piece 3 — A3·A5·A7: Axiom A5's Cultural-Historical Coefficient and Gender Exclusion. Barfield (2010, Afghanistan: A Cultural and Political History) summarized that the Afghan state has historically been maintained through negotiations between Kabul's central power and tribal/regional orders. On top of this, the current government has institutionalized exclusion from the public sphere based on gender. In A5 0.2411 (adult literacy 37.3%, education expenditure empirically measured in 2017), the effects of the ban on secondary education for girls (2021–) have not yet been reflected in the indicators — literacy rate measured in 2021. Viewed through Axiom A5 (κ), the command reflection rate of the cultural-historical layer is high, presenting a structure where restrictions imposed from above are transmitted without resistance. Synthesis check: Piece 3 operates on a generational scale (decades), which has a different time scale from the annual variations of Pieces 1 and 2.

Piece 4 — A9 Environmental Pressure. ND-GAIN Vulnerability 0.612, Readiness 0.272. It is a mountainous agrarian society exposed to droughts and earthquakes, and its readiness (governmental and societal response capacity) ranks among the lowest globally. This aligns with OCHA's explanation that the heavy damage from the 2025 Kunar earthquake was due to mud-brick housing and landslide-prone topography.
