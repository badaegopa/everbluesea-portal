§ Conclusion · The Rotational Axis of the Triangle

The three Eastern Middle East countries are each other's threshold conditions. As Iran approaches the nuclear threshold (NCI=0.92), Iraq's BBD and GPI pressures accelerate, and Turkey's remaining room to exercise ADI expands. Conversely, if Turkey's disarmament of the PKK (2025) clears the security vacuum in the KRG of northern Iraq, Iran's Shia corridor pressure is transformed. The three are linked not by the first derivative (Λ-Score) but by the second derivative (mutual rate of change).

The regional turning point lies in §35 NCI. If Iran's uranium enrichment crosses the 90% threshold, peer pressure for nuclear development in Saudi Arabia and Turkey will be activated, representing a transition to a phase that no region measured in this report has ever experienced. Which direction the second Trump administration's negotiations to re-enter the JCPOA (2025~) take this branch will determine the trajectory of the region for the next five years.

While Iraq appears to be the weakest actor in the region, this analysis reaches the opposite conclusion. The regional highest Λ=0.685 signifies the depth of its recovery potential — 23 years of accumulated learning since 2003, the minimum stable equilibrium of the tripartite Shia-Sunni-Kurdish structure, and simultaneous influence from both the US and Iran leave open the possibility that Iraq will become the rotational axis of regional bifurcation. If Iran is the agent of pressure and Turkey is the peripheral actor, Iraq is the region's center of gravity.
