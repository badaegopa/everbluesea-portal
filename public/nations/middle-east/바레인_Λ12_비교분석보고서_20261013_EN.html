§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

2011
Uprising and Suppression — Uprising and suppression at Pearl Roundabout, GCC forces entering (general fact). This is a year prior to the trajectory.
2016~2017
Dissolution of Opposition Parties — Dissolution of Shia al-Wefaq (2016) and secular opposition Wafa (2017) (Al-Monitor). ACLED protests: 3,720 events in 2017.
2022-11-12
General Election — Minister of Justice: turnout 73%, the highest since 2002 (67% in 2018). Major opposition parties could not field candidates because of the dissolution, and electoral rules deprived 21.5% of citizens of the right to vote. Amnesty: 'a climate of political repression' (Al-Monitor · GSN · Wikipedia).
2023-08
Zawya Prison Hunger Strike — Began with approximately 400 participants on August 7 (HRW), over 800 by August 30 (estimated by the Bahrain Center for Human Rights). Detained in cells for 23 hours a day, demanding medical care, education, and communal prayer. The authorities proposed expanded visiting and outdoor time; the UN referred to an agreement on improved access to healthcare (HRW · Al-Monitor · UN OHCHR).
2024-04-08
Pardons of 1,584 Individuals — In 2023, 1,584 individuals were pardoned on the occasion of the king's 25th anniversary of accession; 65% of those pardoned faced charges related to unrest (government). Reported as the largest since 2011. Human rights organizations: approximately 600 political prisoners remain incarcerated; ACLED: protests continued and increased after the pardon (UCA News · The New Arab · ACLED).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Pardons and Remaining Detainees. The 2024 pardons received broad welcome from civil society, but human rights organizations recorded that political prisoners, including death-row inmates, remain incarcerated (The New Arab · ADHRB).

Political Participation. Official turnout has risen, and no major opposition party exists. Both records pertain to the same election (Al-Monitor).

Public Trends. ACLED protests numbered 3,720 in 2017, 1,620 in 2018, 703 in 2019, 1,138 in 2021, 686 in 2022, and 811 each in 2023 and 2024 (no 2020 tally). Political violence ranged from 4 to 31 incidents per year.
