Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1969-09
Gaddafi Coup — Overthrew the monarchy, leading to 42 years of one-man rule. This was the origin of a structure in which the leader and tribal networks, rather than state institutions, became the center of power.
2011-02~10
Uprising, Civil War, and Gaddafi's Death — The uprising that began in Benghazi escalated into civil war, and following NATO intervention under UN Security Council Resolution 1973, Gaddafi was killed in October.
2012-07
General National Congress (GNC) Election — The first nationwide election post-Gaddafi. Voter turnout in the engine's raw data was 61.58% (voter_turnout 2012).
2014
Government Division — Following the House of Representatives (HoR) election, the government fractured into eastern (Tobruk and Haftar forces) and western (Tripoli) factions. This division subsequently led to the duplication of all state institutions.
2019-04~2020-06
Haftar's Tripoli Offensive — Eastern forces launched an assault on the capital, drawing interventions from external powers including Türkiye and Russia. In October 2020, a UN-mediated ceasefire was signed. ACLED political violence events decreased from 1,155 in 2020 to 107 in 2021 (acled_link aggregate).
2021-03
Inauguration of the Government of National Unity (GNU) — Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh took office. The presidential election scheduled for December 2021 was indefinitely postponed due to disputes over candidate eligibility.
2023-09-10
Derna Floods — Storm Daniel caused the collapse of two dams, sweeping away urban Derna. Death toll estimates diverge widely across institutions (§03-B).
2024-08
Central Bank Governor Replacement Dispute — The Presidential Council's attempt to replace the governor triggered backlash from eastern forces and led to partial shutdowns of oil production. The World Bank estimated a roughly 10% contraction in 2024 GDP.
2025-04-06
Dinar Devaluation — The Central Bank of Libya (CBL) devalued the dinar by 13.3% against the SDR, setting the official exchange rate at 5.5677 dinars per dollar (AFP via Arab News).
2025-05-12~14
Tripoli Clashes — Following the death of Stability Support Apparatus (SSA) commander Abdel Ghani al-Kikli, the 444th Brigade and Rada forces clashed. UN tallies reported at least 8 civilian deaths, and following the May 14 ceasefire, the Presidential Council and UNSMIL set up a ceasefire monitoring mechanism (Africanews · HRW).
2026-05
Zawiya Clashes — Multiple clashes broke out among armed groups in Zawiya, 45 km west of Tripoli, prompting UNSMIL to condemn reports of civilian casualties. Total official death counts remain unconfirmed (Libya Security Monitor).
2026-07-22~08
Blackout Protest Demonstrations — Amid a 48°C heatwave and 10 to 20 hours of daily blackouts, road-blockade protests broke out across the Tripoli area, culminating in the takeover and fire at an electrical substation on August 25. Protesters also demanded the resignation of the GNU (Al Jazeera · Libya Security Monitor).
2026-08-30
Signing of UN-Mediated Election Agreement — The '4+4 Committee' representing the House of Representatives and the High Council of State signed an agreement to hold presidential and parliamentary elections within 24 months under a 'unified executive.' Ratification by both bodies within one month is the next step (Al Jazeera · The National).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

A Divided State. In Libya, the UN-recognized Government of National Unity in Tripoli (Prime Minister Dbeibeh) coexists with the eastern administration backed by Commander Haftar. The election agreement signed at UNSMIL headquarters on August 30, 2026, set conditions of 'within 24 months' and a 'unified executive and unified institutions,' but those conditions themselves remain unmet. UN Special Envoy Hanna Tetteh stated that the agreement must translate into 'tangible action' (Al Jazeera 2026-08-30).

Armed Groups and Capital Security. The May 2025 clashes in Tripoli occurred between the GNU-aligned 444th Brigade and SSA/Rada forces, with HRW reporting that armed groups and quasi-state forces failed to protect civilians (HRW 2025-05-20). In May 2026 in Zawiya and on July 21 in downtown Tripoli, deployments of armed groups escalated tensions once again. Official death tolls largely remain unannounced.

Oil Rents and Economy. The National Oil Corporation (NOC) announced that crude oil production in April 2026 reached 1.43 million barrels per day, a 10-year high, targeting 1.6 million barrels by the end of 2026 (Ecofin Agency). In February 2026, the results of the first international exploration licensing round allocated 5 blocks to companies including Chevron and Eni. Oil and gas account for roughly 95% of exports and about 90% of government revenue (citing CBL · Coface). The World Bank estimated real GDP growth for 2025 at 13.3% (+17.4% in the oil sector) (Libya Economic Monitor, Fall 2025).

Living Conditions. From July 22, 2026, blackout protest demonstrations continued across the Tripoli area. The General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL) estimated the power grid deficit at approximately 1,000 MW, and reports indicated that black-market diesel prices rose up to 8 dinars per liter (Libya Security Monitor · Prime Business Africa). That the capital's electricity is cut off for more than half the day in the very year that oil production hits a 10-year high is a cross-section of this country's rentier structure.

These two directions align with the variable direction of the BH (Black Hole: implosion and irreversibility) condition. This is an observational direction, not a definitive judgment.

However, Libya has already undergone 15 years in a state of national division since 2011, and partial readjustments—such as the 2020 ceasefire and the 2026 election agreement—have also repeatedly occurred. Thus, it is closer to the question of 'where does the state of division converge?' rather than a path classification 'prior to collapse.'
