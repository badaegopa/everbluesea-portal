§ Conclusion · Between the Capital Pole and the Collapse Pole

The 7 countries of the Central Middle East represent a case where two extremes coexist within a single region. The Capital Pole (Qatar, UAE, Oman)is stability created by the combination of resource zones and ruling monarchies, while the Collapse Pole (Yemen)is the result when the same resource zone is combined with foreign intervention. Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and Bahrain lie in between, bearing the pressures of BBD (resource bubble), ADI (authoritarianism), and DCI (sectarian division).

The regional structural anomaly is the BBD §33 average of 0.793—higher than any region measured in this report. While the combination of single-resource commodity dependence (averaging 70% for oil and gas) and megaproject debt (NEOM $500B, North Field $30B, Duqm Port Belt and Road) creates superficial stability, if oil prices drop below $60, BBD will be non-linearly activated according to the threshold function of §33. The 2025 Saudi IPO funding setback is the first signal of this structure.

Yemen crossed the threshold on a path without any of the resource or diplomatic capital possessed by the other six countries in the region. The recovery path does not lie within the region itself. How the six GCC countries share the responsibility for Yemen's reconstruction will determine the direction of the regional average Λ over the next five years.
