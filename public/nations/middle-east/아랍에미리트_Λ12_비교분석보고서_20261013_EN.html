§ 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

Direct Impact of the War. According to the Compendium Status Briefing (as of 2026-10-07), missile and drone attacks by Iran after February 28, 2026 damaged Dubai International Airport, Jebel Ali Port, and the Ruwais industrial complex. On March 26, the UAE Ministry of Defence announced a cumulative 11 deaths and 169 injuries. The government curtailed relations with Iran by closing its embassy in Tehran, revoking residency permits of Iranian nationals, and arresting money changers linked to the IRGC. Twenty-one people in Dubai and 45 in Abu Dhabi were arrested for filming and disseminating footage of the strikes (Compendium Briefing).

Economy. On May 30, 2026, the Federal Competitiveness and Statistics Centre (FCSC) announced that real GDP grew 6.2% in 2025 to reach 1.9 trillion AED, with non-oil GDP expanding by 6.8% (WAM). Projections for 2026 were sharply revised downward — the IMF projected 3.1% in its April 2026 World Economic Outlook, while its July staff mission statement anticipated a slight contraction in overall 2026 GDP due to a slowdown in the non-hydrocarbon sector (tourism, transportation, trade, real estate). The World Bank projected 2.4%. Passenger traffic at Dubai International Airport dropped 20.6% year-on-year in Q1 2026 (compiled by Nasser Saidi & Associates).

Reconfiguration of Export Routes. In June 2026, Minister Thani Al Zeyoudi stated the goal of achieving 'zero dependence on Hormuz,' and ADNOC is pursuing an expansion of oil pipelines bypassing Hormuz (MEES 2026-06-19, paywalled article preview scope). Reports on the ceasefire and strait situation following July 2026 remain conflicting, and the latest status could not be conclusively determined in this investigation (unconfirmed).

Public Trends. Within the verification scope of the Compendium, there are no reports of large-scale collective action. According to ACLED tallies, demonstrations ranged from 0 to 6 per year between 2017 and 2024 (with a peak of 6 in 2022). The scarcity of records must be read alongside the fact that public assemblies are severely restricted by law in this country.

The UAE's published figures, unlike those of Turkmenistan, do not diverge sharply from international organization estimates. That space is occupied by population by nationality, income distribution among migrant workers, and updated cumulative figures of war damage. Published values are kept as originally reported (DECISIONS 17·19).

Point of connection: The distributive contract of ① applies only to the minority citizen population, while the majority non-citizen population of ② remains outside the contract. The maximum pressure of A7 emerges at the boundary between these two strata.

Collapse Path Classification — WD / SN / BH

Path Classification Criteria (UI Canonical Text, Stellar Evolution Metaphor)
WD: E_A2 < 0.3, C_internal < 0.4 · SN: E_A2 0.3~0.5, C_internal > 0.5 · BH: E_A2 < 0.2, C_internal > 0.7
White Dwarf (WD) = suppression and latency · Supernova (SN) = collapse and regeneration · Black Hole (BH) = implosion and irreversibility
The decisive variable distinguishing SN from BH is C_internal (elite internal conflict energy). This is a metaphor and does not assert an astrophysical correspondence.

The UAE exhibits no observations corresponding to either premise of the path classification (decline in E_A2 or rise in C_internal). With A2 and A8 positioned in the outer orbits, E_A2 (institutional-trust effect) is on the higher condition, and no public conflicts between ruling dynasties (Abu Dhabi Al Nahyan · Dubai Al Maktoum) have been recorded. Because WD, SN, and BH are classifications of 'collapse paths,' they are not currently applicable targets for this country.

Nevertheless, the reason these three paths are noted conditionally is that the 2026 shock originated externally rather than internally. When an external shock strikes rents (oil exports) and hubs (aviation/tourism) simultaneously, how long the distributive contract can endure is the variable to observe. This is not a prediction.
