Position within the Region — Why Syria?

This report was selected as the country with the lowest η based on the Country Analysis Compendium among countries without a standalone edition in the West Asia/Middle East round of the daily regional rotation pipeline. In the compendium, the η of the Syrian Arab Republic (hereafter Syria) is 0.3920(195th among 197 countries), the lowest among 18 countries in the region. Directly above are Palestine (0.4226, 181st among 197 countries) and Yemen (0.4307), with a gap of over 0.2 compared to the regional highest, the United Arab Emirates (0.6066).

Syria is a country for which BSLI was not calculated in the compendium (not included in the World Bank PIP or a gap of 10 years or more). Therefore, the center (Sun) of the pressure coordinate system cannot be drawn, and the compendium also lacks a causal loop diagram and a cross-sectional pressure ranking table. There are no missing dimensions. Centered on the contrast between compendium trajectories and published figures, this report combines a qualitative diagnostic, following precedents of data-poor countries (Cuba).

The most important fact when reading this country is the time point. The baseline cross-section of the compendium engine is 2024, and in December of that very year, the Assad regime collapsed. In other words, the 2024 pressure coordinate is the 'final cross-section immediately before collapse,' and the subsequent transitional government period is not included in the engine values.

Executive Summary

Syria ranks lowest in West Asia/Middle East with a compendium η of 0.3920. Dimensions within the 2024 threshold band (P ≥ 0.4458) are five—A2, A4, A5, A7, and A8—the most in the region, and as per compendium observation records, to the simultaneous inclusion of A7·A8 (common configuration across 4 uprising observation countries in KSDS v10.0, n=4 exploratory), it doesapply.

With virtually no institutional mass to absorb policy shocks, the regime collapsed in just 11 days following the rebel offensive in December 2024. In the cross-section immediately preceding it, the engine was recording this country in the state of maximum pressure within the region.

The gap in published figures has different characteristics across eras. Under the Assad era, the 2021 presidential election was announced with a 95.1% vote share, which five Western nations declared 'neither free nor fair.' Under the transitional government era, the official investigation death toll of 1,426 from the 2025 coastal violence falls within the estimate range of independent monitoring groups, but in the Suwayda violence of the same year, no comprehensive government tally was released at all.

§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Centralization. According to the Compendium status briefing (as of 2026-10-07), the transitional government moved toward absorbing the military and civilian organizations of the Kurdish-led SDF into state apparatuses. Following government offensives in January 2026 that seized control of substantial northern and northeastern regions, a 14-point ceasefire (January 18) and a comprehensive integration agreement (January 29) followed, and on August 25 Commander Abdi declared the dissolution of the SDF. Casualty and displacement figures for the January fighting are not present in obtained sources (Compendium data caution).

Political Institutions. In the first indirect general election on October 5, 2025, an electoral college of roughly 6,000–7,000 elected 119–121 of the 140 seats in the People's Assembly (figures vary across sources). The remaining 70 seats are reserved for presidential appointment. As of May 2026 reports, Suwayda seats and 1/3 of the presidential appointments remained unfilled. Despite a presidential decree mandating that 20% of the electoral college be women, women accounted for only 3% of the electees (reporting citing International Crisis Group and IPU data).

Criticism of Poor Governance and Cabinet Reshuffle. In the first cabinet reshuffle on May 10, 2026, al-Sharaa replaced his younger brother Maher, who was chief of staff to the presidency, with a former governor of Homs, and replaced the Ministers of Information and Agriculture as well as the governors of Homs, Quneitra, and Deir ez-Zor. Al Jazeera reported that in the preceding months, there were protests and social media campaigns criticizing economic deterioration and poor governance. Hawachi of the International Crisis Group assessed this as 'not an expansion of the inner circle, but a realignment.' The scale and specific demands of the protests were not confirmed in this investigation (unconfirmed).

Economy and Returns. The World Bank stated that GDP contracted by 1.5% in 2024 and projected 1% growth in 2025, noting that cumulative decline since 2010 exceeded 50% and 2024 GNI per capita stood at $830 (2025-07). UNHCR tallied that over 1.7 million cross-border refugees and approximately 2 million internally displaced persons had returned since December 2024 (April–June 2026 report). The same report noted that the pace of returns slowed in the second quarter.

§ 03-B

Published Figures vs. Independent Sources — Examining the Gaps

Syria is a rare case where the publication methods of two regimes within the same country can be compared. Announcements during the Assad era were figures for regime legitimacy, whereas announcements during the transitional government era took the form of fact-finding commissions, yet in incidents where government forces were identified as perpetrators, no comprehensive tally was released. Published figures were not filtered or adjusted (DECISIONS 17·19).

Collapse Path Classification — WD / SN / BH

Path Classification Criteria (UI Canonical Edition, Stellar Evolution Metaphor)
WD: E_A2 < 0.3, C_internal < 0.4 · SN: E_A2 0.3~0.5, C_internal > 0.5 · BH: E_A2 < 0.2, C_internal > 0.7
White Dwarf (WD) = suppression and latency · Supernova (SN) = collapse and regeneration · Black Hole (BH) = implosion and irreversibility
The decisive variable distinguishing SN from BH is C_internal (elite internal collision energy). This is a metaphor and does not assert an astrophysical correspondence.

In terms of path classification, the end of the Assad regime exhibits multiple characteristics of an implosion (BH)—E_A2 (institutional-trust effect) was in a condition below 0.2 due to bottomed-out A2 and A8, and the military and elites did not defend the regime at the final moment. However, in that the state did not dissolve and a new power assumed administration to commence reconstruction, it also shows aspects of a Supernova (SN, collapse and regeneration). Since the decisive variable C_internal (elite internal collision energy) has not been quantitatively calculated, definitive classification is reserved.

For the transitional government period, the collapse path is not reclassified. This is because the engine values are from the 2024 cross-section and do not encompass this period. Instead, observational variables are recorded—the selectivity wherein official tallies of sectarian violence vary depending on the nature of the event (§03-B), and power concentration manifested through presidentially appointed seats and crony appointments. This is not a prediction.
