Mongolia has a Series η of 0.5040 (88th of 197 countries). The replacements followed institutional pathways: votes of no confidence, dismissal resolutions and presidential vetoes, constitutional court rulings of unconstitutionality, and resignations and parliamentary elections. From an observer standpoint, Mongolia's pattern is one in which elite fractures occur frequently but are handled through voting and judicial channels. Because the canonical SE(t) value required for UI assessment is unavailable, the pathway is not confirmed.

§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1990
Democratic Revolution — End of the one-party system following a hunger strike and rallies at Sükhbaatar Square; introduction of multiparty elections (general fact).
1992
Constitutional Reform — A semi-presidential framework centered on parliament (State Great Khural). Thereafter, a two-party competitive landscape between the Mongolian People's Party (MPP) and the Democratic Party (DP) took hold (general fact).
2009
Oyu Tolgoi Investment Agreement — Development of a large copper and gold mine. The event solidified Mongolia's growth model based on mining and exports to China (general fact).
2018–19
Anti-Corruption Protests — Rallies demanding the resignation of the speaker of parliament following revelations of a high-level official corruption network (peak December 2018–January 2019; event existence confirmed only via Wikipedia).
2019
Constitutional Amendment — Term limits for the presidency, including a six-year single-term limit. Strengthening of the prime minister's authority (general fact).
2021
Year of Surge in Protests — Based on ACLED records, protest events nearly doubled from the previous year (1.95 times). In the same year, President Khürelsükh was elected (took office 2021-06; Series briefing). It is also the year in which A7 fell most sharply over nine years in the Series trajectory (+0.1188).
2022
Protests against Coal Export Corruption(December) — Large-scale protests over corruption related to coal exports to China (event existence confirmed only via Wikipedia).
2024
General Election (Seats Expanded to 126) — After retaining a MPP majority, a grand coalition was formed. V-Dem regime classification dropped to electoral democracy → electoral autocracy for this single year before returning in 2025 (V-Dem RoW raw data).
2025-06-03
Prime Minister Oyun-Erdene Resigns — Following anti-corruption protests triggered by controversy over the prime minister's son's display of luxury goods, he failed to secure a vote of confidence with 44 of 82 votes, falling short of the required quorum (64 votes) (Series briefing, OCCRP). The successor, Prime Minister Zandanshatar, was appointed.
2025-10
Dismissal Resolution → Veto → Unconstitutional — Parliament voted to dismiss (10-17), citing changes in the calculation of mineral royalties and other issues; President Khürelsükh vetoed it (10-20) on grounds of insufficient quorum; the Constitutional Court ruled the dismissal resolution unconstitutional, allowing the prime minister to remain in office (10-22) (Series briefing).
2026-03·04
Third Prime Minister — Amid a Democratic Party boycott of parliament and internal strife within the ruling party, Zandanshatar resigned (3-27); the State Great Khural elected former speaker Nyam-Osoryn Uchral prime minister with 88 of 107 votes present (3-30); a coalition cabinet took office on April 4 (Series briefing, AP, News On AIR).
2026-08-24
Introduction of Basic Pension Announced — Prime Minister Uchral announced the introduction of a basic pension and a pension increase from November 1, 2026 (approximately 1.2 trillion tugriks allocated) (Series briefing, Asia News Network).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Politics. According to the Series briefing (as of 2026-10-07) and AP reports, the ruling MPP was divided into factions loyal to the president and to the former prime minister, and from the start of the spring session in March 2026, opposition Democrats and some ruling-party members boycotted parliament. Prime Minister Zandanshatar's resignation was reported as a compromise to break the deadlock; his successor, Uchral (Nyam-Osoryn Uchral), is an MPP representative and former speaker of parliament. In his confirmation speech, he mentioned together rising international fuel prices, fluctuations in raw-material markets, and domestic political fragmentation. The Uchral government emphasizes deregulation and market liberalization ('economic liberation'), and it is pursuing renegotiations with Rio Tinto over the distribution of proceeds from the Oyu Tolgoi mine (Series briefing).

Economy. IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation (2025-09) recorded an expanded current-account deficit, declining fiscal revenue, and downward pressure on the exchange rate owing to reduced coal exports in the first half of 2025. The IMF 2026 Article IV Consultation (2026-08) assessed that macroeconomic outcomes for 2025 were better than expected despite a sharp fall in coal prices; Supplementary Table 1 records 2025 real growth of 6.8%, an average inflation rate of 8.8%, and a coking coal price of $107.30 per ton (2024) → $66.50 per ton (2025). The uncertainty surrounding future coal exports to China remains an upside risk.

Public Trends. Anti-corruption protests under the slogan 'Resignation is easy (Ogtsrokh Amarkhan)' continued in May–June 2025, and more than 59,000 people signed a petition demanding the prime minister's resignation. Protesters raised inflation, unemployment, budget opacity, and constraints on press freedom together. Even around the time of the dismissal attempt in October 2025, protests by youth over corruption and inequality were reported (Series briefing). Parliamentary developments from April to October 2026 and the results of the Oyu Tolgoi renegotiations have not been confirmed according to official announcements.

Connection with the Engine. The elite fracture affects UI's C_internal (internal conflict energy), while the coal export shock affects A4 Economic Stability (measured: GDP, manufacturing) and A11 External Shocks (measured: energy import dependence). However, in the Series engine, the actual input for A11 is energy import dependence, and the final measurement is 2023 (+1-year carryover), so the sharp fall in coal prices in 2025 has not yet been reflected in the figures.

§ 09

Collapse Path Classification — WD / SN / BH

Path Classification Criteria (Canonical UI, Stellar Evolution Metaphor)
WD: E_A2 < 0.3, C_internal < 0.4 · SN: E_A2 0.3~0.5, C_internal > 0.5 · BH: E_A2 < 0.2, C_internal > 0.7
White Dwarf (WD) = suppression and latency · Supernova (SN) = collapse and regeneration · Black Hole (BH) = implosion and irreversibility
The decisive variable distinguishing SN from BH is C_internal (elite internal conflict energy). This is a metaphor and does not claim astrophysical correspondence.

To assess the path according to the canonical baseline, values for E_A2 and C_internal are required, but neither exists for Mongolia. The confirmable upper bound for E_A2 (0.3143) lies on the boundary between WD (<0.3) and SN (0.3–0.5), so the numbers alone do not permit a determination in either direction. This report therefore does not affirm a path.

The observed event pattern is as follows. Mass protests recurred in 2018–19, 2022, and 2025, but each time ended in leadership turnover rather than regime collapse. ACLED tallies record 0 conflict fatalities for every year from 2019 to 2024 (1 in 2018). Elite rifts were substantial, but they were handled not through street clashes but through no-confidence votes, dismissal motions, presidential vetoes, Constitutional Court rulings, and coalition realignment. From the perspective of the 4th layer, this pattern resembles a repetition at the cabinet level rather than at the regime level of SN's 'collapse and regeneration.' The three cells below are conditions for moving to each path, not probabilities.

⬜
White Dwarf (WD) — suppression and latency
Condition: when institutional channels for change are blocked
A case in which protest is suppressed and accumulates while institutional channels such as parliamentary voting, presidential vetoes, and the Constitutional Court are rendered ineffective. This is remote from current observations. The temporary drop in the 2024 V-Dem classification was a signal in this direction, but it returned in 2025.
💥
Supernova (SN) — collapse and regeneration (observed tendency)
Condition: when change continues within institutions
The current pattern, in which a cycle of corruption exposés → protests → leadership turnover → new coalition repeats at the cabinet level. The regime is maintained, while the duration of policy continuity is observed to shorten.
⚫
Black Hole (BH) — implosion and irreversibility
Condition: E_A2 < 0.2 + C_internal > 0.7
A complex case in which elite rifts spill over outside the voting and judicial channels (physical conflict, suspension of constitutional order) while a fiscally dependent coal economy collapses. This is not observed in current records.
