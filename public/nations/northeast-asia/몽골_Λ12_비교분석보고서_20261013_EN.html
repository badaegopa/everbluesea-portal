Executive Summary

Mongolia's 12-dimensional state vector exhibits an asymmetric structure where infrastructure (A1 0.9203), mobility (A12 0.8922), and environmental pressure (A9 0.8559) axes are high, while educational access (A5 0.0122), economic stability (A4 0.0841), and institutional trust (A2 0.3143) are low. In the pressure coordinate system, the dimensions falling within the exploratory threshold band (P ≥ 0.446) are three under the v2 static baseline (A2, A5, A7), and two under the 2024 time series (A5, A7). Of the A7-A8 combination consistently co-occurring at the ignition moment in previous studies of four uprising nations, A8 (governance, P 0.363) lies outside the band.

The grassroots base BSLI is −0.0596 (Grade E) in the 139-country canonical edition, and in the consistent-baseline time-series edition, it moved from below to above the basic life line (0), with measured years shifting 2016 −0.0264 → 2018 −0.0136 → 2022 0.0582. On the political front, although prime ministers changed three times in nine months during 2025–2026, the turnovers proceeded through institutional channels: no-confidence votes, Constitutional Court rulings, and coalition reshuffling. From the observer standpoint, Mongolia is a case where elite fissures are frequent, but such fissures have been digested within institutions. However, because canonical SE(t) values required for UI determination are unavailable, the collapse path is not confirmed and remains withheld.

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1990
Democratic Revolution — End of the one-party regime following hunger strikes and rallies in Sükhbaatar Square, introducing multi-party elections.
1992
Enactment of the New Constitution — Semi-presidential framework centered on parliament (State Great Khural). Subsequently, a two-party competition structure between the Mongolian People's Party (MPP) and Democratic Party (DP) took root.
2009
Oyu Tolgoi Investment Agreement — Development of massive copper and gold mines. An inflection point cementing a growth model reliant on mining and exports to China.
2018–19
Anti-Corruption Mass Protests — Rallies demanding the resignation of the Speaker of Parliament following revelations of high-ranking official corruption networks (peaking 2018.12.27–2019.1.10).
2019
Constitutional Amendment — A single six-year presidential term, strengthening the powers of the prime minister.
2021
Year of Protest Surge — 127 protests recorded by ACLED (1.95 times the 65 of the previous year). In the same year, a Constitutional Court ruling barred the incumbent president from reelection, leading to the election of President Khürelsükh.
2022
Coal Export Corruption Protests(December) — Large-scale protests over embezzlement allegations related to coal exports to China. 129 ACLED protests.
2024
General Election (Seats Expanded to 126) — MPP maintained a majority and formed a grand coalition. V-Dem regime classification dropped from electoral democracy (2) to electoral autocracy (1) for this single year, returning to 2 in 2025.
2025.6
No-Confidence Vote for Prime Minister Oyun-Erdene — Following protests triggered by reports of luxury spending by the prime minister's son, a confidence motion failed, and Prime Minister Zandanshatar took office.
2025.10
Invalidation of Prime Minister Dismissal Attempt — The Constitutional Court rejected parliament's resolution to dismiss Zandanshatar.
2026.3–4
Third Prime Minister — Amid an opposition boycott, Zandanshatar resigned (March 27), Parliament Speaker Uchral was confirmed as prime minister (88 in favor out of 107 present), and on April 4, a coalition cabinet of MPP, HUN Party, and National Coalition was inaugurated.
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Politics. Between June 2025 and March 2026, Mongolia saw three prime ministers. According to AP reports, the ruling MPP is divided between factions aligned with President Khürelsükh and former Prime Minister Oyun-Erdene; from the start of the March 2026 spring session, the opposition DP and parts of the ruling party boycotted parliamentary sessions. The resignation of Prime Minister Zandanshatar was interpreted as a compromise to break this impasse, and his successor, Prime Minister Uchral, an MPP leader and former parliamentary speaker, was a compromise candidate between both factions. In his confirmation speech, he cited a triple crisis: rising international fuel prices, commodity market volatility, and domestic political division.

Economy. The IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation Report (September 2025) recorded that in the first half of 2025, coal exports fell sharply, expanding the current account deficit, reducing fiscal revenues, and exerting downward pressure on the exchange rate. Consumer price inflation peaked in February 2025 before declining to 8.2% in June. The IMF identified softening Chinese coal demand and falling coal prices as downside risks. Developments after April 2026 were not confirmed in this investigation and are thus omitted.

Connection to the Engine. This situation directly touches two axes of Λ¹². Elite fissures correspond to C_internal (internal collision energy) of UI, while coal export shocks correspond to A4 (economic stability) and A11 (external shocks). However, in the v2 pipeline, the actual input for A11 is energy import dependency, and since the final empirical measurement dates to 2023, the sharp drop in coal exports in 2025 is not yet reflected in the figures.

The axes remaining inside the band are A5 and A7. A5 (educational access, empirical input R&D/GDP) has a value of 0.0122, standing at the 8.8th percentile of the 138-country reference group, and exerts a pressure of 0.618, making it the strongest pressure axis in Mongolia. However, because A7 shifts across 72% of all sample cells (engine-history pattern_measured), high activity alone can hardly be interpreted as an anomalous signal.

A2 (institutional trust, empirical input CPI and control of corruption) is on the boundary. This divergence arises from differences in input years between the two editions. As A2 forms a binary pair with A8 with a coupling coefficient of +0.9554, the possibility of A8 shifting in tandem should A2 reenter the band is established as a monitoring checkpoint.

Collapse Path Classification — WD / SN / BH

Path Classification Criteria (UI Canonical, Stellar Evolution Metaphor)
WD: E_A2 < 0.3, C_internal < 0.4 · SN: E_A2 0.3~0.5, C_internal > 0.5 · BH: E_A2 < 0.2, C_internal > 0.7
White Dwarf (WD) = suppression and latency · Supernova (SN) = collapse and regeneration · Black Hole (BH) = implosion and irreversibility
The decisive variable distinguishing SN from BH is C_internal (elite internal collision energy). This is not claiming an astrophysical correspondence, but a metaphor.

Determining the path according to canonical criteria requires E_A2 and C_internal values, but neither canonical value exists for Mongolia. The verifiable upper bound of E_A2 (0.3143) straddles the boundary between WD (under 0.3) and SN (0.3–0.5), meaning it cannot be separated either way by numbers alone. Therefore, this report does not finalize a path.

Summarizing only the observed event patterns yields the following. Mass protests in Mongolia were repeated in 2018–19, 2022, and 2025, each time ending not in regime collapse but in leadership turnover. ACLED-aggregated fatalities are 0 for all years between 2019 and 2024 (1 in 2018). While elite internal rifts are large, those rifts have been handled through no-confidence votes, Constitutional Court rulings, and coalition reshuffling rather than street clashes. This pattern is close to repeating SN's 'collapse and regeneration' at the cabinet level rather than at the regime level. The three cells below represent conditions for transitioning to each path, not probabilities.
