─── Observer Standpoint ───
Ⅰ · Observer Standpoint

The Powder Keg of the World, and the Intersection of Opportunity

Northeast Asia accounts for approximately 25% of the world's GDP, yet it remains a high-risk region where unresolved historical trauma, nuclear threats, and hegemony competitions overlap. The wounds left by Japan's imperialist colonial rule (1895–1945) still define the diplomatic landscape of this region, and the division structure of the Korean War in 1950 has not been resolved for over 70 years.

At the same time, this region is a global hub for semiconductors, AI, manufacturing, and finance. The structure in which the four axes—South Korea's semiconductors, Taiwan's foundries, Japan's materials and components, and China's manufacturing scale—are interdependently connected creates a dual reality where conflict and cooperation operate simultaneously.

Major Events in the Region (2024–2026): Declaration of martial law and dismissal of the President in South Korea (2024.12–2025.04), election of Lai Ching-te in the Taiwanese presidential election (2024.01), continued slowdown of China's economic growth, North Korea explicitly defining South Korea as a 'hostile state' in its constitution (2024.10), Japan formalizing its defense budget goal of 2% of GDP.

Ⅺ · Special Analysis of the Korean Peninsula

Division Structure and Security Dynamics of Northeast Asia

🇰🇷 South Korea — Democratic Resilience and Internal Rifts

President Yoon Suk Yeol's declaration of martial law on December 3, 2024, was the first such event in 37 years since democratization in 1987. Although the immediate resolution for lifting by 190 members of the National Assembly and the unanimous dismissal by the Constitutional Court (2025.04) demonstrated institutional resilience, the Σ_rep (suppressive elite-people disconnect) index is showing short-term upward signals. Despite strengths in semiconductor infrastructure, the ADI Transitional range (0.416) suggests that weak proprietary LLM capability remains a structural challenge.

🇰🇵 North Korea — An Existence Outside the Comparative Framework

In its 2024 constitution, North Korea explicitly defined South Korea as a 'hostile state' and physically demolished the inter-Korean railway and road infrastructure. Its TBI of 0.857 is significantly above the threshold (0.70), which signifies that the structure in which the regime appropriates trauma as a governing resource has become highly entrenched. Since all variables show extreme values, there are limitations in applying the standard Λ¹² comparative framework, and a separate analytical framework will be applied in the standalone volume.
