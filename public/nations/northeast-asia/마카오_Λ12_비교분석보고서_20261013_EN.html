§ 01

Executive Summary

Macau's Series η of 0.5008 is the mean of nine dimensions: A1 Infrastructure (0.9455), A12 (0.8869), A8 Governance (0.7202), A2 Institutional Trust (0.6062), A7 (0.5767), A6 (0.5414), A4 (0.1316), A10 (0.0503), and A5 (0.0481). The dimension within the band in 2024 is A5 (measured: R&D/GDP, P 0.5958), followed by A7 (0.4366) and A4 Economic Stability (0.4014).

The characteristic of the trajectory is the amplitude of the casino economy. In 2020, A4 (measured: GDP/manufacturing) fell sharply by +0.0455, whereas in the same year A5 (R&D/GDP) actually decreased pressure by -0.0220 — this is a denominator effect as the research and development ratio rose when GDP (the denominator) plummeted (canonical verification required P-9). In the 2023 recovery period, the opposite occurred: A4 was -0.0236 and A5 was +0.0166.

On the political axis, in October 2024, former Chief Judge of the Court of Final Appeal Sam Hou Fai became executive chief after winning 394 votes from the Election Committee as a sole candidate; in the Legislative Assembly election in September 2025, all 12 candidates across two lists lost their eligibility under the determination of the Committee for Safeguarding National Security. On December 31, 2025, the closure of satellite casinos ended operations, making Macau a city without satellite casinos for decades.

§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1999-12
Return — Returned from Portugal to China and became a special administrative region (general fact).
2002
Opening of Casinos — The monopoly ended, and a multi-operator system opened. Thereafter, Macau became the world's largest casino city (general fact).
2020–2022
Pandemic and Casino Shutdown — Visitor numbers and casino revenue plummeted. In the Series trajectory, A4 moved sharply by +0.0455 in 2020 and A10 by +0.0415; thereafter A10 halted due to carryover of the 2020 value.
2021 · 2022
First Disqualification and Gambling Law Revision — In the 2021 Legislative Assembly election, pro-democracy candidates were disqualified for the first time through loyalty vetting. The 2022 revised gambling law restructured business rights and banned revenue sharing for satellite casinos (Macao News · AGBrief).
2024-10-13
Election of the Executive Chief — Sole candidate Sam Hou Fai was elected with 394 out of 398 votes from the Election Committee (Xinhua News Agency · Bernama; 98.5% based on a full membership of 400). The vote concluded in approximately 30 minutes (ASGAM · AP).
2025-09-14
'Patriots' Legislative Election — The Electoral Affairs Committee disqualified all 12 candidates on both lists, in accordance with the National Security Council's (Chair: Chief Executive) determination that 'supporting the Basic Law is not equivalent to loyalty.' The determination is final and subject to no appeal or litigation. Ron Lam, a sitting legislator who had passed the 2021 review, was also included. The number of directly elected seats on the list was 6, the lowest since 2001. Turnout was 175,272 votes, with a turnout rate of 53.35% (approximately 328,000 registered voters; 5th among 7 elections since return), and approximately 13,000 invalid or blank ballots (UCA News · Macao News · AsiaNews).
2025-12-31
Closure of Satellite Casinos — Under the 2022 law's extension, 11 satellite casinos (approximately 480 tables and 270 slot machines) closed. The government stated that 'there is no substantial impact on total revenue' (Macau Business · AGBrief).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Casino Revenue. Following the release of the Gaming Inspection Coordination Bureau, total casino revenue in 2025 reached 24.74 billion patacas (approximately $3.09 billion), an increase of 9.1% and a recovery to approximately 84.6% of 2019 levels. The government had previously lowered its June 2025 forecast to 228 billion patacas. The mass market accounted for 73%, while VIP grew by 24%, driving the growth (Macau Business · GGRAsia · CDC Gaming).

Diversification Goals and Reality. The 2024–2028 Economic Diversification Plan set a target of reducing the share of gambling to 40% of GDP and raising non-gambling industries to 60% by 2028. There are assessments that gambling accounted for 43.3% of total added value and more than 80% of government revenue in 2024 (ASGAM 2026-05). Growth forecasts for 2026 range from Fitch's 4.0% to Macau University's 6.3%.

Political Space. Disqualification of legislative election candidates continued in 2025, as it did in 2021, and the pro-democracy legislators disqualified in 2021 remain under a five-year ban on running (Macao News). Because there are no ACLED records for this unit, street trends cannot be recorded on the same basis.
