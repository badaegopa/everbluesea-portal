§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

2021-04~05
General Election and Constitutional Crisis — Of 51 seats, FAST held 26 and HRPP held 25. On May 22, the head of state issued a proclamation blocking parliament's convening; on the opening day, the parliament doors were locked, preventing judges and lawmakers from entering; on May 24, FAST held an outdoor swearing-in ceremony outside parliament (Wikipedia · SBS).
2021-06~07
Court Rulings — Supreme Court: The outdoor swearing-in was unconstitutional, while simultaneously ordering parliament to convene within 7 days and declaring further obstruction illegal. On July 23, the Court of Appeal confirmed Piauame Naomi Mata'afa as the first female prime minister, concluding the 15-week crisis. HRPP staged protests against the judiciary (Pacific Island Times · Civicus).
2025-01
Ruling Party Division — When party chairman Laulii Leuatia Schmidt, who was criminally indicted, refused to resign and was dismissed from cabinet, Prime Minister Mata'afa and four ministers were expelled from FAST (Wikipedia).
2025-08-29
Early General Election — Held after the dissolution of parliament following the rejection of the budget bill. FAST won 30 seats out of 51 (final tally, preliminary reports of 31 seats · later reported as 31 after independent MPs joined; RNZ · Wikipedia), HRPP 14 seats, SUP 3 seats for Mata'afa's new party, and 4 independents. The HRPP leader warned of legal challenges alleging corruption and vote-buying during the campaign (RNZ · Wikipedia).
2025-09-16
Prime Minister's Inauguration — Prime Minister Lautafi Leuatia Schmidt took office (reported). Criminal trial ongoing (Wikipedia · Model Diplomat).
