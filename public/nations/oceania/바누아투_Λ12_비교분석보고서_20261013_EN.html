§ 01

Executive Summary

Vanuatu has a compendium η of 0.4928 (100th among 197 countries). The maximum cross-sectional pressure is A7 · P=0.578 (measured: aging and population growth), followed by A1 social infrastructure (measured: electricity and internet 0.4905) in second place, and A4 economic stability (0.4478) in third place, all inside the band. Fourth-place A2 institutional trust (0.3888) lies below.

Trajectory: A1 dropped sharply (+0.0380 in 2023; pressure increase). A2 fell from +0.0502 (maximum) in 2017 to +0.0252 in 2023, then departed by -0.0155 in 2024. Governance A8 fell by +0.0180 in 2022 and departed by -0.0100 in 2024. ACLED events are virtually nonexistent, occurring at 0–3 per year.

On the political axis, three prime ministers between 2022 and 2024, repeated no-confidence votes, a constitutional referendum to prevent party defections in 2024, the dissolution of parliament in November 2024, an earthquake in December, and an early general election in January 2025 followed. The compendium includes this country in the data edition, so there is no narrative briefing — political developments were sourced from open sources for this survey.

§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

2022
Early General Election — Voting rate 43.78% (Islands Business·Wikipedia).
2023-08~10
No-Confidence Chain — August no-confidence vote against Prime Minister Ismael Kalsakau, re-elected in September but again voted out, replaced by Charlotte Salwai (Freedom House·NIT).
2024
Stabilization Constitutional Amendments — Two constitutional amendments passed by parliament and ratified by referendum to bind MPs to their parties and require independents and one-person parties to join major parties during parliamentary terms (ConstitutionNet).
2024-11
Dissolution of Parliament — At the brink of a no-confidence vote, President dissolved parliament at Prime Minister Salwai's request (NIT·AAP).
2024-12-17
Capital Earthquake — Magnitude 7.3–7.4 earthquake struck Efate Island and Port Vila. National Disaster Management Office reported 14 deaths (6 landslide, 4 Villa Bong building, 4 Port Vila Central Hospital), over 200 injured, and destruction of two capital water reservoirs. Subsequent government tallies confirmed 9 deaths (Reuters·Gulf News·VBR).
2025-01-16
Early General Election — Postponed by two days due to the earthquake. No majority party; ruling party with 9 seats (11.07%) as first party, coalition of 5 parties (paper 32 seats) elects Natapei Napuat as prime minister. Voting rate 69% (43.78% in 2022), 15 incumbents defeated, 1 woman out of 52 seats (Islands Business·Wikipedia).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Attempts at Political Stabilization. The 2024 constitutional amendment was an instrument to reduce frequent party defections and no-confidence votes. Subsequently, there were parliamentary dissolutions and snap elections (ConstitutionNet · Freedom House).

Disasters and Recovery. The 2024 earthquake inflicted damage on the capital's water source and buildings; the affected population is estimated at approximately 80,000 people (VBR).

Public Trends. ACLED protests numbered 3 in 2021 and 1 per year from 2022 to 2024. Pressure is recorded through parliamentary no-confidence votes and elections rather than public events.
