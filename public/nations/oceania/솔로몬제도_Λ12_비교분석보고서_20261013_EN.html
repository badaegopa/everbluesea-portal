§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1998~2003
Racial Conflict and Regional Assistance Mission — Dispatch of the Regional Assistance Mission to Solomon Islands (RAMSI) in 2003 following the Guadalcanal conflict (general fact).
2019
Shift in Diplomatic Relations — Shift in diplomatic relations from Taiwan to China (Wikipedia).
2021-11-24~27
Honiara Riots — Protests, arson, and looting following the shift in diplomatic relations; destruction of buildings adjacent to Parliament; three bodies found in shops in Chinatown; over 100 arrested. Damage: AFP reported at least $28 million initially, central bank estimated at least $227 million (Honiara lost more than half of its GDP of approximately 30%) (Wikipedia, SBS, CBSI).
2022-04-19
Security Agreement with China — China confirmed signing; described as a government-to-government security cooperation framework 'not targeting third countries.' Australia and the US strongly opposed; then-prime minister stated there were no Chinese military bases (VOA, Janes).
2024-04-17~05-02
General Election and Prime Minister Election — As the first party with 15 seats out of the ruling OUR Party's 50 seats. Former Foreign Minister Jeremiah Manele, who secured support from 21 independents, was elected in a parliamentary secret ballot by 31 to 18 (Al Jazeera·AP).
2025-04~05
No-Confidence Motion — Lost majority due to exit of 10 coalition MPs; no-confidence motion tabled. One report stated that the opposition withdrew its consent, while another reported it was defeated in the vote. The Prime Minister remained in office (NIT·PMN).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Foreign Relations. In 2022 security agreements, China stated it does not target third parties, while Australia and the United States expressed concerns. This report records both sides' positions verbatim.

Parliamentary Politics. Because of the high proportion of independent MPs, the structure where the election of the Prime Minister and no-confidence motions were determined by MP movements was repeated in 2024–2025 (Asia Society·NIT).

Public Trends. ACLED protests numbered 9 in 2021, 5 in 2022, and 2 in 2023, with records missing for 2018, 2020, and 2024.
