Previous Trajectory

The Yoon Suk-yeol administration declared emergency martial law on December 3, 2024, and the National Assembly passed an impeachment motion on December 14. On April 4, 2025, the Constitutional Court unanimously upheld the impeachment and dismissed Yoon Suk-yeol, leading to an early presidential election held within 60 days.

Direction of the Current Administration

The Lee Jae-myung administration announced 123 national policy tasks in August 2025, pivoting toward constitutional revision, decentralizing the authority of the prosecution, police, and Board of Audit and Inspection, and fostering growth centered on strategic industries such as AI, semiconductors, and bio (National Growth Fund). North Korea policy is shifting toward 'reconciliation and cooperation,' while labor policy is transitioning toward expanding prime contractor liability (Yellow Envelope Act) and broadening the application of labor law to small workplaces. Fiscal policy follows an expansionary stance, with the national debt ratio projected to rise from 49.1% in 2025 to 51.6% in 2026.

Chronology 2024–2026

  • 2024-12-03President Yoon Suk-yeol declared emergency martial law.[1]
  • 2024-12-14The National Assembly passed an impeachment motion against President Yoon Suk-yeol, and massive rallies calling for impeachment were held nationwide, including in Seoul.[1][9]
  • 2025-04-04The Constitutional Court unanimously upheld the impeachment, removing President Yoon Suk-yeol from office.[1]
  • 2025-06-04Winning the early presidential election on June 3, President Lee Jae-myung took office without a transition committee period.[2]
  • 2025-07-03The National Assembly passed the confirmation motion for Prime Minister Kim Min-seok with 173 votes in favor (People Power Party boycotted the vote).[3]
  • 2025-08-13The government announced 123 national policy tasks, including a plan to invest 210 trillion won over five years.[4]
  • 2025-08-24The National Assembly passed an amendment to the Trade Union Act (Yellow Envelope Act) expanding the scope of prime contractors as employers.[5]
  • 2025-10-29A South Korea–US tariff agreement was reached on the sidelines of the APEC summit, adjusting tariffs on Korean automobiles from 25% to 15% alongside investment commitments to the US.[7]
  • 2026-02-19The Seoul Central District Court sentenced former President Yoon Suk-yeol to life imprisonment on charges of leading an insurrection (the prosecution had sought the death penalty).[8]
  • 2026-06-03In the nationwide local elections, the Democratic Party of Korea secured 12 metropolitan mayoral and gubernatorial seats, while the People Power Party won 4 (Seoul, Daegu, Gyeongbuk, and Gyeongnam), with a voter turnout of 61%.[10]

Public Trends

Following the martial law declaration in December 2024, rallies calling for impeachment took place across the country, including Seoul (reportedly drawing around 85,000 people), Busan, Daegu, and Gwangju. Voter turnout in the June 2026 local elections was 61%, reported as the highest since the resumption of local elections in 1995. In March 2026, a rally opposing US and Israeli airstrikes on Iran drew hundreds of participants in Seoul.

Data Caution

The size of the National Growth Fund was reported as 100 trillion won during the announcement of national tasks in August 2025, but was cited as 150 trillion won in December 2025 materials, showing discrepancy across reporting periods. The IMF (2025.11) projected economic growth at 0.9% for 2025 and 1.8% for 2026; actual figures for 2026 remain unconfirmed.

Previous Trajectory

The Shigeru Ishiba Cabinet (October 2024–October 2025) dissolved the House of Representatives immediately after inauguration, but the LDP–Komeito coalition consecutively lost its majority in the October 2024 general election and the July 2025 House of Councillors election. Distrust arising from the political funds (slush fund) scandal was reported as the background, and Ishiba announced his resignation in September 2025. In October 2025, Komeito withdrew from the 26-year coalition.

Current Government Direction

The Takaichi government formed a coalition with the Japan Innovation Party and is pivoting toward strengthening defense capabilities. It announced that defense spending reached the 2% of GDP level ahead of the original target (FY2027) via the FY2025 supplementary budget, and is pursuing revisions to the three security documents including the National Security Strategy, deregulation of defense equipment exports, and discussions on the Three Non-Nuclear Principles. Having secured the threshold to initiate constitutional amendment (two-thirds of the House of Representatives) following the February 2026 general election, it is moving toward discussions on revising Article 9 of the Constitution.

In the February 2026 general election, entry of new parties into the Diet was observed, including Sanseito with 15 seats and Team Mirai with 11 seats.
