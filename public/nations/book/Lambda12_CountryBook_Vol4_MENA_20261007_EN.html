Prior Trajectory

Under Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, the regime maintained a standoff against the United States over nuclear issues and sanctions until his death in a joint U.S.–Israeli airstrike on February 28, 2026. On December 28, 2025, a collapse in the rial amid sanctions triggered nationwide protests, which authorities suppressed in January 2026 by shutting down the internet and international phone lines.

Direction of Current Government

Under Mojtaba Khamenei, the regime continues its military standoff with the United States and Israel while pivoting toward institutionalizing control over the Strait of Hormuz (imposing transit tolls). Following the June Memorandum of Understanding (MoU), negotiations have repeatedly stalled and resumed, with reports of contacts between U.S. and Iranian delegations on the sidelines of the September UN General Assembly. Under a U.S. naval blockade and 'Operation Economic Outcast' sanctions, central bank currency defense measures continue.

Chronology 2024–2026

  • 2025-12-28Nationwide protests erupted following the collapse of the rial, and on 2026-01-08 authorities cut off internet access and international phone communications.[7]
  • 2026-02-28Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed in a joint U.S.–Israeli airstrike; Iran responded with missile and drone strikes and a blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.[11][12]
  • 2026-03-08The Assembly of Experts elected Mojtaba Khamenei as the third Supreme Leader.[11]
  • 2026-04-08A two-week ceasefire brokered by Pakistan went into effect, but negotiations in Islamabad on April 11–12 broke down.[12]
  • 2026-04-13U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) initiated a naval blockade of vessels entering and exiting Iranian ports.[1][13]
  • 2026-06-17President Trump and President Pezeshkian signed an MoU stipulating a 60-day negotiation window, lifting of the blockade, and free transit through Hormuz; the blockade was lifted the following day.[4][13]
  • 2026-07-14CENTCOM resumed the naval blockade.[1][2]
  • 2026-08-17The 60-day MoU deadline expired without a final agreement, and the Iranian parliament passed legislation asserting control over Hormuz and imposing transit tolls of up to 7% of cargo value.[3]
  • 2026-08-25The U.S. Department of the Treasury announced 'Operation Economic Outcast', covering five sectors—digital assets, technology, gold, aviation, and shipping—along with secondary sanctions.[5][9]
  • 2026-09-23President Pezeshkian affirmed the right to self-defense in a speech at the UN General Assembly, while simultaneous discussions between U.S. and Iranian delegations were reported.[10]

Public Trends

Protests sparked by economic hardship spread nationwide in late December 2025, and human rights group HRANA recorded at least 7,002 deaths from crackdowns as of February 11, 2026. During the wartime period (2026-09), long queues at Tehran gas stations and crackdowns on diesel smuggling were reported. Beyond these accounts, reports of large-scale anti-government protests could not be confirmed within the verified scope (coverage by AP and GlobalSecurity) after March 2026.

Discrepancies in Figures

[War Casualties] Iranian government sources: Foundation of Martyrs and Veterans Affairs reported 3,468 deaths (announced 2026-04-19), Legal Medicine Organization reported 3,375 (04-12). Other sources: HRANA reported 3,636 (04-07; 1,701 civilians, 1,221 military, 714 unclassified, roughly 170–260 higher than government figures), Hengaw tallied military casualties alone at a minimum of 6,620 (04-08), and JINSA (a pro-Israel U.S. think tank) reported cumulative casualties exceeding 3,724 dead and over 26,580 injured (2026-09-04). AFP stated independent verification was impossible. [Deaths from January 2026 Protest Crackdown] Iranian government 3,117 (2026-01-21) vs. HRANA over 7,002 (02-11), a discrepancy of approximately 3,900. [Economy] Statistical Center of Iran official year-on-year inflation rate 89%, open market exchange rate approximately 2.2 million rials per dollar (2026-09-02). (Sources: 6, 8, 9, 14)

Data Caution

Independent verification of casualty figures is impossible due to wartime internet shutdowns and restrictions on field access. No public video or audio of Mojtaba Khamenei has been confirmed since March 2026, leading to conflicting claims regarding his health between Iranian authorities (normal condition) and reports citing U.S. and Israeli intelligence (critically injured). Accounts of the signing location of the June MoU diverge: NPR's published text and Wikipedia indicate the Palace of Versailles, whereas Kurdistan24 reports Islamabad. Crisis Group's October 2026 Iran risk alert could not be verified due to blocked access to the original text.
