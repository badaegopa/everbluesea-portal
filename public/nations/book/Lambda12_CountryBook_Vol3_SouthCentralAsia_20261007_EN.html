Previous Trajectory

Prime Minister Modi launched his third administration following his 2024 reelection. Following the Pahalgam terrorist attack in April 2025 (26 dead), 'Operation Sindoor' struck 9 locations in Pakistan and Pakistan-administered Kashmir on May 7, leading to a ceasefire established on May 10. Tariff disputes with the United States continued during this period.

Direction of Current Government

In external economic affairs, the government agreed to an interim trade agreement framework with the United States in February 2026, transitioning toward lowering tariffs against the US and reducing dependence on Russian crude oil. Real GDP growth for the 2025/26 fiscal year was officially announced by the government at 7.7%. In educational administration, the government is shifting toward reorganizing its accountability framework following the exam-rigging scandal, marked by the replacement of the Education Minister (July 2026).

Chronology 2024–2026

  • 2025-04-22An attack by militants in Pahalgam, Jammu and Kashmir killed 26 people.[1][2]
  • 2025-05-07The Ministry of Defence announced that the Indian military struck 9 locations within Pakistan and Pakistan-administered Kashmir under 'Operation Sindoor.'[1]
  • 2025-05-10A ceasefire was established between India and Pakistan.[2]
  • 2026-02-07India and the US announced an interim trade agreement framework, lowering tariffs on Indian goods from 25% to 18% and revoking a separate 25% additional tariff.[3]
  • 2026-04In state legislative assembly elections, the BJP won in West Bengal (207 of 294 seats) and Assam (92 of 126 seats), while the UDF took first place in Kerala, and the newly formed TVK became the largest party in Tamil Nadu.[4]
  • 2026-05Amid the cancellation and re-administration of the exam due to the NEET-UG 2026 question paper leak, the youth online movement 'Cockroach Janta Party (CJP)' emerged.[5]
  • 2026-06-05The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) announced a real GDP growth rate of 7.7% for the 2025/26 fiscal year.[6]

Public Trends

The Gen Z online movement 'Cockroach Janta Party (CJP),' which began in May 2026, triggered by the NEET paper leak and demanding the Education Minister's resignation, expanded into nationwide protests in July. AFP reported the July 20 march toward Parliament as 'one of the largest street rallies in Delhi in about five years.' The movement broadened its agenda to unemployment and youth opportunity issues, and negotiations with the government proceeded following the minister's resignation.

Discrepancies in Figures

The 26 fatalities in Pahalgam match across sources, but the composition descriptions differ (Tribune: 25 Indians, 1 Nepali / Hitavada: 25 tourists, 1 local). The number of arrests in the July protests remains unverified.

Data Caveats

Previous Trajectory

Following military clashes with India in May 2025, a ceasefire was reached. On November 13, 2025, parliament passed the 27th Constitutional Amendment, upgrading the Chief of Army Staff to 'Commander-in-Chief of Defense Forces' and creating a Federal Constitutional Court dedicated to constitutional cases. The government cited 'easing the judicial burden' as the rationale, while the opposition PTI criticized the process.

Current Government Direction

Security policy is shifting toward an offensive counterterrorism posture including cross-border airstrikes against TTP and IS-K strongholds in Afghanistan (Operation 'Ghazab lil-Haq' since February 2026). Domestically, the system is being reorganized by codifying the military command structure into the constitution and transferring constitutional jurisdiction from the Supreme Court to the newly created court.

Chronology 2024–2026

  • 2025-05-10A ceasefire was established following military clashes with India.[8]
  • 2025-11-13The 27th Constitutional Amendment passed the National Assembly (234 in favor, 4 opposed) and the Senate, and was signed by the President.[1][2]
  • 2026-01-08The International Crisis Group stated that Pakistan–Taliban relations had deteriorated to 'unprecedented levels,' tallying over 600 security personnel killed in militant attacks in 2025.[3]
  • 2026-02-06Multiple fatalities occurred in a suicide bombing at an Islamabad mosque (reported as carried out by IS-K).[4][5]
  • 2026-02-21Pakistan conducted airstrikes on seven militant strongholds in Nangarhar, Paktika, and Khost provinces in Afghanistan.[4]
  • 2026-03-16A drug rehabilitation facility near Kabul (Camp Omid) was hit by airstrikes, resulting in multiple casualties.[6][7]
  • 2026-08-21The military announced that security forces killed 49 militants in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (12) and Balochistan (37).[9]

Public Trends

No reports of large-scale public protests were identified within this monitoring scope (media from 2025-11 to 2026-09). The opposition PTI criticized the 27th Constitutional Amendment process in parliament. Border clashes with Afghanistan prompted border closures and school shutdowns reported on both sides (EUAA).

Numerical Discrepancies

Afghan conflict casualties (as of 2026-03-15, synthesized from Wikipedia): Pakistani government reported '684 Taliban fighters killed' / Taliban claimed 'over 307 Pakistani soldiers killed' (on Feb 26, Taliban claimed '55 killed' while Pakistan announced '2'). Feb 6 mosque bombing deaths: 31 (Wikipedia) / 36 (HSToday).
