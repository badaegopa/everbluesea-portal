Previous Trajectory

In 2025, the second Trump administration imposed reciprocal and drug-related tariffs pursuant to the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), and from October to November 2025, the longest federal government shutdown in history lasted 43 days. Direction of the Current Government

Externally, it is shifting toward expanding intervention in the Western Hemisphere and Middle East by combining military force and economic sanctions, marked by the operation to capture Venezuelan President Maduro in January 2026, an executive order threatening tariffs on countries supplying oil to Cuba in January, and joint airstrikes with Israel on Iran in February. Trade policy is shifting its legal foundation to temporary tariffs based on Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs. Internally, large-scale immigration enforcement and deportations continue, ahead of the November 2026 midterm elections.

Previous Trajectory

Following the resignation of Prime Minister Justin Trudeau, Mark Carney, former governor of the Bank of Canada and the Bank of England, became Liberal Party leader and Prime Minister in March 2025. In the general election on April 28, 2025, the Liberal Party formed a minority government with 169 of 343 seats, while Conservative Party (144 seats) leader Pierre Poilievre lost the riding he had held since 2004.

Current Government Direction

The Carney government is shifting toward trade and diplomatic diversification, reducing dependence on the U.S. and emphasizing solidarity among 'middle powers' in response to tariff and annexation threats from the Trump administration. Following the breakdown of trade negotiations in 2026, it imposed retaliatory tariffs against new U.S. tariffs. Domestically, it secured a majority (173 seats) in April 2026 through by-election victories and floor-crossing by opposition MPs, and is advancing legislation (Bill C-34) banning social media use for minors under 16.

Chronology 2024–2026

  • 2025-03Following the resignation of Prime Minister Trudeau, Mark Carney took office as Prime Minister.[3]
  • 2025-04-28In the general election, a Liberal minority government was inaugurated with 169 seats for the Liberal Party, 144 for the Conservative Party, 22 for the Bloc Québécois, 7 for the New Democratic Party, and 1 for the Green Party.[1]
  • 2026-04-13The Liberal Party won two by-elections in Ontario and recruited five opposition MPs over five months, securing 173 seats (majority threshold: 172 seats).[2][3]
  • 2026-09-08President Trump signed an executive order banning imports of Canadian liquor, motorcycles, dairy products, and other goods (reported as a response following Canada's retaliatory tariffs after the breakdown of negotiations).[4]
  • 2026-09-10The Carney cabinet held a two-day meeting in Banff to discuss responses to the trade war and the Alberta referendum.[4]
  • 2026-10-19A referendum asking whether to hold a revote on secession and independence is scheduled in Alberta (initiated by citizen petition).[4][5]

Public Trends

Ahead of the secession-related referendum initiated by citizen petition in Alberta (October 19, 2026), the pro-remain 'Forever Canadian' campaign (led by former Deputy Premier Thomas Lukaszuk) is active, while Prime Minister Carney stated that the federal government would not fund the remain campaign. Prior Trajectory

Current Government's Direction

The Paz government is pivoting from a state-led economy toward market liberalization, announcing the abolition of fuel subsidies in December 2025 (Supreme Decree No. 5503), the lifting of the Boliviano's dollar peg in June 2026, and plans for privatizing the electricity sector and opening mining to foreign investment. Externally, it restored relations with the United States, resuming U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) activities after 17 years, and reopened dialogue with multilateral financial institutions. It responded to large-scale blockade protests in May–June 2026 with legislation deploying the military and a 90-day state of emergency.
