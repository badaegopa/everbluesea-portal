Executive Summary

Poland has a Series η of 0.6079 (18th among 197 countries). The maximum cross-sectional pressure is A5 · P=0.473 (measured: R&D/GDP), followed by 2nd-ranked A4 (0.3391), 3rd-ranked A7 (0.3259), 4th-ranked A6 (0.2926), 5th-ranked A2 (0.2772), and 6th-ranked A8 (0.2357) in the pressure ranking table.

Core of the Trajectory: A2 Institutional Trust (measured: CPI · control of corruption) fell (pressure increased) for seven consecutive years from 2017 to 2023, with +0.0204 being the largest in 2023. In 2024, it deviated sharply (-0.0197), indicating a reduction in pressure. A8 governance also deviated, with -0.0131 in 2023 and -0.0302 in 2024. The periods of the rule-of-law dispute surrounding judicial reforms and the period after the 2023 general election when the Tusk government campaigned on 'restoring the rule of law' are recorded directly in the trajectory.

However, in the June 2025 presidential election, PiS-backed candidate Nawrocki won with 50.89%, initiating a cohabitation in which the president and the government belong to different camps. In June 2026, the president exercised his 37th veto, surpassing the historical record; after vetoing the EU defense loan (SAFE) bill, the government circumvented it through borrowing via state banks. The deadlock of 2025–2026, which annual indicators have not yet captured, is the object of observation for the next trajectory.

§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Co-habitation Politics. According to the Country Analysis Series briefing (as of 2026-10-07), the president has exercised his veto power on judicial reform, EU defense loans, the implementation of digital services, and cryptocurrency regulation. In February 2026, citing '132 signed, 20 vetoed since taking office,' he defended the exercise of the veto and called for dialogue and respect for the constitution (Polskie Radio). Credit rating agencies noted that the deadlock between the president and the government burdens fiscal stability and policy-making capacity (cited by Notes from Poland).

Security and Re-Armament. The government established the Ministry of Energy in July 2025, expanded the authority of the Minister of National Defense, and increased defense procurement, including selecting Sweden for the Orka submarine program. Following the rejection of the SAFE loan, it is continuing to fund rearmament through state bank borrowing under the name of the Military Support Fund (Series briefing).

Economy and Public Opinion. Based on provisional figures from Statistics Poland (GUS), real growth in 2025 was 3.6% (3.0% in 2024). In a November 2025 survey, 32.1% of respondents wanted the emergence of a new party, and the far-right 'Polish Crown Confederation' had support near 10% (Series briefing). Large-scale street collective action in 2026 was not reported within the scope of observation.

Public Trends. According to ACLED tallies, protests in Poland are frequent—1,835 events in 2020 and 1,536 in 2024—but political violence fatalities number 0–1 per year (2020–2024). Pressure is expressed on the streets, at polling stations, and now through the presidential veto.

The decisive variable distinguishing SN from BH is C_internal (elite internal conflict energy). This is a metaphor and does not claim astrophysical correspondence.

Poland is a case of 'rival camps rotating within institutions while contesting the institutions themselves.' The retreat of 2016–2023, the reversal of 2024, and the deadlock since 2025 all emerged from election results. It resembles D-3 (types outside the categories) more closely than a collapse path: 'high-intensity competition within institutions' (in the same category as Kosovo).
