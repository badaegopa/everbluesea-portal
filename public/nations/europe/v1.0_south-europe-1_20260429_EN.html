══ Research Purpose ══
Declaration of Research Purpose

Spain·Italy·Greece·Portugal — the four countries once called PIIGS. How do the scars created by the 2010s financial crisis and the austerity prescriptions of the Troika (EU·ECB·IMF) remain as of 2025? Has the economy recovered? — The numbers say yes. Have the lives of the people recovered? — The rise of populism answers no. The four countries share the same scars, but each is walking in a different direction. We read the structure of that divergence through Λ¹².

══ PIIGS Special Feature ══
⚡  PIIGS Special Feature — The Scars of Austerity and the Rise of Populism
Why Did PIIGS Become a Breeding Ground for Populism? — Λ¹² Structural Dissection
2008 Financial Crisis → 2010 Debt Crisis → Troika Austerity → Populism Explosion. This chain of events is the common narrative of the four Southern European countries.

Youth unemployment rate at the time of the debt crisis: Greece 59.5% Spain 55% Italy 40% Portugal 38%. These numbers are the trauma of the generation that has now reached their 30s. That a generation that spent their 20s without jobs does not trust established parties is exactly the trajectory predicted by Gurr's relative deprivation theory.

Two Directions of Populism:
Left-wing Populism — Greece SYRIZA, Spain Podemos: "The Troika exploited us.".
Right-wing Populism — Italy Fratelli d'Italia, Portugal Chega: "Immigrants and elites betrayed us.".

Λ¹² Interpretation: What austerity created was not simply economic pain — the structural blocking of PWT-r was what occurred. Even when a leader poured priming water, the Troika's conditional budget blocked the pipe. The reason β (blocking coefficient) went up was not just due to corruption, but because an external pipe blocker called the EU bureaucracy was added. This is the explanation of the Λ¹² structure of populism in the four Southern European countries.

ⅩⅤ  Southern Europe from the Perspective of the Korean Peninsula
Korean Peninsula Perspective
Korea went through the IMF crisis in 1997. Greece had the same experience in 2010. However, Korea graduated from it in 4 years, whereas Greece took 10 years—viewing this difference through the lens of Λ¹²: Korea had a manufacturing base, while Greece had a single economy of tourism and shipping. This is the difference in F_reach S (self-sufficiency).

Italy's "Two Italies"—the North-South gap—structurally resembles Korea's capital-provincial gap. In a country with a high CFD, priming water cannot flow from the center to the provinces. This is why Korea's provincial extinction problem overlaps with the story of Southern Europe.

The rise of Portugal's Chega is a signal that Korea should also pay attention to. Why does populism grow when macro indicators are good?—Because distribution has failed. The gap between GDP growth rate figures and the perceived economy—this gap is also raising Σ in Korea.

Economic Connection: Hyundai and Kia have European factories in Spain and the Czech Republic. If the Southern European economy falters, South Korea's car exports are directly affected.
