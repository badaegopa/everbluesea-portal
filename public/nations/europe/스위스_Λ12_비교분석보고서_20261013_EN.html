§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Labor Market. The ILO-standard unemployment rate from the FSO's Swiss Labour Force Survey (SAKE) stood at 4.9% in Q2 2026, lower than Q1 (5.2%) and higher than the same quarter of the previous year (4.6%). Over the same period, SECO's registered unemployment rate was approximately 3.0% (140,275 registered unemployed at the end of May 2026; seasonally adjusted 3.1%). The EU's ILO-standard unemployment rate was 5.9% in the same quarter (blue News).

Trade. In November 2025, the US, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein agreed on a framework to lower tariffs from 39% to 15%. The 15% includes existing duties such as most-favored-nation rates (Bloomberg). It began as a political agreement in principle targeting finalization by Q1 2026 — whether a binding final agreement was signed was not confirmed in this investigation.

Direct Democracy. The June 2026 'Against a 10-Million Switzerland' initiative sought to curb population growth, which the Federal Council and Parliament opposed citing threats to the free movement of persons agreement with the EU. The September neutrality reinforcement initiative aimed to limit the government's authority to impose economic sanctions. Demographics and foreign relations are visibly dominating direct democracy agendas.

Public Trends. According to ACLED data, Switzerland experiences 166–227 demonstrations per year and 0–2 political violence fatalities (2020–2024). Conflicts move to the ballot box rather than the streets.
