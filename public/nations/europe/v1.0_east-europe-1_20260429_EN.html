══ Research Purpose ══
Statement of Research Purpose

The four Eastern European countries transitioned to democracy after the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1989–1991. Was that transition entirely successful? — No, it was not. Hungary and Poland dismantled democracy using democratic mechanisms. The "structure of concentrating power through legal means" spoken of by Weber — textbook cases of Levitsky & Ziblatt's "How Democracies Die" emerged from Eastern Europe. The democratic fightback of 2023–2026 has begun. We read this moment through Λ¹².

Combination of Ceaușescu dictatorship remnants + new-elite corruption. Vestiges of Weberian traditional authority. Marx: corrupt structure blocking class mobility. Gurr's relative deprivation + Georgescu's populism. Lack of institutional resolution path.
══ Ⅱ History ══
Ⅱ  History — From Soviet Satellite State to EU Member State
🇵🇱 Poland
1939~45Double occupation by Nazis and Soviet Union. 17% of total population killed (6 million people). Maximum TBI.
1945~89Soviet satellite state. Solidarity (Solidarność) movement resistance. TBI + resistance tradition.
1989Non-violent Round Table democratization. First in Eastern Europe.
2015~23PiS in power — capture of judiciary, media, and electoral system. Rule-of-law conflict with EU. EU fund freeze.
2023~Tusk takes office. Democracy restoration path. EU funds resumed. However, PiS loyalists remain in the Constitutional Tribunal.
★ Highest TBI — Nazi + Soviet double genocide
🇭🇺 Hungary
1945~56Soviet occupation. 1956 Revolution — suppressed by Soviet tanks. Core TBI.
1989Round Table democratization. Orbán was a young democratic activist during this period.
2010Orbán returns to power. Starts constitutional amendments with a two-thirds majority. Declares 'illiberal democracy'.
2018~26EU sanctions and fund freeze. Capture of media, judiciary, and elections. Magyar emerges in 2024.
2026.04Magyar's Tisza Party wins a landslide with 53.6%. Orbán defeated after 16 years.
★ Empirical proof of SR reverse-causality — suppression → threshold → explosion
🇨🇿 Czechia
1938~45Nazi protectorate. Annexation of Sudetenland → full annexation.
1968Prague Spring — suppressed by Soviet tanks. TBI.
1989Velvet Revolution — fully non-violent democratization. Havel becomes President.
1993Velvet Divorce of Czechoslovakia. Peaceful split of Czechia and Slovakia.
2017~21Babiš's ANO populism in power. Corruption scandals. Replaced by Fiala in 2021.
★ Civil society legacy of the Velvet Revolution that prevented Orbánization
🇷🇴 Romania
1947~89Ceaușescu dictatorship 1965–89. Worst dictatorship in Eastern Europe. Extreme TBI.
1989Armed revolution — execution of Ceaușescu. The only violent revolution. Highest TBI.
2004EU and NATO accession. However, remnants of the old Communist Party bureaucratic structures persist.
2017500,000 protesters in anti-corruption demonstrations against corrupt bills — largest pro-democracy protest.
2024Georgescu wins first place in the presidential election → Constitutional Court nullifies election → Political crisis.
★ The generation that witnessed Ceaușescu's execution is still in politics

══ ⅩⅤ Korean Peninsula ══
ⅩⅤ  Eastern Europe as Seen from the Korean Peninsula
Korean Peninsula Perspective
The overwhelming victory of Magyar in Hungary in 2026 is an important message for South Korea. A 16-year authoritarian regime has ended through an election. But how was this possible—the 80% turnout is the answer. It is because the citizens participated. The SR reverse-causality hypothesis worked here as well: when the suppressed resistance crossed the threshold, people went to the polling stations. South Korea's tradition of high voter turnout is meaningful in this context.

Poland's PiS case—it resembles the structure of the Park Geun-hye impeachment experienced by South Korea. A government that came into power through an election tries to seize control of institutions and is stopped by citizen resistance. The difference is that Poland was replaced through an election, not impeachment. Which method is a more powerful signal of democracy?

Romania's Georgescu incident—it overlaps with the context of the 2024 martial law incident in South Korea. When establishment institutions invalidate election results—is this a defense of democracy or the elites blocking the public's will? This question was raised simultaneously in both countries. It is a clash of the 5th-layer frames of the Six-Layer Discourse Reasoning Method.

Economic Connection: Poland is the European production hub for Korea's Samsung and LG. Poland's GDP growth of 3.6% is good news for Korean exports. Romania's fiscal crisis threatens the European consumer market.
