§ 00

Position within the Region — Why Andorra?

This report was selected, in the Western Europe round of the daily regional rotation pipeline, as the country with the lowest η according to the Country Analysis Series among countries without a standalone edition. In the Series, Andorra's η is 0.4005(192nd among 197 countries), and it differs substantially from Italy (0.5548), which ranks 15th in Western Europe. The top country in the same round is Switzerland (0.6515).

First, this must be made clear: this ranking should not be read as a measurement of instability in Andorran society. In the Series, only seven of the twelve dimensions were measured for Andorra (missing A11, A2, A3, A8, and A9 — institutional trust, governance, civil liberties, environmental pressure, and external shocks are all missing), the BSLI was not computed, and there is no Series trajectory file (not included in orbit_all). Even the country name field is blank in the Series. In the raw data diagnosis of this report (read-only), it was confirmed that indicators on the institutional axis (WGI series) were aggregated separately under the World Bank old code 'ADO' and were not combined into the canonical 'AND'. The engine was not modified according to the freeze principle, and this matter was recorded as 'canonical verification required'.

In accordance with the data-poor country principle (DECISIONS 3, Cuba precedent), this report also serves as a qualitative diagnosis. The seven measured dimensions are presented as in the Series, and actual social pressure is examined separately through public sources — there is pressure in Andorra that was not measured but exists (housing crisis, debate over the EU association agreement).

§ 01

Executive Summary

Andorra's Series η of 0.4005 is the simple average of the seven measured dimensions: A1 (social infrastructure, 0.9700), A12 (0.8888), A7 (0.5574), A6 (0.1933), A4 (0.1255), A10 (0.0444), and A5 (0.0240). Among these, A5 (R&D/GDP), A10 (absolute number of tourists), and A4 (GDP/manufacturing) are empirical measurements that lie at the bottom for a small service/tourism economy, while the institutional axes (A2·A8), A9, and A3 that raise η in most European countries are missing. This is a configuration that cannot but yield a low average (canonical verification required: opposite direction of T-2 missing data bias).

Another structure: A6 (measured: CO₂/renewable energy, 0.1933) has a negative coupling r, so in the pressure coordinate system a lower value indicates less pressure. Yet in the η average, this low value falls on the 'unstable' side — it is the opposite case of P-7 (Liberia and Gabon), where the signs of η and r diverge.

The actual pressure outside measurement is housing. In May 2026, approximately 3,000 people marched amid concerns that rent could rise to market levels as the mandatory lease extension system was phased out. This is a large scale for a country with a population in the 80,000 range. The EU association agreement divides evenly, and 67.6% of respondents said they lacked information.

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1278
Condominium Agreement (Pareys) — A system began in which Bishop of Urgell and Count of Foix jointly held suzerainty. The status of Count of Foix later passed to the head of state of France, so today the Bishop of Urgell and the President of France are joint heads of state (general fact).
1993
Constitution and UN Membership — The first written constitution was adopted by referendum, establishing parliamentary democracy, and the country joined the United Nations in the same year (general fact).
2010s
Financial Liberalization — Tax policy was aligned with European standards, including the phased lifting of banking secrecy and the introduction of income tax (general fact; specific years not cross-checked against original sources).
2022–2026
Housing Crisis — As rents rose owing to population growth and demand for tourism and migration, the government proposed legislation gradually repealing the mandatory lease extension regime. The government stated that it had returned 1,530 units to the housing market and built more than 500 of the first public affordable homes.
2026-05-16
Housing Marches — Housing marches took place in Andorra la Vella and Escaldes-Engordany (estimated at approximately 3,000 participants by the unions). The unions advocated for housing reform, while the government defended a gradual repeal bill. Prime Minister Esport presented separate protection for contracts expiring in 2022.
2026
EU Association Agreement — The government stated that the process would proceed in the order of EU Council finalization → European Parliament vote → information provision referendum → Grand Council approval. The referendum rules and budget for July were finalized. Some opposition figures demand a binding referendum before signature. Bulgaria's review delayed EU approval by one week (alto.ad).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Housing. The May 16, 2026 march concerned the repeal of the mandatory lease extension regime. Proponent analyses argued that the repeal would expose thousands of households to rent increases of up to 40% aligned with market prices (Andorra Insiders — read only as analysis). The government claimed the return of 1,530 units to the market and more than 500 public affordable homes as achievements, and the prime minister announced separate protection for contracts expiring in 2022 (alto.ad).

EU Association Agreement and Public Opinion. In AR+I Observatory's Q3 2025 survey, 35.2% of respondents expected the agreement to be mainly positive, 34% negative, and 20.1% (the highest figure) undecided, while 67.6% said they lacked information. There is also analysis that housing issues could translate into a protest vote in the association referendum (interpretation).

Demographics. Reports exist that parties debate balanced growth amid a '10% population increase', but the period and nationality composition could not be verified against the original source (Statistics Office).

Records Outside Measurement. The Series contains no cross-check with ACLED — the ACLED-linked data in vault are also separately coded as 'ADO'. What the engine sees for this country is not part of society, but only part of the indicators that matched the code.

Why the scores are low. A4, A5, and A10 are measured values that structurally bottom out in a small service and tourism economy (manufacturing share, research investment ratio, absolute tourist arrivals). Monaco (0.6500), San Marino (0.6299), and Liechtenstein (0.6264) — similarly small Western European states — are positioned at the top because their institutional axes were measured — the difference with Andorra arises from measurement composition rather than society (interpretation).

Missing dimensions. A2, A3, A8, A9, and A11 were not populated. This report does not arbitrarily import values that exist under a different code (ADO) and recalculate η — that would be changing the engine (DECISIONS 13·18).
