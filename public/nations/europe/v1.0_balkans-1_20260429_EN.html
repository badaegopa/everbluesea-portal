Declaration of Research Purpose

The Balkans are the region with the highest TBI in Λ¹² history. The Yugoslav Wars of the 1990s — the 1,425-day Siege of Sarajevo, the Srebrenica massacre of 8,000 people, the Kosovo War — this history dominates the language of current politics. We read three different aftermath treatments of the same war through Λ¹².

Korean Peninsula Perspective
Bosnia has a structure most similar to the division of the Korean Peninsula. A place that was once a single country was divided after war, and the conflict was frozen by international agreement (Dayton = the Armistice Agreement of the Korean Peninsula). Thirty years have passed, but there is no reconciliation. The three ethnic groups are officially one country, but practically live as different political units. In discussions on Korean Peninsula unification, Bosnia is frequently cited as a "precedent of failed reintegration" — by observing why the Dayton system is unsustainable.

The Novi Sad protests in Serbia resemble the post-Sewol ferry trend and structure in South Korea. When citizens die due to state corruption — and no one takes responsibility — citizens take to the streets. The SR reverse-causation hypothesis worked in South Korea as well. Candlelight protests after the Sewol ferry, and then impeachment. Where its end will go in Serbia is the open question of the 6th layer of the Six-Layer Discourse.

Croatia's EU accession path — shows that South Korea's integration into an economic community has the effect of absorbing TBI. It is similar to the structure in which South Korea's US-ROK alliance and Korea-EU FTA contribute to regime stability.
