Executive Summary

Norway has a Series η of 0.6819 (1st among 197 countries), and its cross-sectional maximum pressure is A5 · P=0.444 (measured: R&D/GDP), just below the band (0.4458). Second on the pressure ranking is A6 (P 0.4256, measured: CO₂ · renewable energy), and third is A7 (0.3795). Institutional axes A2 institutional trust (0.9119) and A8 governance (0.9237) are near the very top of the empirical anchors across 197 countries (Series: comparative group extremes 98.6% and 99.3%).

The gap between published figures and independent sources manifests in this country as an issue of 'boundaries.' Territorial emissions within national borders (48.9 million metric tons CO₂e in 2020) and emissions generated from the combustion of exported oil and gas (approx. 500 million metric tons annually) differ by a factor of 10. While its income Gini is among the lowest in the world, Statistics Norway (SSB) research reports that wealth inequality has increased substantially since the 1980s.

§ 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

Climate Policy of a Petrostate. A Norwegian National Human Rights Institution (NHRI) report noted domestic emissions in 2020 at 48.9 million metric tons CO₂e, while emissions from the combustion of exported oil and gas were estimated at approximately 500 million metric tons per year (citing government report NOU 2023:25) — at least 10 times domestic emissions. Carbon Pulse (2025-12) reported on findings that while Norway's domestic climate measures are almost sufficient for the 2°C target, support for oil and gas exports offsets those effects.

Wealth Inequality. A Statistics Norway (SSB) discussion paper (Aaberge, Modalsli, & Solbakken, 2025-10) reported that the wealth Gini from 1912 to 2019 was very high in the early 20th century, declined post-war, and rose substantially after the 1980s driven by an increasing wealth share among the top 1%. A University of Oslo researcher commented that 'income disparity is low, but the wealth gap is close to the United States' (commentary). Engine A3 (measured: income Gini 0.9235) does not capture this wealth dimension.

Public Trends. According to ACLED tallies, protests in Norway are frequent at 300–490 incidents annually, but political violence fatalities remain at 0–2 per year (2020–2024). This reflects a pattern where pressures spill onto the streets but are addressed within institutional channels without violence.
