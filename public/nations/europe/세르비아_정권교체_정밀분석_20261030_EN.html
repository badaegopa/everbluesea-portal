§ 03

Direction by Rate of Change

Cheonghae Hypothesis 3 states that "level values are confirmation stamps, and direction is read from the rate of change in the time series."
This section applies this principle directly to calculate slope and number of increases/decreases for each indicator in the political/economic annual table (Data B) and survey time series (Data A) for each indicator, the slope and number of increases are calculated.
The calculations were performed by the institute's calculation script reading CSV files, and the numbers in the table below are the raw results.

Calculation Method

Annual slope: calculated using only years with values ((year, value) fitted to a least-squares line), unit is indicator unit/year.
Slope since 2020: calculated only for indicators with three or more values after 2020 in the same manner.
Number of declines/rises: counts how many times the value declined or rose from the previous observation, in order (missing years are skipped).
Direction: positive slope (+) was rated as improvement for indicators where higher is better, and negative slope (−) for indicators where lower is better (RSF ranking, inflation, unemployment rate, debt).
Population and remittances were not rated because the direction of good and bad is not specified.
V-Dem regime classification is a categorical value (3 Liberal Democracy → 1 Electoral Autocracy), so the slope is kept only for reference.
Surveys: a %p/month slope fitted to monthly data from the survey start date (or publication date) using least-squares regression.
Calculations were performed only between surveys by the same institution (comparison of levels across institutions is not made because each institution has different inclusion of undecided and question wording).

4-3. Sources of the Sympathetic Forces — Who, When, and What Did They Want to Come Together?

Instructions from Cheonghae: If the established generation sympathetic to youth and those seeking change came together under the gravity of a 'common purpose,' it is necessary to examine the source of each force (§11).
This subsection divides the sympathetic forces by generation, profession, party, region, and diaspora, placing side by side their points of convergence, timing and triggers, unique interests, and similarities and differences with student demands.
Points where they may diverge are noted only as observation items, without judgment.
Of the 20 items in the table of points that could diverge (Data J), 9 have already been reported as disagreements, while the remaining 11 are latent items yet to emerge (calculation).

The Studentska lista – Studenti pobeđuju registered as a civic group rather than a party, and among its 250 candidates there were no students; party officials and SNS career politicians could not run as candidates (Data C).

The forces gathered around it fall into five layers.

1. Core: university plenums (general assemblies of barricaded faculties) and high school students.

Student spokesperson Čičić said that about 250,000 students were organized in the plenums (BBC Serbian edition, reprinted by Danas 2026-10-29).

2. Professional and generational sympathizers: professors, doctors, scientists (the main source of candidates), teachers, lawyers, some judges and prosecutors, actors and cultural figures, farmers, workers and unions, veterans, bikers, and taxi drivers.

They joined in the street phase of 2025 and, in the 2026 election phase, one union (Free Trade Union Confederation SSSS) and more than 170 businesspeople publicly endorsed them.

3. Political parties: 8 parliamentary parties (DS, ZLF, PSG, NPS, Écologie ustanak, NLS, part of POKS, SPO) and numerous extra-parliamentary ones (Kreni-Promeni, Dveri, SDS, DJB, LSV, etc.) did not field candidates or withdrew their lists (N1 2026-09-19, 021.rs 2026-09-16).

Left (ZLF), centrist-liberal (PSG, DS), and right (Dveri, POKS) stood behind a single list.

4. Regions and minorities: 17 regional civic organizations affiliated with 'Free Serbia', groups from Niš, Kragujevac, and Čačak, minority parties of Montenegrin, Macedonian, Ruthenian, and Hungarian origin, and 4–5 Serbian organizations in Kosovo (N1 2026-09-19).

5. Diaspora: 79,597 out of 102,147 registered abroad voted; the student list received 57,756 votes (about 72.8% of valid votes, N1 headline 72.71%) against 19,763 for United Serbia (RIK 2026-10-29, reports by N1 and Telegraf).

Those who did not stand together are also noted.

The pro-EU 'European Serbia' (SSP, SRCE, coalition) ran separately and reached only 0.29%, and some of its officials (e.g., SSP deputy leader Stefanović) withdrew from the list and joined the student side (N1 2026-09-19).

The nationalist 'True Right' (NDSS, part of POKS) also ran separately and reached 0.90% (Wikipedia "2026 Serbian parliamentary election").

The pensioners' party PUPS (professing pensions, farmers, and workers) was within the SNS list (Dan u Beogradu, search summary).

The Orthodox Church leadership (Metropolitan Porphyrios) made neutral statements, and in 2025-05 it called the protests an "alleged color revolution" (RFE/RL 2025-05-18).

The five axes along which differences between forces are most pronounced are as follows (details in §4).

  • Sanctions against Russia and EU: The list formally opposed sanctions (Bakic and Čičić, 10-26–29), while pro-EU supportive parties (ZLF, DS, PSG) and the European Commission (Marta Czarnacka) demand alignment.
  • Kosovo: The list's position of 'constitutional indivisibility' aligns with nationalist supporters, whereas Čičić's remarks on "temporarily occupied territories" conflict with EU normalization conditions (Danas report).
  • Lithium (Zadar): List candidate Kovačević argues that the project should be halted by law, while the European Commission designated Zadar as a strategic project.

  • Presidential candidates: The student side announced its own candidate, saying "dialogue, not negotiations" (10-29), while Ponoš and Miloš Jovanović have spoken of a 'joint candidate' (2026-04–06).

1.1 Student Congress (Plenum) and the Core Student Movement

  • Who: Faculty-by-faculty blockades at national universities, 62 out of 80 faculties as of 2024-12 (Data A), estimated by Sitchić at approximately 250,000 participants (Danas/BBC 2026-10-29).

The generation is roughly those born 1995–2007, with 89.6% supporting protests in the KOMS survey of 18–29-year-olds (Data A).

  • ), when: FDU blockade began on 2024-11-22; the Novi Sad canopy collapse on 2024-11-01 (16 dead) was the trigger (Data A).
  • ), what they want: initial four demands (disclosure of documents, prosecution of those responsible, etc.) → 2025-03 Student Decree → 2025-05 snap general election → 2026-09 11-point student plan (Data H-6).

), core is the demand that 'the institutions must work' and anti-corruption/purification (lustration).

  • Ideology: No official ideology, 'respect for the Constitution' is the minimum line.

The student plan calls for close cooperation with the EU and full accession by referendum, opposes sanctions against Russia, military neutrality, and an 'indivisible' Kosovo (Data H-7, Euronews 2026-10-29).

  • ), relationship between movement and list: Student spokesperson Mićilo Bobić said that "the movement and the student list are separate, each has its own coordinating body, and they cooperate" (Danas/BBC 2026-10-29).

Sitchić stated that "students decide on student matters, and the list is a political expression of demands," and that the prime minister would be elected by MPs in agreement with the movement (same article).

  • Actual case of internal decision-making: In the 2026-09-06 Plenum vote, Belgrade University rector Jokić was removed from candidacy 43:42 (or 43:40, with 2 abstentions), and approximately 35 others were also removed (N1 2026-09-07, 021.rs).

Some faculties expressed dissatisfaction with the result, but a position emerged that "the movement is one" (Vreme, search summary).

1.2 Professors, Universities, and Rectors; Academic Networking

  • Who: Professors, doctors, scientists, some members of the Serbian Academy of Sciences and Arts (SANU).

Notable figures on the list include No. 1 Srdanović (cardiology, Novi Sad Medical Faculty), Sitchić (chemistry, Belgrade University, SANU member), Bakic (sociology), Šoškić (former central bank governor), and Bubnić (physics) (Data C, Euronews).

), many of the 250 candidates are professors, doctors, or psychologists (search summary of Danas candidate list).

  • When: 2024-12 public support by rector Jokić; over 5,000 professors signed; on 2025-03-12, medical faculty professors resolved a general strike (Data A).

), the government's reduction of professorial salaries (mostly 12.5%) acted as retaliation (Data A).

), MASA (Academic Solidarity and Participation Network) urged the opposition to withdraw its candidates (Wikipedia "Student List," date unverified).

  • What they want: university autonomy, restoration of budgets and salaries, research and knowledge economy, appointment of experts (student plan Articles 5 and 7).
  • Ideology: Widely scattered among individuals.

Sitchić described Kosovo as a 'temporarily occupied territory' and said that Russia supported territorial integrity more than the previous government (RTS·KoSSev, 2026-10).

Šoškić made EU accession contingent on 'whether there is an offer of full membership and a referendum' (Euronews 2026-10-29).

  • Common: anti-corruption, rule of law, expert government.
  • Difference: Rector Jokić expressed willingness to run but was excluded by the Plenum.

There had already been friction between the student Plenum's principle of horizontality and the hierarchy and prestige of the professorial body.

After the election, students demanded the resignations of the rectors of Novi Sad University and Niš University (Danas/BBC 2026-10-29), showing that even within universities there is no single bloc.

1.3 Teachers and Teacher Unions

  • Who: Elementary and secondary school teachers.

The unions include USPRS (Federation of Education Workers) and NSPRS (Independent Education Union).

  • When: 2025-01-20, 35% school strike; 2-13, 65% class suspension (PULS); 3-04, retaliation for salary cuts (Data A).

), on 2026-08-04 NSPRS appealed to teachers, parents, and students for support of its anti-government demands (NSPRS website, search summary).

  • What they want: salaries (USPRS: even after the 5.1% raise in 2026-01, new teachers' salaries are approximately 111,478 dinars, about 500 dinars below the national average, 021.rs), withdrawal of strike retaliation, educational autonomy.

The student plan Articles 6 and 10 (free textbooks, low-cost housing for teachers) relate to these interests.

  • Ideology: Not confirmed (no electoral endorsements by unions were found in 2026 materials).
  • Difference (potential): The student plan suggests the possibility of fiscal transparency and austerity (Radosavljević "the first task is to disclose the financial situation," Danas/BBC 10-29), whereas teachers' interests lie in salary increases.

1.4 Bar Associations and Legal Profession

  • Who: Serbian Bar Association (AKS), Belgrade Bar Association (AKB), some judges and prosecutors.
  • When: 2025-01-18, AKS passed a 120/120 resolution for a 7-day work stoppage; 2-02, 30-day strike (Data A).

), open letter by 17 judges and prosecutors (2025-01-23), statement by 220 signatories (2025-03-22) (Data A).

), in early 2026, AKS held a strike vote and presidential election against the judicial reform bill ('Murdich Law'), and a general assembly on 3-07 was scheduled (Danas, search summary).

  • What they want: judicial independence, rule of law, professional interests (opposition to the judicial reform bill).
  • No statement of support for the list by bar associations in the 2026 election phase could be confirmed.
  • Common: student plan Articles 1–2 (special police under direct prosecution jurisdiction, confiscation of illicit property, purification) and alignment with Chapters 23 and 24.
  • Difference (potential): scope of purification and prosecution versus due process.

The student side demanded the criminal accountability and immediate resignation of the 10-29 prosecutor (Danas 2026-10-29).

1.5 Farmers

The list includes farmers, and among the anti-lithium farmer activists are Rozniča ('We will not give Zadar away') and Zeljko Kokanović (AA 2026-10-27; Wiki "Student List").

  • When: Tractor blockades, student escorts, and food aid since 2024-12-13 (Data A, Agroklub 2025).

Separate farmer blockade in February 2026 (Data A).

On 2026-10-16, the same initiative issued a statement urging not to package the use of tractors at the Novi Sad rally on social media as 'farmer support' (N1 headline; Naslovi.net).

  • What they want: Subsidies and purchase prices, curbing import lobbying, protecting farmland and water (student plan Article 9), halting the Zadar lithium project.
  • Ideology: Not confirmed (no official ideological materials for individual groups).
  • Opposition: PUPS (Pensioners, Farmers, Workers Party) was within the SNS list.
  • Differences (potential): EU common agricultural policy standards and import liberalization vs. 'food security' protectionism; lithium (§4).

1.6 Workers and Unions

  • Who: Confederation of Free Trade Unions of Serbia (SSSS), miners, public transport workers (Data A).
  • When: General strike on 2025-01-24, joint May Day rally on 2025-05-01 (Data A).

SSSS appealed for a vote on the student list on 2026-10-09 (N1; signed by Nebojsa Aleksic, president) and 10-22 (Danas/FoNet; signed by Kristina Starčević, vice-president).

  • What they want: Dignified wages and pensions, safety, protection against unfair dismissals, collective bargaining (Danas 2026-10-22).

SSSS stated that "support is not a surrender of union independence; it means monitoring the fulfillment of promises and publicly criticizing any decision that infringes on rights, whoever makes it" (same article).

  • Connection to student plan: Article 8, limit on fixed-term contracts from 24 to 12 months.
  • Other unions: The largest confederation, the Confederation of Independent Trade Unions (Samostalni), and 'Nezavisnost' did not confirm their official stance for the 2026 election.
  • Differences (potential): Unions seek wage and pension increases; key list candidates (Šoškic et al.) are fiscal and monetary experts.

1.7 Businesspeople and Self-Employed

  • Who: Businesspeople signing the open letter "We Believe in a Serbia That Can Do Much Better".

  • What they want: Normal functioning of institutions, legal stability, investment and employment, responsible budgeting (Danas 2026-10-21).
  • Common points: Student plan Articles 3 and 7 (procurement transparency, support for startups and self-employment).

Comparison Cases After a Grand Coalition Government

5.1 Cases Where Internal Division Continued After a Grand Coalition Government

Serbian DOS (2000–2003).

Formed on 2000-01-10 by 19 parties, won 176/250 seats in the 2000-12 Serbian general election, Prime Minister Žutić (Wikipedia "Democratic Opposition of Serbia").

In 2001-07, DSS under Koštunica left the government protesting the extradition of Milošević to the ICTY, and formally withdrew from the coalition in 2002-07.

Žutić assassination on 2003-03-12.

The 2002 and 2003 presidential elections were invalidated three times due to low turnout.

SDP ousted in 2003-11, DOS dissolved on 2003-11-18.

Observation: The divided axis was 'the method of settling the past (extradition to The Hague)' and 'ethnic identity'.

Armenia (2018).

Pashinyan elected Prime Minister on 2018-05-08, government formed on 5-12: the 'exit' coalition minority government included the Charouktsyan Alliance (Prosperous Armenia) and ARF (Wikipedia "First Pashinyan government").

On 2018-10-03, when the two parties supported a bill making early general elections difficult, Pashinyan dismissed six ministers, and ARF denounced it as an attempt at 'one-party rule'.

In the 2018-12-09 snap election, 'My Step' won 70.44% and 88/132 seats, while ARF won 3.89% and 0 seats (Wikipedia "2018 Armenian parliamentary election").

Observation: The path led to the concentration of power under a single force rather than to chaos following the dissolution of the coalition.

Matovič Coalition Government in Slovakia (2020–2023).

2020-03-21 OĽaNO, We Are Family, SaS, and For the People formed a four-party government (Wikipedia "Igor Matovič").

In 2021-03, conflict over the introduction of Sputnik V; on 2021-03-30 Matovič resigned, and on 2021-04-01 Heger became Prime Minister while Matovič became Finance Minister in a swap of positions.

In 2022-09, SaS left the coalition demanding Matovič's dismissal, forming a minority government (Euronews 2022-09-05).

On 2022-12-15, a motion of no confidence was passed with 78 out of 150 votes (Al Jazeera, PISM).

On 2023-05-07, Heger announced his resignation (Wikipedia "Eduard Heger").

In the 2023 snap election on 2023-09-30, Smer of Fico returned to first place (Wikipedia article title "2023 Slovak parliamentary election"; vote share figures not cross-checked against original sources this time).

Observation: The anti-corruption coalition split over 'the personal style of the leader and pandemic policy,' followed by the return of previous governing forces.

Bangladesh Transitional Government (2024-08–2026-02).

2024-08-05 Hasina fled, Yunus transitional government (C_genz_cases).

On 2025-02-25, student leader Nahid Islam resigned from her post and joined the new party NCP (Wikipedia "Yunus interim government").

2025-05 Yunus hinted at resignation; BNP demanded December general elections; NCP and Jamat-e-Islami demanded implementation of the 'July Charter' before elections (The Peninsula 2025-05-22, Financial Express, search summary).

2025-10-17 Signing of the July Charter (BNP Parkul speech, UNB).

2025-12 Two student-origin advisors resigned; the Home Affairs Special Envoy resigned due to deteriorating security (Wiki).

2026-02-12 General election: BNP 209 seats · 49.97%, NCP 6 seats · 3.05% (Electoral Upset Table (Data G)).

Observation: The 'overthrown forces' (students) and the 'forces that took over' (established opposition) were different, and the axis of conflict was 'election timing vs. reform precedence'.

Nepal (2025-09~2026).

2025-09-09 Oli resigned; Karkee served as acting prime minister from 9-12; general election on 2026-03-05, RSP won 182/275 seats; Balenci Shah became prime minister on 2026-03-27 (G Document §1).

This is not a case of 'internal strife within a coalition' because it was a single-party majority rather than a grand coalition.

5.2 Cases Where the Coalition Was (Partially) Maintained

Croatia: First Račan Cabinet (2000–2003).

2000-01-27 Six-party coalition (SDP·HSLS·HNS·HSS·IDS·LS) launched, confidence 122/151 (Wiki "Cabinet of Ivica Račan I").

2001-06-21 IDS withdrew over the Istria County Charter dispute.

2002-03 Replacement of HSLS leadership led to replacement of deputy prime minister; cabinet reshuffle on 2002-07-30 (Račan II).

Most ministers' terms continued until 2003-12-23.

Observation: The government completed its term even though some key partners withdrew.

In this survey, a case where the grand coalition remained intact without fissures could not be confirmed (§6).

What was wanted and what was rejected (as confirmed in the data)

Wanted:

Rejected:

  • Participation of party politicians and disguised justification (candidate eligibility rules).

  • Violent regime change (the student side stated it would govern within legal procedures).

Not explicitly stated or deferred:

  • EU accession timeline (excluded from the 2025 Charter, the 2026 plan focuses on domestic reforms).
  • Sanctions against Russia (opposed).
