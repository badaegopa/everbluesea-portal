§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1991
Independence recovered — Recovered independence during the dissolution of the Soviet Union. Some Russian-speaking residents who did not obtain citizenship remained stateless ('grey passports') (general fact).
2022
Inflation at 19.4% — Statistics Estonia: consumer prices rose by an annual average of 19.4% (17.6% year-on-year in December); two-fifths of the increase was related to housing (Statistics Estonia). The auxiliary BSLI fell from 0.4208 in 2021 to 0.3448 in 2022.
2023~2024
Economic contraction — Approximately −3% in 2023 (one source), −0.3% in 2024 (Statistics Estonia figure). The European Commission described 2024 as a 'second consecutive year of contraction.' Figures for 2022 differ across sources, at −0.5% and −1.3% (EC · wiiw).
2024~2025
Taxation strengthened — Following the 2024 VAT increase, the 2025 budget proposal included a 24% VAT surcharge, a 22% income tax rate, and a new vehicle tax (EU Council documents · accio).
2025-03-26
Constitutional amendment on voting rights — Parliament adopted a constitutional amendment stripping third-country nationals of the right to vote in local elections by 93 votes for, 7 against, and 1 abstention (threshold: 68 votes). The government bill originally applied only to Russian and Belarusian citizens but was amended to cover all third-country nationals. Stateless persons are subject to it from 2026 onward (ERR · ConstitutionNet · IDEA).
2025-10-19
Local Elections — Local elections under the first application of the constitutional amendment (ERR).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Economy. Inflation in 2024 fell to 3.5%, with 1.6 percentage points of this attributed to the effect of the VAT increase. The 2025 HICP inflation forecast is 3.6% (EU Council document).

Constitutional amendment on voting rights. The government cited security, and 7 central party legislators opposed. In the 2021 local elections, voter turnout among both target groups was approximately 40% (IES Lublin).

Public Trends. ACLED protests numbered 141 in 2020, 114 in 2021, and 74 in 2022, followed by 26 each in 2023 and 2024. Political violence is virtually absent.
