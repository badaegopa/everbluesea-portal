Executive Summary

With a Series η of 0.3992, Eritrea ranks lowest in East Africa. The five dimensions within the 2024 critical band are A1, A2, A6, A7, and A8, corresponding to the simultaneous inclusion of A7 and A8 (the common configuration of the 4 uprising-observed countries in KSDS v10.0, n=4 exploratory). On the political axis, this country has been ruled by a single person, President Isaias Afwerki, since its independence in 1993, and the People's Front for Democracy and Justice (PFDJ) is the sole legal political party. Independent media has been banned since 2001, and national service, statutory for 18 months, is operated virtually indefinitely (HRW). In December 2025, it officially withdrew from the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD), and in October 2026, it severed diplomatic ties after Ethiopia closed its embassy in Asmara.

The gap between published figures and independent sources opens at the most fundamental numbers. Population estimates diverge nearly twofold from 3.6 million (UN) to 6.7 million (COMESA), and participation in the Tigray War was denied for several months before being acknowledged only in April 2021 via a UN letter.

Historical Time Series — Events That Built the Structure

1961–1991
War of Independence — A 30-year armed struggle for independence from Ethiopia. The Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) entered Asmara in 1991. The identity of the current ruling regime was formed in this war.
1993
Independence — Achieved independence through a UN-monitored referendum. EPLF leader Isaias Afwerki became head of state, and no elections have been held since (general fact).
1998–2000
Border War — Border war with Ethiopia resulted in heavy casualties in both countries. The indefinite extension of national service began during this period (HRW: extended indefinitely since 1998).
2001-09
G-15 Crackdown — High-ranking figures demanding reform were arrested, and independent media was banned. The whereabouts of 10 independent journalists arrested in September 2001 remain unknown today (HRW 2026).
2009 · 2018
Sanctions and Lifting — Sanctioned by the UN in 2009, sanctions were lifted after normalization of relations with Ethiopia in 2018 (Series briefing).
2020-11 · 2021-04
Participation in Tigray War — Eritrean forces participated in the Ethiopian Tigray War. Both governments denied it for several months, until the Ethiopian Prime Minister acknowledged it before parliament in March 2021, and the Eritrean UN ambassador first explicitly acknowledged it via a Security Council letter in April (AP · Al Jazeera).
2025
Rejection of Monitoring and Departure — In June, submitted a resolution to the UN Human Rights Council to terminate the mandate of the Special Rapporteur on Eritrea, but in July the Council rejected it and extended the mandate (HRW). In December, officially withdrew from IGAD.

Severance of Ties with Ethiopia — In February, Ethiopia deployed troops near the border, and in September, as fighting between the TPLF and federal forces resumed in Tigray, Ethiopia alleged Eritrean support for the TPLF. On October 1, after Ethiopia closed its embassy in Asmara and expelled 10 diplomats, Eritrea announced the severance of all diplomatic relations on October 2 (Series Briefing, AFP).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Confrontation with Ethiopia. According to the Country Analysis Series briefing (as of 2026-10-07), in February 2026 Ethiopian troops were deployed near the Eritrean border toward Tigray, and the Ethiopian government condemned Eritrea's belligerence and support for armed groups (Chatham House 2026-05). Fighting resumed between the TPLF and federal forces in Tigray in September, and on October 1–2 both countries responded with embassy closures, diplomat expulsions, and severed diplomatic relations (AFP, The EastAfrican). Eritrea has characterized Ethiopia's claims to Red Sea access as a 'continuous hostile agenda.'

Domestic. The 2026 World Report of Human Rights Watch noted that in 2025 as well, indefinite military and national service continued, students were conscripted for military training before completing high school, and arbitrary detention and enforced disappearances persisted. The Eritrean military still occupies parts of Tigray, where sexual violence, disappearances, and livestock theft have been reported (HRW). Because independent media, civil society, and opposition activity are not permitted, there are no reports of domestic collective action (Series briefing).

Asylum. HRW noted that as of late 2024, more than 660,000 Eritreans were abroad — approximately 18% of the estimated population of 3.8 million. According to UNHCR, registered refugees and asylum seekers in 2024 exceeded 683,000, with large numbers in Ethiopia (approx. 180,000) and Sudan (approx. 150,000). Egypt and Ethiopia detained and deported Eritreans, and Ethiopia suspended asylum registration after 2021.

International Monitoring. Eritrea is reported as one of the few countries that have never accepted a visit by independent UN human rights experts (HRW). According to ACLED data, political violence incidents range from 0 to 6 per year, and protests have been 0 since 2019 — the absence of records reflects the absence of reporting channels, not the absence of events.
