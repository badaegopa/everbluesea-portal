§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1847
Independence — Descendants of freed slaves who migrated from the United States (Americo-Liberians) established the republic. For the subsequent 133 years, the hierarchy between the settler-descendant elite and the indigenous majority became the foundational baseline of the state structure.
1980
Doe Coup — Master Sergeant Samuel Doe assassinated President Tolbert and seized power. It was the first rule by an indigenous leader, but it led to governance through ethnic mobilization.
1989–2003
Two Civil Wars — Civil wars in 1989–1997 and 1999–2003 resulted in massive casualties and turned a significant portion of the population into refugees. They ended with the 2003 Accra Comprehensive Peace Agreement, followed by the deployment of the UN Mission in Liberia (UNMIL).
2006
Sirleaf Administration — Ellen Johnson Sirleaf took office as Africa's first elected female president. In 2009, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (TRC) recommended the establishment of a war crimes court, but implementation stalled for a long time.
2014–2015
Ebola — The West African Ebola epidemic collapsed the healthcare system. This was a period when the vulnerability of healthcare infrastructure was exposed outside the measured dimensions.
2018
Peaceful Transition and UNMIL Withdrawal — George Weah took office and the UN peacekeeping mission concluded.
2024-01
Inauguration of Boakai — In the 2023 runoff election, Joseph Boakai narrowly defeated incumbent Weah, and Weah conceded. This marked the third peaceful transfer of power since the civil war.
2024-12
Capitol Building Fire — Amid a legislative deadlock over an attempt to unseat House Speaker Koffa, the Joint Chambers of the Capitol caught fire on December 18. The previous day, police fired tear gas at protesters demanding respect for the rule of law (Al Jazeera). The police subsequently concluded it was deliberate and politically motivated arson, and Koffa resigned in May 2025.
2026-05
War and Economic Crimes Court Bill — President Boakai extended the mandate of the preparation office until April 2027 under Executive Order 164 and submitted bills to the House of Representatives to establish the War and Economic Crimes Court and the National Anti-Corruption Court (allAfrica 2026-05).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Residue of Legislative Deadlock. The deadlock triggered by the 2024 attempt to unseat the House Speaker was settled after the Supreme Court reprimanded the majority bloc's 'meetings without the Speaker' (2025-03) and Speaker Koffa resigned (2025-05), citing the executive branch's noncompliance with the Supreme Court ruling. The police concluded the Capitol fire was deliberate and politically motivated arson, but as of June 2026, criminal proceedings against defendants including Koffa remain stalled at the Supreme Court level, and the court granted them permission to travel abroad for medical treatment (allAfrica 2026-06). The arson conclusion relies on single-outlet reporting, and the official report could not be verified.

War Crimes Court and Budget. In May 2026, the President maintained the annual $2 million allocation for the preparation office but transferred it under the Ministry of Justice and tightened oversight. The government stated that fund disbursement was withheld due to failure to submit an expenditure plan, a claim the office disputed (allAfrica). Just Security pointed out that the scope and structure of the court remain uncertain as long as the overall budget is not finalized. JusticeInfo reported that the initial funding strategy relied on US aid, and the Swedish Embassy, one of the largest donors in peace and security, is set to withdraw in October 2026.

Aid Reduction. Following the 2025 US aid freeze, stop-work orders were issued to major implementing agencies running water, sanitation, health, vaccination, and education programs (FrontPage Africa 2025). Reports also indicate that after a 90% cut in anti-human trafficking funds, the country was downgraded to the Tier 2 Watch List in the US Trafficking in Persons Report. This aid-dependent structure aligns with the rock-bottom status of A4 Economic Stability (measured: GDP and manufacturing 0.0271).

Public Trends. According to ACLED records, protests in Liberia remain low at 25–70 incidents per year, with political violence fatalities at 2–11 deaths annually (2017–2024, compiled from vault acled_link). The fact that violence metrics remain low despite six dimensions being within the band demonstrates that social pressure is manifesting in elite-level confrontations—such as legislative deadlocks and arson—rather than street violence.

Collapse Path Classification — WD / SN / BH

Path Classification Criteria (UI Canonical, Stellar Evolution Metaphor)
WD: E_A2 < 0.3, C_internal < 0.4 · SN: E_A2 0.3~0.5, C_internal > 0.5 · BH: E_A2 < 0.2, C_internal > 0.7
White Dwarf (WD) = Suppression and latency · Supernova (SN) = Collapse and regeneration · Black Hole (BH) = Implosion and irreversibility
The decisive variable distinguishing SN from BH is C_internal (elite internal conflict energy). This is a metaphor and does not assert an astrophysical correspondence.

E_A2 (institutional trust effect) falls under the lower-end condition as both A2 and A8 are inside the band, and C_internal (elite internal conflict) was actually observed during the 2024–2025 legislative deadlock. However, that conflict was absorbed by Supreme Court and parliamentary procedures along with the resignation of the Speaker, without escalating into armed conflict or regime collapse.

Therefore, current observations resemble neither WD (suppression and latency) nor SN (collapse and regeneration), but rather 'low-intensity elite conflict within a competitive electoral regime' (linking to the category beyond D-3 needing canonical confirmation). The path since the end of the civil war in 2003 can be read as a regeneration phase of a society that has already undergone one SN (collapse and regeneration). This is an enumeration of observations, not an adjudication.
