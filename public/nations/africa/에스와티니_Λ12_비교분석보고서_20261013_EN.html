§ 02

Historical Timeline — Events That Shaped the Structure

1973
Ban on Political Parties — Political party activities banned by King Sobhuza II's decree. The 2005 Constitution also bars political parties from participating in general elections (HRW·Wikipedia).
2021-05~07
Pro-Democracy Protests and Crackdown — Student and teacher protests erupted over allegations that law student Thabani Nkomonye was killed by police in May. Following a protest by about 500 youth in Manzini on June 20, a blanket ban on protests and live-ammunition crackdowns ensued. Fatalities: HRW reported at least 46, other estimates at least 37. In July, two opposition MPs were arrested on terrorism charges and convicted in June 2023 (HRW).
2023-01-21
Assassination of Maseko — Human rights lawyer and opposition coalition figure Thulani Maseko was shot dead at his home. IDEA recorded it as 'suspected state assassination,' while the government denied involvement. SADC called for an expeditious and transparent investigation (HRW·IDEA·Al Jazeera).
2023-09-29
General Election — MPs elected as individual candidates without political parties across 59 constituencies (Tinkhundla) (51 men, 8 women). Registered voters: 583,177. Government called it a 'free and fair election'; SADC and AU observer missions noted a 'peaceful, calm, and well-organized election.' Legislation requires royal assent (Africanews·News24).
§ 03

Recent Political Situation (2025–2026)

Absolute monarchy. King Mswati III holds legislative assent and veto power, and parliament is noted to function primarily in an advisory capacity (Africanews·News24).

Judicial aftermath. Two opposition MPs related to the 2021 protests were convicted in 2023 on murder and terrorism charges. HRW noted that there has been no accountability for the 2021 violence (HRW 2023-06-26).

Public trends. ACLED protests dropped from 129 in 2021 and 86 in 2022 to 22 in 2024. Violence targeting civilians decreased from 70 in 2022 to 16 in 2024.
