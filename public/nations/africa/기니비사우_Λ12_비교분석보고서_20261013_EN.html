§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

2022-02-01
Coup Attempt — Armed forces attacked the government palace during a cabinet meeting. Government announced at least 11 deaths (civilians, military, paramilitaries, police; breakdown varies across reports). The government characterized it as a coup aimed at assassinating the president, after which 20 individuals including a former prime minister and military commanders were placed on trial lists (The Citizen·Anadolu·Solar Daily).
2022-05
First Dissolution of Parliament — Dissolved by the president citing 'unresolvable differences' (press synthesis).
2023-11-30~12-04
Second Dissolution of Parliament — Following a shootout between the Presidential Guard battalion and the National Guard, the president issued a dissolution decree citing an 'attempted coup.' Parliamentary leadership rejected it, citing a constitutional provision barring dissolution within 12 months after an election. Subsequently, parliament remained vacant until 2025 (AllAfrica·LBC).
2025-11-23~27
Elections and Military Seizure of Power — Following the presidential and legislative elections on November 23, both President Embaló and opposition candidate Fernando Dias claimed victory. On the 26th, one day prior to the scheduled results announcement, the military seized the electoral commission and declared a 'Supreme Military Command' regime; on the 27th, General Horta N'Tam was inaugurated as acting president for a 1-year transitional term. Embaló was detained and subsequently transferred to Senegal via ECOWAS mediation. Detentions of opposition figures including Dias and the former prime minister were reported (citing IDEA·The Star·BBC).
§ 03

Recent Political Situation (2025–2026)

Conflicting Claims. Civil society groups and Dias claimed that Embaló staged a 'sham coup' with the military to prevent unfavorable results, which Embaló denied. Reports have not confirmed this claim. The African Union condemned the situation and the ECOWAS observer mission expressed deep concern (IDEA). This report does not judge either claim.

Transitional Regime. Subsequent transitional charters and election timelines were not confirmed in this investigation.

Public Trends. ACLED protests were recorded as 9 in 2018, 7 in 2020, 14 in 2021, 18 in 2022, 17 in 2023, and 17 in 2024.
