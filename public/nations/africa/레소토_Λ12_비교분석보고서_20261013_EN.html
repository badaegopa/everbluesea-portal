§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Built the Structure

2018~2019
National Dialogue — National reform dialogue addressing prime ministerial powers, judicial independence, politicization of security agencies, and parliamentary weakness. The outcome culminated in the Omnibus Constitutional Amendment (11th Amendment) (The Conversation·ConstitutionNet).
2022-07~08
Collapse of the Reform Bill — When passage within the parliamentary term became difficult, Prime Minister Majoro reconvened parliament under a state of emergency, but the courts blocked it. The country proceeded to general elections without reform legislation (The Conversation).
2022-10-07
General Election — Matekane's RFP, founded in March, became the largest party with 56 seats out of 120 (57 seats according to some sources), falling short of a majority (61 seats) and forming a three-party coalition. The previously governing ABC plunged from 48 seats to 8 seats. Turnout was 37.8% (around 47% in 2017). International and domestic observer missions described it as a 'credible and peaceful election', while expressing concerns over voter roll accuracy and the lack of campaign finance regulations (Africanews·The Conversation·ACCORD).
2023-05
Bifurcation of the Reform Bill — The government announced that the omnibus constitutional amendment would be split into three bills subject to simple majority, two-thirds majority, and referendum (ConstitutionNet).
2025-04
50% Reciprocal Tariff Announcement — Lesotho was subjected to the highest rate of 50% in the reciprocal tariffs announced by the United States. It was subsequently suspended, with a 10% baseline tariff applied instead (BusinessDay·EWN).
2025-07
National State of Disaster — A national state of disaster was declared under the Disaster Management Act through June 30, 2027, to address unemployment. The government warned of the potential loss of up to 40,000 textile jobs if AGOA is not renewed (EWN·Africanews).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Textiles and Employment. The textile industry is the largest private employer, employing approximately 40,000 people and accounting for about 90% of manufacturing exports (citing Oxford Economics). The vast majority go to the US market and have relied on AGOA duty-free benefits (BusinessDay·EWN).

Unemployment. Official unemployment was reported at 30%, with youth unemployment near 50%, while the Minister of Finance stated youth unemployment at 38% in February 2025 (EWN).

Public Trends. ACLED recorded 17 protest events in 2021 and 23 in 2022, followed by 7 each in 2023 and 2024.
