§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

2005
Faure Gnassingbé's Rise to Power — Assumed power after his father's death (general fact). 2017–2018 protests demanding constitutional reform (ACLED recorded 50 protests in 2017).
2022-05~06
First Armed Attack in the North — Government announcement: Northern attack left 8 soldiers dead and 13 wounded; 'security state of emergency' declared in Savanes Region (Le360, Long War Journal).
2024-04~05
Constitutional Amendment to a Parliamentary System — Parliament adopted a new constitution, establishing the Fifth Republic upon taking effect in May. The presidency became a ceremonial post, concentrating authority in the 'President of the Council of Ministers' (Wikipedia, ConstitutionNet).
2024-07
Attacks in the North — Attack on a military outpost in Savanes killed at least 12 soldiers (reported by private intelligence outlet LSI Africa, no official government confirmation) (Long War Journal).
2025-05-03
Inauguration as President of the Council of Ministers — Parliament elected Gnassingbé, leader of the majority party, as President of the Council of Ministers without term limits (unlimited 6-year terms). The opposition criticized it as a 'constitutional coup' (TimesLive, ConstitutionNet).
2025-06-26~28
Protests and Deaths — Security forces dispersed protests in Lomé with tear gas and batons. Civil society: 7 bodies found in the river. Government: Forensic findings showed 'drowning', unrelated to protests. Amnesty: Testimonies from 18 victims/witnesses, allegations of torture and 3 missing (Africanews, Amnesty, Anadolu).
§ 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

Power structure. Under the 2024 constitutional amendment, authority was concentrated in the President of the Council of Ministers elected by parliament, and Jean-Lucien Savi de Tové became president (Wikipedia).

Northern conflict. Activities of Sahelian armed groups expanded into the north. Secondary tallies also indicate over 60 deaths (including 54 civilians) across 15 incidents between January and July 2025 (UCDP, secondary tallies).

Public trends. ACLED recorded 3–44 protests annually following 50 in 2017. Political violence increased to 62 incidents in 2024.
