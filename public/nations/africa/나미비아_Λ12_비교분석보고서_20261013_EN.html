§ 02

Historical Timeline — Events That Shaped the Structure

1904–1908
Herero and Nama Genocide — German colonial forces massacred the uprising Herero and Nama peoples. It is referred to as the first genocide of the 20th century. The legacy of land dispossession continues into today's land and income inequality (general fact).
1915–1990
South African Rule — After World War I, the Union of South Africa administered it under a mandate, and the apartheid regime was instituted. SWAPO's liberation struggle (1966–1989) followed.
1990
Independence — Achieved independence following UN-supervised elections. SWAPO subsequently won every election.
2021-05
German Joint Declaration — Germany recognized it as 'genocide from today's perspective' and agreed to provide €1.1 billion over 30 years as a 'gesture of reconciliation' (in the form of development assistance). Affected community organizations rejected it, noting the absence of reparations, an official apology, and direct participation.
2024-11
First Female President — SWAPO's Netumbo Nandi-Ndaitwah garnered approximately 57%, and IPC's Panduleni Itula won 25.5%. Voting was extended due to ballot shortages, scanner malfunctions, and long queues. In the National Assembly, SWAPO won 51 out of 96 seats, and IPC 20 seats.
2025-01 · 02
Unemployment Statistics and Election Lawsuits — In January, the Statistics Agency announced the 2023 unemployment rate of 36.9% (33.4% in 2018), sparking controversy over definition changes. On February 28, the Supreme Court dismissed IPC's petition to invalidate the presidential election, and Itula accepted the ruling (IPC maintained its stance of non-recognition of the results).
2025–2026
Renegotiation of Reparations — Parliament deemed the €1.1 billion insufficient, and the president stated that 'we must renegotiate with Germany,' expressing hope for a final agreement before the end of 2026. A signed revised agreement has not been confirmed.
§ 03

Recent Political Situation (2025–2026)

Post-Election. In the 2024 election, voting was extended in some areas due to ballot shortages, scanner failures, and long queues; IPC claimed the extension was illegal and held the Electoral Commission of Namibia (ECN) responsible. On February 28, 2025, the Supreme Court dismissed the application to nullify the presidential election and ordered both parties to share the litigation costs (EWN). The conclusion of the litigation regarding the National Assembly results could not be confirmed.

Unemployment. The Statistics Agency's 2023 Labour Force Report (released in January 2025) announced a 36.9% rate, stating that 320,442 out of 867,247 in the labour force were unemployed. Youth (15–34) unemployment ranges between 44.1% and 44.4% depending on reports. By region, Kavango West saw the steepest rise, from 33% in 2018 to 52.8% in 2023. The 2023 definition excluded discouraged jobseekers from unemployment (pointed out by a Cirrus Capital economist).

Genocide Reparations. Affected community organizations, such as the Ovaherero Genocide Foundation, rejected the 2021 Joint Declaration in both procedure and substance, demanding Germany's admission of guilt, reparations, an official apology, and direct victim participation. The Okandjoze Chiefs' Assembly demanded a national conference with the participation of victim descendants. The president stated that one must be cautious in drawing comparisons with reparations for Jewish victims.

Public Trends. According to ACLED records, demonstrations in Namibia peaked at 118 in 2021, and political violence fatalities remained at 0–6 per year (2017–2024). Pressure manifests through demonstrations, elections, and litigation rather than violence.
