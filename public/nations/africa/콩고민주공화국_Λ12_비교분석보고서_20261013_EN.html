§ 02

Historical Timeline — Events That Shaped the Structure

1960–1961
Independence and Lumumba — Independence was gained from Belgium, and the first Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba was assassinated in 1961. The independence of a resource-rich nation began amidst the Cold War and foreign intervention (general facts).
1965–1997
Mobutu's 32 Years — Renamed the country Zaire and established a regime of personal rule and rent plundering. It is studied as a prime example of 'kleptocracy'.
1996–2003
Two Congo Wars — Wars involving multiple countries including Rwanda, Uganda, Angola, and Zimbabwe resulted in massive casualties (excess mortality estimates vary widely across studies). The issue of armed groups in the east is a remnant of these wars.
2019-01
First Peaceful Transition — But Contested Numbers — The Electoral Commission (CENI) announced Tshisekedi at 38.57% and Fayulu at 34.8%. Leaked CENI electronic data (analyzed by the FT and the Congo Research Group) showed Fayulu at 59.42%, and the leaked tally from the Catholic Bishops' Conference (CENCO) was 62.80%. The Constitutional Court dismissed Fayulu's appeal citing lack of evidence.
2023-12
Re-election — CENI announced Tshisekedi at 73.34%. The joint Catholic-Protestant observer mission (CENCO-ECC) recorded 'numerous irregularities capable of impacting the integrity of the results,' stating that over 60% of 5,402 reported polling station incidents disrupted voting. The government rejected demands for a rerun.
2025-01 · 02
Fall of Goma and Bukavu — M23 seized Goma (January) and Bukavu (February). A WHO-led assessment showed deaths around Goma rising from 700 (Jan 26–30) → approx. 900 bodies recovered → approx. 2,900, while the government spokesperson announced on February 18 'over 5,000 buried'.
2025-06 · 11 · 12
Peace Process — June 27 Washington Peace Agreement (Congo–Rwanda), November 15 Doha Framework (Government–M23/AFC), December 4 'Washington Agreement for Peace and Prosperity' (including U.S.–Congo cooperation on critical minerals). In the same December, Uvira in South Kivu fell (Series briefing).
2026-06 · 09
Referendum Act and Third-Term Debate — In June, the Organic Law on Referendums passed Parliament, and a local human rights group reported recovering 2 bodies from clashes between opposing rallies (government figures unannounced). In July, the Constitutional Court granted conditional approval, and on September 15, the opposition coalition C64 staged a march of several hundred people in Kinshasa against a 'third term' (Series briefing).
§ 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

Eastern peace process. According to the Country Analysis Series briefing (as of 2026-10-07), the Congolese government is pursuing 'negotiated disarmament' of some factions of the Rwandan rebel FDLR within the framework of implementing the Washington Agreement (a faction accepted the process on 2026-07-28), while Rwanda rejects this and demands 'neutralization.' The U.S. Department of the Treasury sanctioned Rwandan military leadership on March 2, 2026, and the State Department announced visa restrictions. A Joint Security Coordination Mechanism (JSCM) meeting was held in Geneva on September 16, but its outcome could not be confirmed. In the eastern conflict zone, a parallel administration operated by M23 functions, beyond the reach of government statistics.

Displacement. The IOM reported 6.9 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) in early 2025, calling it an all-time high, while an OCHA-linked report (ACAPS) recorded over 7.3 million in April 2025. The Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre (IDMC) 2026 report recorded 9.7 million internal displacements (on a flow basis) due to conflict and violence during the single year of 2025 — cumulative figures (stocks) and annual occurrences (flows) cannot be directly compared.

Constitutional amendment debate. President Tshisekedi's second term ends in 2028. The passage of the referendum bill in 2026 is read as opening the process to amend the presidential term clauses (Articles 70 and 220), and the opposition coalition C64 followed up its 'ghost city' protests in June with a march in September. Protests were banned in some areas, including Lubumbashi (Series briefing). In July, Cardinal Ambongo conveyed the president's willingness to hold a national dialogue.

Scale of conflict. According to ACLED data, political violence fatalities rose from 3,211 in 2017 to 8,074 in 2022, with 5,505 and 5,508 in 2023 and 2024, respectively. Demonstrations peaked at 671 events in 2020.
