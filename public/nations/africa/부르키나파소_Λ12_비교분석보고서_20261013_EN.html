§ 00

Position within the Region — Why Burkina Faso

This report was selected as the country with the lowest η based on the Country Analysis Series among countries without a standalone edition in the West African cycle of the daily regional rotation pipeline. In the Series, Burkina Faso's η is 0.4336(177th out of 197 countries), nearly identical to fellow Alliance of Sahel States (AES) member Mali (0.4344) and lower than Niger (0.4657). The top-ranking country in the same cycle is Liberia (0.5582).

Its position as the regional lowest must be read in contrast to Liberia. Both countries have six dimensions in the band for 2024 and qualify for concurrent inclusion of A7·A8. A significant portion of the difference in η (0.5582 vs 0.4336) arises not from the pressure configuration, but from the missing data configuration — in Liberia, A5·A10, where bottom values are common, are missing and thus excluded from the average, whereas in Burkina Faso, A5 (0.0156) and A10 (0.0005) enter the average (a phenomenon similar to the 'Canonical Verification Required' T-2 missing data bias). The difference for Burkina Faso lies not in the number of pressures, but in the fact that these pressures materialize as actual armed conflict — with 8,499 conflict deaths recorded by ACLED in 2023.

§ 01

Executive Summary

Burkina Faso ranks lowest in West Africa with a Series η of 0.4336. The dimensions inside the 2024 critical band are six—A1, A2, A5, A6, A7, and A8 (based on time series)—and the cross-sectional maximum pressure is A1 · P=0.632 (measured: electricity and internet). The BSLI is -0.9458 (Grade E · 127th out of 139 countries), showing that the grassroots base lies deep below the basic living line. The missing dimension is A11 and has not been imputed.

On the political axis, this country has been under the military junta of Captain Traoré following two coups in 2022. It departed from the multiparty system through a five-year extension of the junta in 2024, withdrawals from ECOWAS and the ICC alongside the abolition of the independent electoral commission in 2025, and the dissolution of all political parties in January 2026. Simultaneously, conflict with armed groups such as JNIM has continued with over 2,000 deaths annually since 2019.

The gap between officially announced figures and independent sources is greatest in 'not releasing figures.' The military government does not announce the number of fallen soldiers, official statistics on internally displaced persons have frozen at the March 2023 figure (2,062,534 people), and it has not disclosed the death toll of the 2024 Barsalogho massacre.

§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events that Shaped the Structure

1960
Independence — Gained independence from France as Upper Volta. Thereafter, a political history marked by recurring military coups began.
1983–1987
Sankara Revolution — Thomas Sankara took power and changed the country's name to Burkina Faso ('Land of Incorruptible People'). He was assassinated in a 1987 coup, and that memory is rewritten into the legitimation narrative of the current junta.
2014-10
Popular Uprising — When Compaoré, who had ruled for 27 years, attempted to extend his rule via constitutional amendment, he was ousted by massive protests. A 2015 coup attempt by the presidential guard failed.
2016-01
Ouagadougou Terrorist Attacks — Following attacks on hotels and cafes in the capital, armed group activities spread to the north and east. ACLED conflict fatalities surged from 303 in 2018 to 2,220 in 2019.
2022-01 · 09
Two Coups — In January, Lieutenant Colonel Damiba ousted President Kaboré, and in September, Captain Traoré ousted Damiba. ACLED recorded 4,234 conflict fatalities in 2022.
2024-05 · 08
Junta Extension and Barsalogho — In May, national consultations boycotted by the opposition extended the junta until 2029. In August, civilians and militiamen digging trenches in Barsalogho were massacred in a JNIM attack. The government did not disclose the death toll.
2025
Year of Withdrawals — Official withdrawal from ECOWAS with Mali and Niger in January; JNIM attack on the Djibo base in May (approximately 100 reported dead); abolition of the independent electoral commission in July; joint declaration of ICC withdrawal in September; launch of the AES joint security force in December (Series Briefing).
2026-01 · 02
Party Dissolution and Offensives — Following the government's announcement of foiling a coup plot in January, the Council of Ministers dissolved all political parties and confiscated their assets to the state. In February, analyses emerged indicating over 130 people died in simultaneous attacks by JNIM (Series Briefing).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Extinction of Political Space. According to the Country Analysis Series Briefing (as of 2026-10-07), the junta abolished the independent electoral commission in July 2025, transferring election administration to the Ministry of Territorial Administration, and dissolved all political parties on January 29, 2026, citing 'division and abuse.' HRW reported the forced conscription and detention of critics, judges, and journalists under emergency laws, and in March 2025, three journalists including the president of the journalists' association were arrested and the association was dissolved. Within the verified scope (reports from 2025-01 to 2026-07), no reports of large-scale anti-government protests were gathered.

Conflict. On July 4, 2026, JNIM carried out a coordinated attack near Dédougou, killing at least 22 people (AFP, reprinted by The Defense Post). Analyses indicate that the government-controlled territory stands at about one-third of the country (World Politics Review 2025-06), and because the government does not disclose casualties, conflict statistics rely on ACLED and media tallies.

Economy — Numbers Propped Up by Gold. In the fourth review of the Extended Credit Facility (2026-02-19), the IMF estimated real growth for 2025 at 5.0% (4.8% in 2024). The drivers of growth were the supply response of artisanal mining driven by rising gold prices and mining reforms, with gold exports increasing by 43.2% in 2025 (IMF and press reports). The fiscal deficit remained within the program target (4.0% of GDP). The same IMF document cites security concerns, climate shocks, and infrastructure deficits as downside risks to growth. Gold production of an 'all-time high of 94 tons' could not be officially verified through secondary reporting.

Foreign Policy Line. In December 2025, the AES joint Sahel security force was launched, and Traoré assumed the rotating chairmanship of the AES (Series Briefing). The policy course of distancing from Western-led institutions culminated in the declaration of withdrawal from the ICC (2025-09).

The decisive variable distinguishing SN from BH is C_internal (elite internal conflict energy). This is a metaphor and does not claim an astrophysical correspondence.

E_A2 (institutional trust effect) falls into the very low condition because A2 and A8 are both within the band and the military junta dismantled institutional checks. Observations are accumulating in the direction of the BH (implosion) condition variables (E_A2 < 0.2, C_internal > 0.7), but quantitative values have not been calculated.
