Historical Time Series — Events That Built the Structure

1960
Independence — Gained independence from France. The export economy of oil, manganese, and timber, along with close ties with France (Françafrique), forms the baseline of the state structure.
1967–2009
Omar Bongo's 42 Years — Established a one-party system in 1968 and maintained power even after the return to a multi-party system in 1990. Distribution of oil rents was the axis of rule (general fact).
2009
Dynastic Succession — After the death of Omar Bongo, his son Ali Bongo was elected in the presidential election.
2016-08
Haut-Ogooué 99.93% — Ali Bongo was reelected by a margin of about 6,000 votes. The official turnout in Bongo's home province of Haut-Ogooué was 99.93% (reported as 99.83% by Le Monde), with about 95% of votes for Bongo, while national turnout excluding this province was 54.24%. The EU mission pointed out an 'evident anomaly' and questioned the integrity of the results in its final report. Post-election violent deaths diverged between 3 according to the government and up to 100 according to the opposition.
2023-08-30
Coup — Immediately after the announcement of Ali Bongo's third term, Republican Guard Commander Brice Oligui Nguema seized power. The 56-year Bongo rule came to an end.
2024-11
New Constitution — In the referendum, the new constitution passed with 91.64% approval in the Constitutional Court's final tally (91.80% announced by the Ministry of the Interior) and approximately 54% turnout. It included the abolition of the prime minister post, prohibition of dynastic succession, and a single renewable 7-year term (AA·IDEA).
2025-04
Oligui Elected — Elected with 90.35% in provisional presidential election results (87.21% turnout, Ministry of the Interior). Domestic civil society observation groups stated that 94.8% of observed polling stations had satisfactory conditions, while competing candidate Bilie-By-Nze criticized the mobilization of state resources (AP).
2026
Debt and the IMF — In March, an IMF program was officially requested; in June, a public debt audit commenced (approx. 8.7 trillion CFA francs, 70–74% of GDP); in July, the revised budget cut revenue forecasts by 22% and authorized borrowing up to 1.5 billion USD; in September, the IMF staff visit concluded without an agreement. In October, diesel rationing in Libreville was reported.
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Fiscal. The revised budget signed on July 17, 2026, reduced total revenue forecasts by 22% to 3.24 trillion CFA francs and authorized international market borrowing of up to 1.5 billion USD (Reuters, reprinted in Polity). In July, 920 million USD was raised via 7-year bonds at an interest rate of 9.375% per annum. The public debt audit initiated on June 17 estimated debt as of late March 2026 at approximately 8.7 trillion CFA francs (70–74% of GDP), but reports based on IMF figures suggest it could rise to 86.1% of GDP in 2026, leaving the ratio itself uncertain (Financial Afrik, etc.).

IMF. Gabon officially requested an IMF program on March 11, 2026. The September 14–25 IMF staff visit concluded without an agreement, with both parties agreeing to continue technical fiscal discussions including the results of the debt audit. The amended finance law on May 22 lowered the 2026 growth forecast from 6.5% to 4%. The crude oil production plan is 10.8 million tons for 2026 (approximately 200,000 barrels per day).

Livelihood. In October 2026, reports emerged that diesel supplies were being rationed at 10,000 CFA francs per person around Libreville (Africanews). Government officials denied this, stating there were 'no supply issues.' In this investigation, no reports of fuel price protests in Gabon were found.

Public Trends. According to ACLED statistics, protests in Gabon peaked at 64 incidents in 2023, and political violence fatalities remained at 0–6 per year (2017–2024). The coup occurred not through street uprisings, but through decisions by the military leadership.

Collapse Path Classification — WD / SN / BH

Path Classification Criteria (UI Canonical, Stellar Evolution Metaphor)
WD: E_A2 < 0.3, C_internal < 0.4 · SN: E_A2 0.3~0.5, C_internal > 0.5 · BH: E_A2 < 0.2, C_internal > 0.7
White Dwarf (WD) = suppression and latency · Supernova (SN) = collapse and regeneration · Black Hole (BH) = implosion and irreversibility
The decisive variable dividing SN and BH is C_internal (elite internal conflict energy). It is a metaphor and does not claim an astrophysical correspondence.

The 2023 coup was a case where C_internal (elite internal conflict) erupted between the Bongo family and the military, but it ended swiftly without bloodshed and was subsequently legitimized through constitutional and electoral processes. Rather than collapse and regeneration (SN), it is closer to a path where 'only the leadership was replaced within the same rentier state'.

Current observations indicate that E_A2 (institutional-trust effect) is on the lower side within the A2·A8 band, and C_internal shows no public signals under the military-origin presidential regime. Short-term conditions are close to WD (suppression and latency), but the phase in which declining oil rents and the debt crisis deplete fiscal resources for the distributive system remains a subject of observation. This is an enumeration of observations, not an adjudication.
