§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

2018
First Unconstitutionality Ruling — The Supreme Court ruled the original contract for the Cobre Panamá mine unconstitutional, after which it operated under legal uncertainty (reported by Mining.com).
2023-10~11
Protests Against Mine Contract — Nationwide protests following Law 406 (new contract) on October 20. ACLED recorded 744 protests in 2023 (acled_link).
2023-11-28
Unconstitutionality Ruling and Closure — The Supreme Court unanimously ruled the entirety of Law 406 unconstitutional. The mine was subsequently closed. The mine accounted for approximately 300,000 tons of copper concentrate annually, 75% of exports, and about 5% of economic output (aggregated reports).
2024-05
Presidential Election — José Raúl Mulino elected (general fact).
2025-04~07
Strike Against Law 462 — Strike against the reform of the Social Security Fund (CSS) from April 24. Teachers' unions claimed pensions could decrease from 60–70% of salary to about 35%. State of emergency in Bocas del Toro at the end of May (no suspension of fundamental rights); on June 25, a province-wide state of emergency suspended some constitutional rights and blocked internet and mobile phone services. The government cited looting, while the strikers claimed it was a pretext for repression. Official tally of arrests: 140 (Jurist · La Estrella · Panamá América).
2025-05~07
Banana Worker Layoffs — Chiquita terminated day laborers citing 'unjustified absence' and losses exceeding $75 million. On July 18, terminated remaining 1,189 employees (approved by the Ministry of Labor for economic reasons) (Al Jazeera · Bloomberg Línea).
2026
Discussions on Mine Reopening — June release of SGS independent audit results (evaluating 370 environmental compliance commitments for 2019–2023). Late May march opposing reopening. As of October 1, no final decision by the government (Mining.com · Nasdaq · Industrial Info).
§ 03

Recent Political Situation (2025–2026)

Mines. The President referred to the mine as a national asset, stating that reopening would be considered 'within the framework of the law,' and mentioned bypassing congress to avoid mass protests like those in 2023 (Outlook Business report). The Chamber of Commerce and Mines supported reopening on condition of reflecting audit results and ensuring independent oversight (Industrial Info).

Social Security. Law 462 was criticized for shifting public pensions toward individual accounts and retroactively applying changes to some existing subscribers (People's Dispatch · Jurist). Government-side pension figures were not verified in this survey.

Public Trends. ACLED protests were 523 in 2018, 357 in 2020, 493 in 2021, 625 in 2022, 744 in 2023, and 269 in 2024. Political violence ranged from 6 to 19 cases annually.
