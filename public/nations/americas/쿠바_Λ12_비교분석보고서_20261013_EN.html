Cuba's dimensions within the 2024 critical band are A5 and A8. On the geopolitical axis, 2026 was the year of the fuel crisis. On January 3, Cuba lost its primary crude oil supplier due to the U.S. operation in Venezuela (Cuban government announced the deaths of 32 of its operatives), and on January 29, the U.S. signed Executive Order 14380 authorizing tariffs on countries supplying oil to Cuba (revoked following the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling on February 20, while maintaining the declaration of a national emergency). The gap between official figures and independent sources is largest in population. § 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

Power and Daily Life. During the power grid collapse in March 2026, the Deputy Minister of Energy stated that diesel, bunker oil, gasoline, aviation fuel, and LPG had not entered at all for three months (Reuters, reprinted by US News). The March 21 blackout was attributed to a generator shutdown at the Cienfuegos Thermoelectric Plant in Camagüey Province (Al Jazeera). Reports indicated that prolonged blackouts disrupted communications, education, and transportation and delayed thousands of surgeries (Series briefing).

Releases. The government disclosed only that the 51 individuals 'had served a significant portion of their sentences and exhibited good behavior while incarcerated,' without revealing their names. Miami-based human rights organization Cubalex stated that at least 20 political prisoners had been released by late March, all of whom were imprisoned for participating in the July 11–12, 2021 protests (The Defense Post).

Conflicting Explanations of Causes. Cuba's Foreign Minister describes US sanctions as 'collective punishment,' while the US UN Ambassador attributes them to Cuban government policy (Series briefing). This report makes no judgment on either side.

Public Trends. No large-scale street protests were identified in English-language reporting for January–July 2026 (Series briefing). According to ACLED tallies, political violence (mostly targeting civilians) numbered 92 cases in 2021 and 41 in 2024.
