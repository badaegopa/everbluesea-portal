Executive Summary

The Bahamas' compendium η of 0.5481 is the average of eight dimensions: A1 Infrastructure (0.9597), A12 (0.8853), A2 Institutional Trust (0.7205), A8 Governance (0.6666), A7 (0.5837), A6 (0.4833), A4 (0.0701), and A10 (0.0153). In 2024, 0 dimensions were inside the band, and on the trajectory, the dimensions under the greatest pressure are A7 (P 0.4419) and A4 Economic Stability (P 0.4296).

On the political axis, in the snap general election on May 12, 2026, Prime Minister Philip Davis's Progressive Liberal Party (PLP) regained power with 33 of 41 seats. For the first time in 30 years, an incumbent prime minister won consecutive terms (Al Jazeera · AA). A 51.21% vote share translated into 80% of parliamentary seats, with a voter turnout of 66.4% (65% in 2021). The CARICOM Election Observation Mission assessed the election as peaceful and orderly.

The gap in officially announced figures is most evident in disaster statistics. Immediately following Hurricane Dorian in 2019, official fatalities rose from 30 → 43 → 50, and the list of missing persons was revised from approximately 2,500 to about 1,300 after cross-referencing shelter rosters. Final casualty and missing figures could not be confirmed through official sources in this survey.

§ 02

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1973
Independence — Gained independence from the United Kingdom. Retains a Westminster parliamentary system and a first-past-the-post single-member district electoral system (general fact).
2019-09
Hurricane Dorian — Struck Abaco and Grand Bahama. Official fatalities increased from 30 (initial) → 43 (Minister of Health) → 50, and the list of missing persons was revised from about 2,500 to roughly 1,300 following cross-checks with shelter lists (AA · Bernama · SBS). A former prime minister expressed concern that the final death toll could reach hundreds.
2021-09
Change of Government — The PLP won the general election and Prime Minister Davis took office (general fact, 65% turnout is a comparative figure cited in 2026 reports).
2026-05-12
First Consecutive Re-election in 30 Years — In the snap general election (originally scheduled for October, brought forward citing hurricane season), PLP won 33 seats and the Free National Movement (FNM) won 8 seats. Vote shares were PLP 51.21% and FNM 35.28% (Wikipedia infobox), with 139,026 total votes cast and a 66.4% turnout (official). Both of the two newly established constituencies were won by the PLP. Former Prime Minister Hubert Minnis ran as an independent and was defeated in the Killarney constituency he had held for 20 years (Al Jazeera · AA · Nassau Guardian).
§ 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

Elections. Opposition Leader Michael Pintard retained his seat and conceded defeat to Prime Minister Davis by phone (Nassau Guardian). While the FNM avoided total wipeout, it failed to recover from its 2021 defeat, with both the party chairman and deputy leader losing their seats. Speculation that turnout would hit historic lows circulated before ballot counting, but the official figure was slightly higher than in 2021 (Nassau Guardian commentary).

Monitoring. The CARICOM Election Observation Mission stated that the 2026 election was peaceful and orderly (Caribbean National Weekly).

Popular Trends. According to ACLED data, protests in the Bahamas ranged between 3 and 22 incidents per year, with 0 to 4 political violence fatalities annually (2018–2024).
