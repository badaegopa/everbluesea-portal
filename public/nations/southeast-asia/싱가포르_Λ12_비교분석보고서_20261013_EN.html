§ 03

Recent Developments (2025–2026)

Politics. The general election on May 3, 2025, was the first election held after Prime Minister Lawrence Wong took office. Synthesizing reports and results announced by the Elections Department (ELD), the PAP won approximately 65.6% of the vote, rising by over 4 percentage points compared to 2020 (61.2%), securing 87 out of 97 seats (including 5 uncontested GRC seats). The Workers' Party retained 10 constituency seats. Voter turnout was approximately 92%. Although opposition seats have gradually increased since 2011, this does not represent a competitive landscape capable of unseating the ruling regime. The election outcome demonstrates the robust support for the ruling party, alongside the fact that the opposition base remains solid without shrinking.

Economy. The Ministry of Trade and Industry (MTI) calculated the 2025 growth rate at 5.0%. In November 2025, citing a slowdown in global demand caused by US tariff measures, it issued a 2026 forecast of 1.0–3.0%. In February 2026, it raised this to 2.0–4.0%, and in May maintained that range, noting increased downside risks from the Middle East conflict. On August 11, it raised the forecast again to 4.5–5.5%, citing first-half growth of 6.1% and accelerated global AI-related capital expenditure. The same announcement noted that outbound travel by residents increased while visitors declined, contracting the food and beverage services sector. The advance estimate for the third quarter of 2026 is scheduled for release on October 14 and is not reflected in this report.

Connection to the Engine. This phase connects to the external linkage axes of Λ¹². However, the inputs for A4 (economic stability) in Canonical v2.2 are GDP per capita and manufacturing share, with the final empirical measurement from 2024; thus, tariff and AI demand fluctuations in 2025–2026 are not yet reflected in the figures. The input for A10, international tourist arrivals, also uses 2019 empirical measurements in v2.2, meaning the 2026 decline in visitors is not captured in this indicator.

No corresponding country in bsli_timeseries_v12_20260726.json — Singapore lacks income percentile data in the World Bank PIP, making it impossible to compute the four BSLI terms (F_ML, H, D, H_s). The position of the Sun (center) in the pressure coordinate system is marked as 'Absent'.

BSLI is a grassroots base index combining food and livelihood for the 30th–50th income percentiles, basic living adequacy, poverty rate, and housing stress (Palma ratio). As Singapore does not report household income percentile data to the World Bank PIP, it is omitted from both the 139-country canonical sample and the time-series edition. While government statistics (household income reports) contain figures such as the Gini coefficient, differing calculation standards prevented their inclusion in the BSLI canonical formula.

Consequently, regarding Singapore, whether the 'grassroots base is above or below the basic living line' cannot be stated using this engine. This void is the primary reason for withholding a definitive determination.
