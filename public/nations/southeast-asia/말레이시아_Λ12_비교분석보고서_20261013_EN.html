§ 01

Executive Summary

Malaysia has a compendium η of 0.5433 (52nd out of 197 countries). The maximum cross-sectional pressure is A5 · P=0.526 (measured: R&D/GDP), and second place A7 (0.5088) remains inside the band. Positions 3 through 7 are A10 (0.3063), institutional trust A2 (0.3052), A4 (0.3007), A3 (measured: Gini 0.2738), and governance A8 (0.2731), clustered tightly in the 0.27–0.31 range.

Trajectory: A2 departed sharply in 2019 at -0.0415, and A8 departed sharply in 2018 at -0.0422 — corresponding to the period of the first regime change after independence in May 2018. Following that, A2 fell in 2020 at +0.0091 and in 2021 at +0.0156, while A8 fell in 2021 at +0.0151 — corresponding to the period of coalition collapse and regime transition in 2020. In 2024, both A2 (-0.0101) and A8 (-0.0255) departed again.

On the political axis, the Anwar government (2022–) switched to targeted subsidies for RON95 gasoline in September 2025, the National Front (BN) won 48 out of 56 seats in the Johor state election in July 2026, and in September, the BN president announced that cooperation with the Hope Alliance (PH) would end upon dissolution of parliament (compendium briefing). The gap between published figures opens when domestic announcements (RM40 billion–50 billion annually) and overseas analyses (approximately $1.8 billion monthly) speak of different scales for the same subsidy.

Historical Time Series — Events That Shaped the Structure

1957 · 1963
Independence and Federation — Malaya's independence (1957), formation with Sabah and Sarawak as Malaysia (1963) (general fact).
2015–2018
1MDB and the First Replacement — In May 2018, the regime changed for the first time after independence amid the 1MDB scandal involving the state investment fund (general fact). In the compendium trajectory, A8 departed sharply in 2018 at -0.0422, and A2 departed sharply in 2019 at -0.0415.
2020-02
Coalition Collapse — The ruling coalition collapsed due to defections from parliamentary majority without an election, resulting in a regime change (general fact). A2 fell in 2020–2021, and A8 fell in 2021.
2022-11
Anwar Government — Anwar Ibrahim became prime minister after the general election as part of a coalition including PH and BN (compendium briefing).
2025
Sabah Election and Targeted Subsidies — In the Sabah state election, the Democratic Action Party (DAP), affiliated with PH, lost all contested seats. On September 30, targeted subsidies for RON95 gasoline were implemented, providing it at RM1.99 per liter to eligible recipients within monthly limits (compendium briefing).
2026-07-11
Johor State Election — BN won 48 seats (59.66% of votes), PH 8 seats, and PN 0 seats; voter turnout was 69.57%. It was reported as BN's best performance since 2008. Immediately after the count, unofficial BN tallies of 49 seats differed from the Election Commission's tally of 48 seats (Malay Mail).
2026-09-28
Coalition Fractures — BN President Ahmad Zahid Hamidi announced that cooperation with PH would end upon dissolving parliament (The Edge). In April, reports emerged of early general election considerations prior to planned subsidy cuts (Bloomberg, citing RANE).
§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Subsidies. The government stated it spends RM40 billion to RM50 billion annually on RON95 subsidies and maintains a policy where international oil price increases due to Middle East developments are absorbed by the budget (The Vibes). Overseas analyses (cited RANE, Bloomberg) estimated this at approximately $1.8 billion per month (Compendium Briefing).

Coalition Politics. UMNO's push for a pardon of former Prime Minister Najib Razak, who was convicted of corruption, was reported as a burden on its coalition partners who campaigned on anti-corruption. The expiration date of the parliamentary term is described differently: 2027 (analysts) or early 2028 (RANE) (Compendium Briefing).

Public Trends. No large-scale collective action was reported within the scope of subsidy and election coverage in 2025–2026 (Compendium Briefing). ACLED protests peaked at 175 incidents in 2019, followed by 118 in 2024.
