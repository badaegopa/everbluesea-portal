§ 03

Recent Situation (2025–2026)

Politics. The military junta held the general election in three phases on December 28, 2025, January 11, 2026, and January 25, 2026, because conflict areas prevented simultaneous nationwide voting. Major opposition parties, including the NLD, boycotted, while UN experts and human rights organizations criticized the fairness of the election. According to reports, the military-aligned Union Solidarity and Development Party (USDP) won the vast majority of elected seats, alongside the constitutional military quota comprising one-fourth of all seats. On April 3, 2026, parliament elected Min Aung Hlaing president with 429 out of 584 votes. As the constitution bars the president from concurrently serving as Commander-in-Chief, he transferred the Commander-in-Chief position to his close confidant, former intelligence chief Ye Win Oo. Western governments and analysts assessed that while formatted as a transition to civilian rule, the military's power structure remained intact.

Conflict. Following the offensive by the ethnic armed organization alliance in October 2023 (Operation 1027), the military suffered major losses in the northern border areas. In January 2025, a ceasefire in northern Shan State was reached through Chinese mediation, and subsequent reports indicated that control of some captured towns was returned to the military. Various conflicting estimates exist regarding the area under effective military control (such as estimates in the 20–30% range of national territory), and this report does not adopt a specific figure. Because accounts of frontline conditions from 2026 onward diverge across sources, only verified facts are documented.

Economy and Disaster. The World Bank Myanmar Economic Monitor (June 2025) estimated direct asset damages from the March 2025 earthquake at $11 billion (14% of GDP) and projected GDP to contract by 2.5% in fiscal year 2025/26. The same report estimated the pre-earthquake 2024 poverty rate at 31.0%, higher than the last official estimate of 24.8% in 2017. The June 2026 edition noted that despite some recovery, business activity remained far below pre-2021 and pre-earthquake levels, forecasting 2% growth for 2026/27.

Connection with the Engine. This phase directly touches the two absorber axes of Λ¹². The coup was an event where A8 (Executive) attacked A2 (Electoral Institutions), and WGI-based A8 pressure increased from 0.551 in 2016 to 0.647 in 2024. Conversely, because the last measured year for BSLI is 2017, the rise in the poverty rate in 2024 (31.0%) has not yet entered the engine.

Whether this trajectory constitutes BH (implosion and irreversibility) or a prolonged deadlock amid fragmentation cannot be spoken to by this engine without empirical measures of C_internal and territorial control. Judgment belongs to the members of that society, and this report documents only up to the current ongoing trajectory.
