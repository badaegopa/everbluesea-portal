Research Objectives Declaration

This comparison edition simultaneously analyzes the three countries of Malaysia, Indonesia, and Vietnam using the Λ¹² 12-dimensional framework, observing the shared structural conditions and diverging orbits of the three emerging Southeast Asian countries. Although all three countries adopt open trade economies, their political systems, ethnic compositions, and development paths differ. Ⅹ PPR-4 Environmental
🇲🇾 Malaysia
Process
Palm oil agriculture is the primary cause of rainforest destruction. Deforestation in Borneo and Sarawak continues. Declared Carbon Neutrality by 2050.
Phenomenon
EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) — risk of blow to palm oil exports. CO₂ emissions per capita 7.8t (2022). Increasing ESG pressure. Climate risks of floods and typhoons rising.
Result
Palm oil vs Environment dilemma: The cost of green transition conflicts with rural livelihoods. Achieving both economic and environmental goals simultaneously is the biggest challenge.
🇮🇩 Indonesia
Process
Owner of the world's largest rainforest. World's 1st coal exporter (2023). World's 8th largest greenhouse gas emitter. Recurring forest fires in Sumatra and Borneo.
Phenomenon
Capital relocation to Nusantara: Aimed at addressing Jakarta's sinking (25cm/year) + population concentration. Nickel mining — environmental pollution reported in Sulawesi. ADB: Classified as high risk for climate vulnerability.
Result
Resource export-environmental trade-off is at its maximum: Nickel is key to EV batteries, but mining damage is concentrated on local communities. A classic case of unequal environmental burden.
🇻🇳 Vietnam
Process
Mekong Delta: Saline intrusion and land subsidence are severe due to climate change and upstream Chinese dam construction. Rapid expansion of solar and wind power (one of the fastest rates in the world, 2020-2024).
Phenomenon
CO₂ per capita 3.8t. Concentration of manufacturing → concerns over air pollution. However, the expansion rate of renewable energy is impressive. Threat of displacement in climate-vulnerable agricultural population-dense areas (Mekong Delta).
Result
Lack of control over the upstream Mekong River is the epicenter of a combined food-environmental crisis. F_reach S strength contains the possibility of being damaged by climate shocks.

ⅩⅤ Emerging 3 Southeast Asian Countries from the Perspective of the Korean Peninsula
Korean Peninsula Perspective — Standing in Front of a Mirror
South Korea passed through somewhere among the three emerging Southeast Asian nations 40 years ago. Vietnam's 6.5% growth rate resembles South Korea in the 1970s, and Indonesia's military-business collusion is a déjà vu of the Park Chung-hee era. Malaysia's ethnic preference policy is structurally similar to regionalism in South Korea.

However, resemblance does not mean destiny. That South Korea did not experience 1MDB is not because its institutions were different — South Korea also went through the IMF crisis — but due to the difference in what was learned from that shock.

Supply Chain Perspective: Vietnam is a key subcontracting base for South Korean manufacturing. A significant portion of Samsung's revenue is produced in Vietnam. The moment Vietnam's Σ crosses the threshold is also the moment it becomes a risk for South Korean companies.

Geopolitical Perspective: Malaysia, Indonesia, and Vietnam are all parties to the South China Sea issue. The hedging strategies of these three nations toward China are linked to the security structure of the Korean Peninsula.
