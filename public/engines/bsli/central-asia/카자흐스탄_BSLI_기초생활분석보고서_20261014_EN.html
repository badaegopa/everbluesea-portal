Executive Summary

Kazakhstan is positioned just above the basic living line (0) with a Series BSLI of +0.0913 (Grade E · 47th out of 139 countries). In the auxiliary time series v1.2, empirical measurements are available annually for 2016–2021: +0.0759 in 2016, +0.0917 in 2018, +0.0864 in 2020, and +0.0884 in 2021. § 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Weight is low because food crises have room for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Basic living sufficiency (median income normalized) — Weight is high because housing pressure is an irreversible chain from jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness.
D (subtraction): Deprivation index = $3.65/day (2017 PPP) poverty rate · H_s (subtraction): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant and excluded from variables due to global oversupply. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic living line. This country's Series value: +0.0913 (Grade E · 47th out of 139 countries).

Kazakhstan has a structure where, while the additive terms increase slightly, the subtractive term H_s (distribution disparity) expands alongside them to offset the gains. Deprivation at $3.65/day is virtually absent at 0.1%.
