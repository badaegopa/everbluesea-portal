Executive Summary

Zambia sits at a Series BSLI of -1.5316 (Grade E · 139th out of 139 countries), sunken more than 1.5 below the basic livelihood baseline (0). The empirical measurements in the supplementary time series v1.2 are for 2015 (-1.5780 in the 2016·2017 slots) and 2022 (-1.3566 in the 2020–2024 slots), while the 2018·2019 slots are empty.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index for 30th–50th percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Weight is low because food crises offer room for short-term recovery via reserves and aid.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Basic livelihood fulfillment (median income normalized) — Housing pressure has an irreversible chain of jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, hence the higher weight.
D (subtraction): Deprivation Index = $3.65/day (2017 PPP) poverty rate · H_s (subtraction): Housing Stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant due to global oversupply and removed from the variables. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic livelihood baseline. This country's Series value: -1.5316 (Grade E · 139th out of 139 countries).

Zambia presents a rare configuration in the BSLI 4-term equation where H_s (Palma^1.5) pulls down the index by almost the same magnitude as D. Food (F_ML) 0.0016 and Housing (H) 0.0027 are close to 0.
