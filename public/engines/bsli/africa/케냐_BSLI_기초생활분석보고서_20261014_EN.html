Executive Summary

Kenya has a Series BSLI of -0.8220 (Grade E · 123rd out of 139 countries), positioned far below the basic life line (0). Supplementary time series v1.2 consists of the 2016 and 2017 cells (2015 data, -0.6951) and empirical measurements for 2020 (-0.7446), 2021 (-0.8021), and 2022 (-0.7795), with 2023 and 2024 carrying over from 2022. The Series value is lower than any year in the time series.

Deprivation (D) determines BSLI: 0.5628 → 0.6695 → 0.6922 → 0.6748 ($3.65/day poverty rate 48.6% → 57.9% → 58.3%). Housing stress (H_s) shifted 0.1585 → 0.0963 → 0.1256 (Palma 1.92 → 1.52 → 1.72), and food (F_ML) and housing (H) are extremely small at 0.0221 and 0.0202.

According to Hermes collection (2026-10-14), the World Bank API 2021 PPP $3.00/day poverty rate went from 37.7% in 2015 to 44.4% in 2020, 46.4% in 2021, and 45.5% in 2022, while the national poverty line poverty rate moved from 36.1% to 42.9%, 38.6%, and 39.8%. Consumer inflation moved from 7.66% and 7.67% in 2022 and 2023 to 4.49% in 2024 and 4.07% in 2025.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Formula)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Mean real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — The weight is low because food crises have room for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Basic life fulfillment degree (median income normalized) — The weight is high because housing pressure is an irreversible chain from jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness.
D (subtraction): Deprivation index = $3.65/day (2017 PPP) poverty rate · H_s (subtraction): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing is treated as a constant due to global oversupply and excluded from variables. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic life line. Series value for this country: -0.8220 (Grade E · 123rd out of 139 countries).

In Kenya, D is 0.67, determining most of the BSLI. Between 2015 and 2020, inequality narrowed (Palma 1.92 → 1.52), but BSLI dropped because the poverty rate rose sharply, and in 2021, both the poverty rate and inequality increased simultaneously.
