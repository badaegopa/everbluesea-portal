§ 01

Executive Summary

Nigeria has a series BSLI of -0.7890 (Grade E · 118th out of 139 countries), lying well below the basic life line (0). Empirical measurements in auxiliary time series v1.2 are 2018 (-0.5901) and 2022 (-0.6867); the 2016 entry uses 2015 data, while the rest are carryovers. Between the two empirical measurements, BSLI declined, and the series value is lower than both.

The decline stemmed from an increase in Deprivation (D) from 0.5345 → 0.6375 ($3.65/day poverty rate 46.2% → 55.1%) and decreases in Food (F_ML) 0.0285 → 0.0236 and Housing (H) 0.0277 → 0.0221. Housing stress (H_s) decreased from 0.0836 → 0.0719 (Palma 1.43 → 1.35).

Based on Hermes collection (2026-10-14) from the World Bank API, the 2021 PPP $3.00/day poverty rate rose from 34.2% in 2018 to 41.8% in 2022, and the national poverty line rate rose from 40.1% to 56.2%. Consumer inflation was 24.66% in 2023, 33.24% in 2024, and 23.01% in 2025, while GNI per capita (Atlas) fell from $2,940 in 2022 to $1,360 in 2025.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What It Measures

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Lower weight due to short-term recovery potential from stockpiles and aid during food crises.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Basic life adequacy (normalized median income) — Higher weight due to the irreversible cascade of housing pressure: jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness.
D (Subtraction): Deprivation Index = poverty rate at $3.65/day (2017 PPP) · H_s (Subtraction): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing is treated as a constant and excluded from variables due to global oversupply. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic life line. Series value for this country: -0.7890 (Grade E · 118th out of 139 countries).

In Nigeria, D is 0.64, dragging down BSLI; between 2018 and 2022, BSLI declined as D grew and positive terms shrank. The subsequent 2023–2025 inflation of 23–33% and income decline in dollar terms fall outside the auxiliary time series.
