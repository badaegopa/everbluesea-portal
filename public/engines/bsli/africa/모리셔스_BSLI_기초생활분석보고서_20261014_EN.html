Executive Summary

Mauritius stands just above the basic living line (0) with a Series BSLI of +0.0155 (Grade E · 54th out of 139 countries). The empirical measurement in the auxiliary time series v1.2 is a single point in 2017 (+0.0164); the 2016, 2018, and 2019 cells carry this value forward, and 2020–2024 are absent of data. In 2026, reports emerged indicating that inflation reached its highest level in over two years, along with an October 13 report of the 'worst unrest since 1999.'

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, Weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — food crises have room for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid, hence a lower weight.
H (Housing, Weight 0.60): Basic living adequacy (normalized to median income) — housing pressure is an irreversible chain of jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, hence a higher weight.
D (Subtractive): Deprivation Index = $3.65/day (2017 PPP) poverty rate · H_s (Subtractive): Housing Stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant due to global oversupply and excluded from the variables. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic living line. This country's Series value: +0.0155 (Grade E · 54th out of 139 countries).

Mauritius has additive and subtractive terms nearly balancing each other out, placing BSLI near 0. Because international distribution statistics halted in 2017, changes in livelihood following the 10.77% inflation rate in 2022 are not captured in the auxiliary time series.
