Executive Summary

Pakistan's Series BSLI is -0.4598 (Grade E · 96th out of 139 countries), placing it below the basic living line (0). The empirical measurements in the supplementary time series v1.2 are 2018 (-0.3758) and 2024 (-0.4665); the remaining cells are carry-forwards of adjacent empirical measurements (the 2016 cell uses 2015 data), and 2021 lacks data. Between the two empirical measurements, BSLI declined, and the Series value is close to the 2024 value.

The decline was jointly driven by an increase in deprivation (D) from 0.3693 → 0.4280 ($3.65/day poverty rate 31.9% → 37.0%), an increase in housing stress (H_s) from 0.0426 → 0.0726 (Palma 1.12 → 1.35), and decreases in food (F_ML) from 0.0392 → 0.0360 and housing (H) from 0.0342 → 0.0327 — all four terms moved in the direction of lowering BSLI.

Based on the Hermes collection (2026-10-14), according to the World Bank API, the 2021 PPP $3.00/day poverty rate rose from 16.5% in 2018 to 23% in 2024, the national poverty line poverty rate rose from 22.2% to 28.9%, and the Gini rose from 29.6 to 33.5. Consumer prices have no values in the API, and the unemployment rate (ILO model) is around 5.4%.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Food crises have room for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid, hence a lower weight.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Degree of basic living fulfillment (normalized to median income) — Housing pressure forms an irreversible chain of jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, hence a higher weight.
D (subtraction): Deprivation Index = poverty rate at $3.65/day (2017 PPP) · H_s (subtraction): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing is treated as a constant due to global oversupply and excluded from the variables. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic living line. This country's Series value: -0.4598 (Grade E · 96th out of 139 countries).

Pakistan is a country where all four terms moved in the direction of lowering BSLI between 2018 and 2024. During the same period, both the international extreme poverty line and the national poverty line rose, showing no directional discrepancy between measurement criteria.
