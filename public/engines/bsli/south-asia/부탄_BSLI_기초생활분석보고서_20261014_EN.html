§ 01

Executive Summary

Bhutan lies below the basic living line (0) with a Series BSLI of -0.1396 (Grade E · 73rd of 139 countries). However, the auxiliary time series v1.2 has only two measured points, in 2017 (-0.0841) and 2022 (+0.1429), and the 2022 value lies above the basic living line at Grade D — the sign differs from the Series value (canonical verification required C-1). The remaining cells are carried forward, and data for 2020 are absent. The reference value is the Series.

Between the two measurements, Deprivation (D) decreased from 0.0672 → 0.0020 (poverty rate at $3.65/day 5.8% → 0.2%), Housing Stress (H_s) decreased from 0.1078 → 0.0302 (Palma 1.60 → 1.02), and Food (F_ML) and Housing (H) nearly doubled from 0.0906 and 0.0912 to 0.1813 and 0.1709, respectively.

Based on the Hermes collection (2026-10-14), the World Bank API 2021 PPP poverty rate at $8.30/day fell from 42.5% in 2017 to 9% in 2022, and the Gini index dropped from 37.4 to 28.5. The poverty rate at the national poverty line was 12.4% in 2022. In 2026, reports were collected on the cost of living burden on the working poor and rising urban rents.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Weight is low because food crises have room for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Baseline living fulfillment (median income normalized) — Housing pressure is an irreversible chain from jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, hence the higher weight.
D (Subtraction): Deprivation Index = poverty rate at $3.65/day (2017 PPP) · H_s (Subtraction): Housing Stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant due to global oversupply and excluded from the variables. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic living line. This country's Series value: -0.1396 (Grade E · 73rd among 139 countries).

Bhutan is a rare case in which all four terms moved in a direction that raised BSLI between 2017 and 2022. Yet the Series cross-sectional value is negative — the difference in calculation criteria between the two windows reverses the sign for this country.
