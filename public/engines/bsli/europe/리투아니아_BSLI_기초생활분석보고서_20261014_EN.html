Executive Summary

Lithuania's Series BSLI is +0.3172 (Grade C · 32nd among 139 countries), placing it above the baseline living threshold (0). Supplementary time series v1.2 features annual empirical measurements from 2016 to 2023: rising from +0.1452 (Grade D) in 2016 to +0.3251 (Grade C) in 2021, declining to +0.2972 in 2022, and reaching +0.3105 in 2023. Consumer price inflation was 19.71% in 2022 and 9.12% in 2023, and reports collected in September 2026 noted a Eurozone-high inflation of 5.8%.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtractive Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Food crises carry a lower weight due to room for short-term recovery via stockpiles and aid.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Baseline living adequacy (normalized median income) — Housing pressure carries a higher weight because it represents an irreversible cascade: jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness.
D (Subtractive): Deprivation index = $3.65/day (2017 PPP) poverty rate · H_s (Subtractive): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant and removed from variables due to global oversupply. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = baseline living threshold. Series value for this country: +0.3172 (Grade C · 32nd among 139 countries).

Lithuania is a country that climbed from Grade D to Grade C as its additive terms expanded. Its BSLI underwent an inflection during the high inflation period of 2022 — because it is measured annually, this inflection is directly reflected in the time series. The national poverty line uses a relative standard (60% of median income) and thus measures something different from the absolute standard D-term of the BSLI.
