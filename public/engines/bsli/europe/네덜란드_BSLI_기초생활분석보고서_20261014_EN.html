Executive Summary

The Netherlands stands well above the basic life line (0) with a Compendium BSLI of +0.7441 (Grade A · 8th among 139 countries). In auxiliary time series v1.2, 2016–2021 are annually empirically measured: rising from +0.6648 (Grade B) in 2016, through +0.6819 in 2019, to +0.7640 in 2020 and +0.7770 (Grade A) in 2021. Consumer price inflation moved from 10% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2025.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical (4-Term Subtractive Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML(食, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — food crises have room for short-term recovery via stockpiles and aid, hence a lower weight.
H(住, weight 0.60): Basic life fulfillment degree (median income normalized) — housing pressure entails an irreversible chain of jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, hence a higher weight.
D(subtracted): Deprivation index = poverty rate at $3.65 a day (2017 PPP) · H_s(subtracted): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
衣(clothing) is treated as a constant and excluded from variables due to global oversupply. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic life line. This country's compendium value: +0.7441 (Grade A · 8th among 139 countries).

The Netherlands' BSLI is determined by the additive terms, reaching Grade A as inequality narrowed in 2020–2021 (Gini 25.7). During the same period, the poverty rate at the relative poverty risk line rose — disparities across the overall income distribution and poverty relative to median income measure different phenomena.
