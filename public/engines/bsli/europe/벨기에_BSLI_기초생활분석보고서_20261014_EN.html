Executive Summary

Belgium stands well above the basic living line (0) with a Series BSLI of +0.7387 (Grade A · 9th out of 139 countries). The supplementary time series v1.2 features annual empirical measurements from 2016 to 2023: rising steadily each year from +0.6340 (Grade B) in 2016 to +0.7077 (Grade A) in 2021, before recording +0.6844 in 2022 and +0.6999 (Grade B) in 2023. Consumer inflation dropped from 9.6% in 2022 to 2.47% in 2025, with an unemployment rate (ILO model) of 5.91%.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtractive Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Given that food crises offer leeway for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid, it carries a lower weight.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Basic life adequacy (normalized to median income) — Housing pressure forms an irreversible chain of jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, hence receiving a higher weight.
D (subtraction): Deprivation Index = $3.65/day (2017 PPP) poverty rate · H_s (subtraction): Housing Stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) is treated as a constant due to global oversupply and excluded from variables. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic living line. This country's Series value: +0.7387 (Grade A · 9th out of 139 countries).

Belgium has small disparities with a Gini of 26.8 and a Palma of 0.95, meaning the BSLI directly reflects the magnitude of the additive terms. During the high-inflation period of 2022, a drop in additive terms brought it down from Grade A to Grade B.
