Executive Summary

Thailand stands just above the baseline of basic living (0) with a Series BSLI of +0.0550 (Grade E · 51st among 139 countries). The auxiliary time series v1.2 consists entirely of empirical measurements from 2016 to 2024: rising from +0.0381 in 2016 to +0.1026 in 2023 (the only year above the Grade D boundary), before settling at +0.0972 in 2024. For 2026, reports were gathered stating that the impoverished population had grown to 3.4 million and that the official CPI was being re-examined for being misaligned with household burdens.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Formula — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Formula)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML(Food, weight 0.40): Mean real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Weighted lower as food crises offer room for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid.
H(Housing, weight 0.60): Basic living adequacy (normalized median income) — Weighted higher because housing pressure constitutes an irreversible chain: jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness.
D(subtracted): Deprivation Index = $3.65/day (2017 PPP) poverty rate · H_s(subtracted): Housing Stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant and removed from variables due to global oversupply. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = baseline of basic living. This country's Series value: +0.0550 (Grade E · 51st among 139 countries).

Even though Thailand's deprivation (D) is nearly 0, its BSLI hovers near the baseline of basic living. Rather than the subtracted terms, the magnitudes of the additive terms (F_ML · H) determine the BSLI. The disparity between the extreme poverty line of 0% and the upper-middle-income line of 11.7% demonstrates this structure.
