Executive Summary

Brazil lies below the baseline of basic living (0) with a Compendium BSLI of -0.2409 (Grade E · 80th among 139 countries). The supplementary time series v1.2 features annual empirical measurements from 2016 to 2024: -0.5319 to -0.5403 for 2016–2019, -0.2420 in 2020, -0.5162 in 2021, and subsequently rising to -0.2976 in 2024. National poverty line values are not recorded. Consumer price inflation dropped from 9.28% in 2022 to 5.02% in 2025, and unemployment (ILO model) fell from 9.23% to 5.97%.

§ 02

BSLI Four-Term Formula — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (Four-Term Subtraction Formula)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — food crises have room for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid, hence the lower weight.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Baseline living fulfillment (normalized by median income) — housing pressure entails an irreversible chain of jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, hence the higher weight.
D (Subtraction): Deprivation Index = poverty rate at $3.65/day (2017 PPP) · H_s (Subtraction): Housing Stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant and removed from variables due to global oversupply. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = baseline of basic living. This country's Compendium value: -0.2409 (Grade E · 80th among 139 countries).

In Brazil, H_s ranges from 0.35 to 0.60, far exceeding the additive terms (F_ML and H around 0.12 to 0.18), placing its BSLI below the baseline of basic living. In 2020, Palma fell from 4.01 to 2.98 and the poverty rate dropped from 9.1% to 3.9% for a single year before rebounding in 2021. From 2021 to 2024, Palma and the poverty rate decreased annually, causing the BSLI to rise.
