§ 01

Executive Summary

Chile's Series BSLI is -0.0510 (Grade E · 62nd of 139 countries), just below the basic living line (0). However, the measured values in the auxiliary time series v1.2 are -0.0534 in 2017, -0.1171 in 2020, +0.0240 in 2022, and +0.0548 in 2024, with the 2022 and 2024 values above the basic living line — the signs differ from the Series value (canonical verification required C-1). The remaining cells are carried forward from adjacent measured years. The reference value is the Series.

In this country, housing stress (H_s) is far greater than deprivation (D): in 2024, H_s was 0.2012 (Palma 2.18) and D was 0.0067 (poverty rate at $3.65/day was 0.6%). In 2020, as H_s (0.2904, Palma 2.67) and D (0.0263) both increased, BSLI declined; in 2022–2024, Food (F_ML) rose from 0.2403 → 0.2627 and Housing (H) rose from 0.2408 → 0.2627, crossing above the basic living line.

Based on Hermes collection (2026-10-14), the World Bank API poverty rate at $8.30/day (2021 PPP) was 9% in 2017, 11.3% in 2020, 6.1% in 2022, and 4.5% in 2024, while the Gini index moved from 45.3 → 47 → 43.2 → 43. The unemployment rate (ILO modeled estimate) is in the 8–9% range.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Formula — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Formula)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Mean real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Given room for short-term recovery via stockpiles and aid during a food crisis, its weight is lower.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Basic life fulfillment (normalized to median income) — Because housing stress forms an irreversible chain of jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, its weight is higher.
D (Subtraction): Deprivation Index = poverty rate at $3.65/day (2017 PPP) · H_s (Subtraction): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant and excluded from the variables due to global oversupply. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic living line. This country's Series value: -0.0510 (Grade E · 62nd out of 139 countries).

Chile's BSLI is determined by distribution disparity. Although the additive terms (0.26 each for F_ML and H in 2024) are not small, H_s (0.20–0.29) stemming from a Palma ratio above 2 almost offsets them, keeping the BSLI hovering around 0.
