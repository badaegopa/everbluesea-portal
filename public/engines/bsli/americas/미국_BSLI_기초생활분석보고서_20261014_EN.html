Executive Summary

The United States is well above the basic livelihood line (0) with a Series BSLI of +0.6998 (Grade B · 13th out of 139 countries). The supplementary time series v1.2 is entirely empirically measured from 2016 to 2024 and all Grade B: rising significantly from +0.5062 in 2016 to +0.5779 in 2019, +0.6653 in 2020, and +0.6678 in 2021, falling to +0.5495 in 2022, and rising again to +0.5701 in 2023 and +0.5959 in 2024. In September 2026, reports that the Census Bureau's official poverty rate reached an all-time low were collected alongside mixed assessments regarding this.

§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Formula)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — weighted lower because food crises have room for short-term recovery through stockpiles and aid.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Basic living fulfillment degree (median income normalized) — weighted higher because housing pressure is an irreversible chain from jeonse → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness.
D (subtraction): Deprivation Index = $3.65/day (2017 PPP) poverty rate · H_s (subtraction): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing (衣) was treated as a constant due to global oversupply and excluded from variables. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic livelihood line. Series value for this country: +0.6998 (Grade B · 13th out of 139 countries).

The BSLI of the United States is jointly determined by large additive terms and a non-negligible H_s (Palma around 2). The large fluctuations of 2020–2021 and 2022 reflect the changes in the income distribution itself during the same period being directly captured in annual empirical measurements (recorded without interpretation).
