§ 02

BSLI 4-Term Equation — What Does It Measure?

BSLI v7.0 Canonical Version (4-Term Subtraction Equation)
BSLI = 0.40·F_ML + 0.60·H − D − H_s
F_ML (Food, weight 0.40): Average real income index of the 30th–50th income percentiles (ML stratum) (World Bank PIP) — Food crises have room for short-term recovery through reserves and aid, so the weight is low.
H (Housing, weight 0.60): Basic living sufficiency (normalized to median income) — Housing pressure forms an irreversible cascade of key money deposit → monthly rent → gosiwon → homelessness, so the weight is high.
D (Subtraction): Deprivation Index = poverty rate at $3.65/day (2017 PPP) · H_s (Subtraction): Housing stress = Palma ratio^1.5.
Clothing is treated as a constant due to global oversupply and excluded from the variables. Range [−2.0, +1.0], 0 = basic living line. This country's Series value: -0.3583 (Grade E · 92nd among 139 countries).

In Nicaragua's 2014 data, H_s (0.28) and D (0.13) are far larger than the additive terms (approx. 0.08), placing the BSLI below the basic living line. Since then, because household income distribution data have been absent from international databases, changes cannot be observed through the auxiliary time series.

For the same reason, the BSLI auxiliary time series contains only a single point. Published figures were kept verbatim (DECISIONS 17·19) and placed side by side.
