01 · Summary

One in four rulers met their end at the hands of internal forces. Out of 741 European monarch reigns, 186 (25%) ended in deposition or murder, and out of 249 Chinese rulers, 62 (25.3%) were deposed by elites. These data come from two mutually independent research teams.

The Power Parasite Index (PPI) measures actors and structures that attach themselves to public power to extract private gain and cloud the leader's judgment to preserve their status. This report re-establishes the prototype of June 1, 2026 into an axiomatic system and bolsters it with three types of evidence.

  • 226 Modern Cases: Approx. 45 countries, monarchy, theocracy, dictatorship, prolonged rule, United States, South Korea. The status of events in 2025–2026 was verified as of 2026-10-12.
  • Complete Enumeration of 16,661 Rulers Across Eras(Wikidata) and externally validated data: Archigos 4.1 (3,409 leaders, 1875–2015), Kokkonen et al. (European monarchs, 1000–1799), Wang (Chinese rulers, 221 BC–1911).
  • Prior Research: Dictator's loyalty–competence tradeoff, selectorate theory, kleptocracy models, state capture, elite capture of aid.

Three Key Findings

  1. Downfall caused by internal forces knew no boundaries of civilization. Europe 25%, China 25.3%. In both regions, this plummeted sharply after 1000 AD.
  2. Parasitism did not disappear; it changed its form. In the Middle Ages, it operated through regencies, blood relations, and depositions (the sword); in modern times, it functions through legalization, pardons, and transnational rents (law and money).
  3. Out of 226 modern cases, only three countries severed parasitism from within. South Korea (impeachment, special counsel, courts), the United States (courts), and Hungary (elections). The rest relied on external justice or sanctions, or lacked any checks.

04 · Measurement — Two-Layer Index and Leader Deduction

The PPI measures individual parasites (actor layer) and state structures (structure layer) separately, deducting the cost of parasitism from the leader index.

Actor Layer — Individual Parasite i

PPI-Ai = α·Ps + β·Fb + γ·Lc

Ps is private gain pursuit (changes in asset disclosures, conflict-of-interest findings, offshore deposits), Fb is feedback blocking (6 stages below), and Lc is leader capture (the extent to which personnel and policy tilt toward that associate's interests). Each term is 0–1, and weights α, β, and γ are 1/3 prior to calibration.

Structure Layer — State

PPI-S = α·Capture + β·Circulation + γ·Corruption + δ·Media

Capture is capture of regulatory and oversight bodies, Circulation is elite circulation disruption, Corruption is entrenched corruption (inverse of TI CPI, etc.), and Media is press control (inverse of RSF Index, etc.). Legalized parasitism—that is, parasitism with no point of detection—is measured in this layer.

Leader Deduction

PPI-Ai = α·Ps + β·Fb + γ·Lc

LRI-P₀ is leader competence excluding the influence of parasitic aides. h is hiring responsibility (red flags already exposed prior to appointment, 0–1), s is oversight responsibility (delay from onset of parasitism to detection ÷ tenure, 0–1), and Rt is retention responsibility (retention duration post-detection ÷ tenure prior to detection, with promotions, pardons, and reinstatements added). The original 06-01 draft had only Rt; h and s are reinforcements completing Axiom P2 (Three-Stage Responsibility).

05 · Historical Baseline — How Rulers Met Their End

One in four rulers met their end at the hands of internal forces, and that proportion plummeted sharply after 1000 AD.

Of the 186 depositions of European monarchs, 61 (32.8%) were perpetrated by close blood relatives (children, siblings, uncles, spouses, cousins). Wang (2022) explains China's decline through the “ruler's dilemma”: the emperor preserved himself by dividing the elites, at the cost of weakening the state.

The top cause for irregular exit is “military ousted without foreign support” (277 cases).

As child accessions disappeared, the conditions structurally giving rise to regencies also vanished. The hypothesis of this report is that in contemporary times, titular parasitism and family-mediated parasitism have filled that void.

Data Limitations. Wikidata's records on manner of death are heavily skewed toward dramatic deaths. Among medieval monarchs with recorded manners of death, violent deaths account for 47%, and for Chinese emperors, 81%. External baselines are approx. 22% for Europe (Eisner 2011) and 25% for China (Wang). Therefore, the proportions for “how they met their end” rely exclusively on externally validated data.

Middle Ages (incl. prior)

① Merovingian Dynasty — A King in Name Only, the Mayor Who Took the Throne (Frankish Kingdom, 7th–8th centuries). From the late 7th century, kings handed over de facto administrative and military power to the Mayor of the Palace (Major Domo), an office that became hereditary within the Pippinid family. In 751, Mayor Pippin III deposed the last king, Childeric III, confined him to a monastery, and proclaimed himself king. Loop R7 (titular parasitism → host replacement). Records portraying the late Merovingian kings as “do-nothing kings” were mostly compiled by the Carolingians—the narrative of the victors.

② Emperors Xianzong and Wenzong of Tang — Eunuchs Who Replaced the Emperor (Tang Dynasty, 9th century). Following the An Lushan Rebellion, eunuch factions holding command over the Imperial Palace Guard intervened in the enthronement and deposition of emperors. Official histories attribute Emperor Xianzong's death in 820 to eunuchs. Emperor Wenzong's attempt to eliminate the eunuchs in 835 (the Sweet Dew Incident) failed, leading to the massacre of numerous high officials. According to Wang (2022), 5 out of the 12 late Tang emperors were deposed by elites. Loop R3 (the Imperial Guard, meant to protect the emperor, serving as an instrument of eunuchs) and R7.

③ Hugh III of Cyprus — From Regent of an Infant King to King (1261–1284). Became regent for the infant king Hugh II in 1261, and succeeded to the throne when Hugh II died at age 14 in 1267. This is one of the 8 cases identified in this report's regency analysis where “a regent became monarch themselves.” Loop R7.

④ Karl Knutsson of Sweden — The Man Who Became King Three Times and Was Ousted Twice (1438–1470). Regent 1438–1440, king in 1448. Amidst factions of nobility and the church, exiled in 1457, restored in 1464 only to be ousted again in 1465, ascended a third time in 1467, and died in office in 1470. Loop R2 (a ruler with a weak base leans on factional coalitions, and is ousted when the coalition turns).

⑤ Yeonsangun of Joseon — The King Who Shut the Channels of Remonstrance (1494–1506). After executing and exiling scores of critical officials through the Literati Purges of Muo (1498) and Gapja (1504), he abolished remonstrance institutions such as Saganwon and royal lectures. A textbook case of Axiom P4 (feedback blocking). Deposed in 1506 by the Jungjong Coup, exiled to Gyodong Island, and died that same year. Loop R1. The Annals of Yeonsangun was compiled by the coup faction—the relationship between the recorder and the subject must be noted.

Modern

⑥ Sedo Politics of Joseon — 60 Years of Young Kings and Maternal In-Law Clans (King Sunjo to King Cheoljong, 1800–1863). When Sunjo ascended the throne at the age of 11, maternal in-law families seized control of the Bibyeonsa (Border Defense Council) and key government posts following a period of regency behind the purdah. The sale of government offices and the corruption of the Three Tax Administrations (land, military, and grain loan systems) ensued, erupting into the Hong Gyeong-nae Rebellion of 1811 and the Imsul Peasant Uprising of 1862. Loops R7·R8 (maternal relatives) → B1. East Asian regencies are scarcely structured in Wikidata, necessitating primary sources from the Veritable Records (Sillok).

⑦ Nicholas II and Rasputin — A Parasite without an Official Title (Russian Empire, 1894–1917). Having gained the Empress's trust through the belief that he could treat the hemophiliac Tsarevich, Rasputin held no official post. After the Tsar departed for the front lines in 1915, he exerted influence over ministerial appointments, and despite a deluge of criticism, the Tsar did not dismiss him — retention coefficient (Rt) maximized. In late 1916, nobles assassinated Rasputin, the Tsar abdicated due to the 1917 revolution, and his family was executed in 1918. Loop R1 → B1. This is the first case in the historical table of the Cheonghae (淸海) draft (06-01).

Modern Era

⑧ Syngman Rhee — Constitutional Amendment, Rigged Elections, and Revolution (1948–1960). The term-limit restriction for the inaugural president was eliminated via the 1954 round-to-the-nearest (sasa-oip) constitutional amendment (legalization). In the March 15 election of 1960, massive electoral fraud took place to secure the election of his successor, Lee Ki-poong. April 19 Revolution → resignation on April 26 → exile to Hawaii, died in 1965. Lee Ki-poong's family committed mass suicide on April 28. Loop R4 → B1.

⑨ Park Chung-hee and Cha Ji-chul — The Confidant's Bullet (1961–1979). A military coup in 1961, and term limits were abolished through the 1972 Yushin Constitution. Presidential Security Chief Cha Ji-chul overstepped his authority, clashing with KCIA Director Kim Jae-gyu. On October 26, 1979, Kim Jae-gyu shot and killed Park Chung-hee and Cha Ji-chul. A modern reenactment of a medieval-style demise concluded by extra-institutional internal violence. Loop R4 → R1.

⑩ Chun Doo-hwan and Hanahoe — Seizure of Power by a Private Faction and Pardon (1980–1988). Came to power following the December 12 military insurrection and the bloody suppression of May 18. Hanahoe, an internal military private faction, seized core posts, and the Ilhae Foundation was established as a post-retirement base. Hanahoe was dismantled in 1993; sentenced to death in the first trial in 1996, life imprisonment in the second trial, and finalized in April 1997. However, he received a special pardon on December 22, 1997, and died in 2021 with a substantial portion of forfeiture fines unpaid. Loop E6 (parasitic network) → B2 → R5.

⑪ Ferdinand Marcos — Kleptocracy and the Return of the Dynasty (Philippines, 1965–1986). Martial law in 1972, "crony capitalism" granting monopolies to crony enterprises. External debt surged from $360 million in 1962 to $17.2 billion in 1980. The Presidential Commission on Good Government (PCGG) estimated that $5 billion to $10 billion was siphoned off. 1983 Aquino assassination → 1986 People Power Revolution → exile to Hawaii, died in 1989. Switzerland returned $684 million, and Imelda was convicted of corruption in 2018. In 2022, his son Bongbong Marcos was elected president. Loops E2·R8 → B1 → post-mortem persistence.

⑫ Mobutu Sese Seko — The Face of "Kleptocracy" (Zaire, 1965–1997). Thirty-two years of one-man rule, with Western support propping up the regime during the Cold War. In 1997, as rebel forces approached the capital, he fled into exile via Togo to Morocco, dying in September of the same year. Loops E2 (leader themself as the parasitic agent)·R6 (external patronage).

⑬ Suharto — The Value of Connections Revealed by Stock Prices (Indonesia, 1967–1998). 32-year reign, during which his children and crony businesses dominated major enterprises. Fisman (2001) showed that whenever rumors of Suharto's deteriorating health circulated, stock prices of connected firms fell more sharply. Following the 1997 financial crisis where the rupiah crashed from 2,600 to approximately 17,000 per dollar, he stepped down in May 1998, was declared unfit to stand trial due to poor health, and died in 2008. Loop R8 → B1.

⑭ Robert Mugabe — The Rise of the Spouse and the Successor's Counterattack (Zimbabwe, 1980–2017). In his final years, his spouse Grace emerged as successor, and when he dismissed Vice President Mnangagwa in November 2017, the military intervened, leading to his resignation. Even after retirement, he received a mansion, up to 23 staff members, and approximately $10 million, preserving his wealth (post-retirement protection). His successor Mnangagwa signed a constitutional amendment on July 7, 2026, extending the presidential term to 7 years and abolishing direct elections. Loop R8 → internal purge → post-retirement protection → R4.

⑮ The Rajapaksa Brothers — The Outcome of a Family Running a Nation (Sri Lanka, 2005–2022). The family divvied up major offices, including Mahinda (President/Prime Minister), Gotabaya (President), and Basil (Finance Minister). Amid foreign exchange depletion in 2022, protesters occupied the presidential residence on July 9, and Gotabaya fled abroad and resigned. In November 2023, the Supreme Court ruled that the brothers bore responsibility for the economic mismanagement. Loop R8 → B1. This is the first empirical case cited in the original draft.

⑯ Sheikh Hasina — Suppression Accelerated the End (Bangladesh, 2009–2024). 15 consecutive years in power. In 2024, she violently suppressed student protests, but demonstrations spread, leading her to resign on August 5 and flee to India. The International Crimes Tribunal conducted an in absentia trial beginning in June 2025. Loop R3 → B1.

⑰ Bashar al-Assad — A Hereditary Nation, the Younger Brother's Safe (Syria, 2000–2024). Succeeded his father, and the illicit drug (Captagon) revenue apparatus of the 4th Armoured Division commanded by his younger brother Maher was exposed after the collapse. On December 8, 2024, he fled into exile in Moscow, and on August 11, 2026, a Damascus court sentenced Assad and Maher to death in absentia. Loops R8·R6 → B3.

⑱ Vladimir Putin — The Rebel Dies, the Minister Is Promoted, and the Deputy Minister Is Imprisoned (Russia, 1999–). In June 2023, Prigozhin staged a mutiny and died in a plane crash two months later. In May 2024, Defense Minister Shoigu was reassigned to Secretary of the Security Council immediately after his dismissal, while his deputy minister Ivanov was sentenced to 13 years in prison in July 2025. Navalny died in prison in February 2024, and on February 14, 2026, five European countries announced that the toxin epibatidine was detected. Ongoing. Loop R3 (tier-selective punishment).

⑲ Nicolás Maduro — The Leader Is Seized, but the Structure Remains (Venezuela, 2013–2026). On January 3, 2026, US forces arrested Maduro and his wife in Caracas, and the trial was scheduled for June 1, 2027. Acting President Delcy Rodríguez cooperated with the US and agreed to reopen the US Embassy. Loop B3 → R6 (leader is removed, but the crony structure bonds with a new patron).

⑳ Viktor Orbán — The Tycoon Friend and the Judgment of the Ballot Box (Hungary, 2010–2026). Insights Revealed Across the 24 Pathways

  1. The starting point is identical — Shutting out remonstrance (Yeonsangun), opening doors to people without official posts (Rasputin, Choi Seo-won), and parceling out the country to family members (sedo politics, Rajapaksa, Marcos).
  2. When institutions failed to sever it, the blade or the street severed it — Internal violence for the Merovingians, Tang, and Park Chung-hee; popular uprisings for sedo politics, Nicholas II, Syngman Rhee, Rajapaksa, and Hasina. Where institutions severed it (Chun Doo-hwan's trial, the dismissals of Park Geun-hye and Yoon Suk-yeol, Orbán's election), less blood was shed.
  3. Even when severed, it returns — Pardons (Chun Doo-hwan, Park Geun-hye), reappointments (Jeong Ho-seong), the return of dynasties (Marcos 2022), constitutional amendments by successor regimes (Zimbabwe), and shifting patrons (Venezuela, Hungary).

09 · Conditions Under Which Balancing Loops Overcome Reinforcing Loops — Hypotheses to Test

The most crucial hypothesis is H12: Grassroots resistance ignites only where internal immunity has failed.

10 · Structure of the Guidebook — Six-Layer Discourse Reasoning Method

This study is a data-driven edition of the venerable "Mirror for Princes" genre. It belongs to the lineage traced through the Zhenguan Zhengyao, Zizhi Tongjian, the Islamic Book of Government (Siyasatnama), Ibn Khaldun's Muqaddimah, and Machiavelli's The Prince. Blaydes, Grimmer & McQueen (2018) quantitatively analyzed medieval Christian and Islamic mirrors for princes, demonstrating that The Prince was not an abrupt rupture but the culmination of centuries of advice literature.

Layer 6 — The Author's Question (Cheonghae (淸海))

"Leader, for whose sake do you act?" (Recorded 2026-06-29). The author finalized the wording with this sentence (2026-10-12). This is as far as data can speak: "Leaders who walked this path met such ends — which path are you walking right now?"

According to Egorov & Sonin (2011), choosing loyal incompetence is rational for a vulnerable leader. Therefore, the message of the guidebook should not be "they harbor parasites because they are foolish," but "the more vulnerable they are, the greater the temptation becomes," in order to align with the evidence.

11 · Limitations and Counterarguments

The greatest limitation lies in the bias of historical records themselves.

  • Winners' History — Medieval records were mostly compiled by successor courts (Yeonsangun/Gwanghaegun, Richard III, late Merovingians). The relationship between chroniclers and their subjects must be clearly noted.
  • Survivorship Bias — Rulers of fallen minor states left no records. 55–65% of medieval and modern records in Wikidata are European.
  • Cause of Death Recording Bias — Wikidata records causes of death more frequently when the death was dramatic (81% for Chinese emperors vs. 25% for Wang). For the ratio of "how it ended," only externally validated data are used.
  • Anachronism — "Privatization of public interest" cannot be applied unadjusted to the era of the divine right of kings. It must be measured as a deviation relative to the legitimacy norms of each era.
  • Measurement Asymmetry — Asset declarations and inspection reports are more abundant in transparent countries. Cross-national comparisons are conducted exclusively via the structural layer (PPI-S).
  • Over-attribution to Leaders — Structures also make leaders. This is the rationale for separating the actor layer from the structural layer.
  • Ongoing Cases — Allegations concerning living individuals are not assigned the same weight as finalized judgments until a definitive verdict is reached. Cases in 2026 are evaluated as of 2026-10-12.
  • Misuse of Prescriptions — Patterns are correlations, not laws. Rulers who read the guidebook could exploit it in reverse (as seen in the history of The Prince).
