Historical Structure

§D · Historical Structure Analysis — From Uncolonized Empire to Ethnic Federalism

Today's multi-front reality is not an event of 2026. In Africa, Ethiopia was the only empire never colonized (repelling Italy at the 1896 Battle of Adwa), inheriting both that pride and the fracture that the empire was an Amhara-centric integration. Following the violence of the Derg military regime (1974–91), 27 years of TPLF-led EPRDF rule (1991–2018) — ethnic federalism (1995 constitution) codified self-determination for each ethnic group, yet real power remained concentrated in the minority Tigrayan elite.

Abiy's rise in 2018 was a reaction against this 27-year structure. However, as reforms leaned toward central recentralization, various regions that formally held self-determination defected one after another citing "broken promises." Tigray against loss of power, Amhara against territorial (Wolkait) and security integration, and Oromo against incomplete autonomy revolted. The very design of ethnic federalism a structure that turns central recentralization attempts back into centrifugal separation had inherently embedded.

Layered onto this is the GERD (Blue Nile) and Red Sea geopolitical stratum. Upper Nile development turned water conflicts with Egypt and Sudan into a constant, while Red Sea port claims formalized a border crisis with Eritrea. Domestic centrifugal separation and external pressure resonating at the same time is the essence of the 2026 situation.

"Abiy inherited a 27-year structure and attempted reform, but ethnic federalism was designed to turn central recentralization back into centrifugal force. The epicenter is not the mistake of a single leader, but the very method by which empire and federation were bonded."— Observation of this analysis session / Everblue Sea Institute for Social Dynamics
