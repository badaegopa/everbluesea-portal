Explanatory Notes

  1. This book is Volume 1 of the Uprising Series and forms the foundation for the regional country sections starting from Volume 2. The regional division of the country sections is identical to that of the Country Analysis Series (2026-10-07).
  2. Eras are divided benchmarked to 1760 (decision by Cheonghae, 2026-10-14). The year 1760 is commonly used as the onset of the British Industrial Revolution. Uprisings, peasant revolts, and protests prior to that are recorded as narratives based on historical records and monographs, whereas uprisings, protests, regime overthrows, civil wars, and coups after that are recorded using data where systematic datasets exist, and research literature where they do not.
  3. All figures in modern data were transferred programmatically from raw data files. No figures were entered by hand. Raw data values are neither altered nor filtered (preserving empirical measurements). Each event is annotated with the dataset name and version.
  4. All narratives and literature citations include sources. Wikipedia, Namuwiki, and blogs are not used as sources; primary historical records, public databases, academic papers, judicial rulings, and reports by governments and commemorative foundations are used instead. When official announcements differ from independent investigations, both are documented side by side.
  5. Entries absent from data are marked as "Unrecorded," and unverified items are marked as "Unconfirmed." A distinction is made between 0 and unrecorded.

Introduction — What This Series Does

The Uprising Series collects records of people rising up against power across countries. Because systematic event data do not exist prior to the Industrial Revolution, stories preserved in historical records—who rose, why, how, and how it ended—are rendered into narrative form. After the Industrial Revolution, since datasets compiled by researchers according to defined criteria and standards are available, their definitions are clarified and the data are transcribed exactly as recorded.

Definitions vary across datasets. The same event may appear in one dataset and be absent from another. This series does not erase such discrepancies but exposes them. Differences are not flaws in the data, but distinct choices made by researchers regarding what constitutes an event, and the points of divergence between a nation's historical narratives or judicial rulings and the data are likewise part of the record.

Part 1 Periodization — The Year 1760

The same benchmark year (1760) is applied to all countries. Although the timing of industrialization varies across countries, a shared benchmark is required to enable cross-national comparison. The period from 1760 to 1815 predates systematic datasets (the earliest COW civil war data begins in 1816). This interval is documented through research literature.

Part 3 State-Linking Rules

  1. Country codes were mapped to ISO3 using COW Country Codes (correlatesofwar.org) and the Institute's Country Name Table (vault data/ref).
  2. Historical states are linked to their modern successor states, preserving the original state name (e.g., Joseon → Republic of Korea; Federal/Democratic Republic of Germany → Germany; Yugoslavia → Serbia; North/South Yemen → Yemen; Ottoman Empire → Türkiye).
  3. Campaigns in disputed or non-sovereign territories (e.g., Tibet, West Papua, Western Sahara) are placed in the section of the current administering state according to the location recorded in the data, preserving the original place name and a 'Disputed Territory' marker. This is a geographical record, not a political judgment.
  4. COW Civil War is based on participating states. Cases of intervention in another country's civil war also appear in that country's section with an 'internationalized' designation.

Part 4 How to Read Differences Across Datasets

  1. Thresholds differ: COW uses a benchmark of 1,000 battle-related deaths per year, whereas UCDP uses 25 per year. The same conflict may appear only in UCDP.
  2. The definition of coup is narrow: Powell & Thyne counts only attempts to oust the sitting executive head. Events designated as 'military mutiny' or 'military rebellion' by national courts or academia may be omitted, and are therefore recorded alongside literature in the country sections.
  3. Data relying on media coverage inherit reporting imbalances: Mass Mobilization relies on major international press, and ACLED relies on reports and local partners. Lower counts in countries where press access is restricted may not imply lower event occurrences.
  4. Bias toward success: NAVCO's codebook notes that nonviolent campaigns suppressed early and not covered in media are omitted.
  5. Coverage start years: ACLED start dates vary by region. Blank entries prior to the start date represent unrecorded data.

Part 5 Structure of Country Volumes

  1. Basic Indicators — η and BSLI from the Country Analysis Series (linked) and this country's data holding status
  2. Pre-Industrial Revolution (~1759) — Narrative of uprisings and peasant revolts, chronological order
  3. 1760~1815 — Literature
  4. Modern Data (1816~) — Civil wars, armed conflicts, coups, regime overthrows, uprising campaigns, protests
  5. Modern Events Viewed Through Literature — Events not covered or recorded differently by data, comparison of figures between official announcements and independent investigations
  6. Related Studies — Articles and research reports
  7. Sources

Part 6 Axioms and Assessment Formulas — Original Text from Country Analysis Series

The Uprising Series does not construct new formulas. The framework for reading event records consists of Axioms A0–A12 and the UI uprising ignition condition from Country Analysis Series Vol. 1 (2026-10-07, canonical edition). Below is a verbatim reproduction of the relevant section of that book, and the original text is located in Country Analysis Series Vol. 1. Consistency is verified against the original text by a script (formula_check.py) at each publication (per-block SHA-256: axioms fd13589220da · elasticity 781f7557a834 · ui 2af33d12f4a7).

The country sections of this series merely record events and do not compute F(t) and SE(t) for each country. Assessment is the domain of the Country Analysis Series and UI engine reports.

1.3 Axiomatic System A0–A12

Λ¹² rests upon 13 axioms. Axioms are not starting points of deduction, but principles induced from accumulated observations; each axiom specifies rejection conditions in accordance with Popper's falsifiability principle. The two tables below reproduce the verbatim text of §3.2 of the consolidated paper.
