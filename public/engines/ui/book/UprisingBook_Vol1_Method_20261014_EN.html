Explanatory Notes

  1. This book is Volume 1 of the Series of Uprisings, serving as the foundation for the regional country sections beginning in Volume 2. The regional division of the country sections is identical to that of the Country Analysis Series (2026-10-07).
  2. The era is divided based on the year 1760 (Cheonghae decision, 2026-10-14). The year 1760 is commonly used as the beginning of the British Industrial Revolution. Uprisings, peasant revolts, and protests prior to that are recorded as narratives grounded in historical records and research literature; subsequent uprisings, protests, regime overthrows, civil wars, and coups are recorded via systematic data where available, and via research literature where data is absent.
  3. All figures in modern data were transcribed programmatically from primary source files. None of the figures were entered by hand. Primary source values are neither altered nor filtered (preserving empirical measurements). The dataset name and version are attached to each event.
  4. All narratives and literature citations include sources. Wikipedia, Namuwiki, and blogs are not used as sources; historical records, public databases, academic papers, judicial rulings, and reports from governments and memorial foundations are utilized. When official announcements diverge from independent investigations, both are presented side by side.
  5. Items absent from the data are recorded as "Unindexed," and unconfirmed items are recorded as "Unverified." A distinction is made between 0 and unindexed.

Prologue — What This Series Does

The Compendium of Uprisings gathers records of people rising up against power in each country. Because systematic event data does not exist prior to the Industrial Revolution, stories preserved in historical records — who rose up, why, how, and how it ended — are rendered into narrative. For the period following the Industrial Revolution, as datasets compiled by researchers according to defined criteria exist, their definitions are clarified and transcribed exactly as recorded by the data.

Definitions vary across datasets. The same event may appear in one dataset and be absent from another. This Series highlights rather than erases these discrepancies. Discrepancies do not denote data defects, but rather reflect distinct choices made by researchers regarding what counts as an event; points of friction between historical accounts, court rulings, and data in each country are also part of the record.

Part 1 Periodization — The Year 1760

The same baseline year (1760) is applied to all countries. Although the timing of industrialization varies across countries, a common benchmark is required to enable cross-national comparison. The 1760–1815 interval predates the emergence of systematic datasets (the earliest, COW Civil War data, begins in 1816). This interval is recorded using research literature.

Part 3 Country-Linking Rules

  1. Country codes were mapped to ISO3 using COW Country Code tables (correlatesofwar.org) and the Institute Country Name Table (vault data/ref).
  2. Historical states are linked to their current successor states, retaining the original state name (e.g., Joseon → Republic of Korea, Federal/Democratic Republic of Germany → Germany, Yugoslavia → Serbia, North/South Yemen → Yemen, Ottoman Empire → Türkiye).
  3. Campaigns in disputed or non-sovereign territories (e.g., Tibet, West Papua, Western Sahara) are placed in the section of the current administering state recorded by the data, retaining the original place name and a 'Disputed Territory' marker. This is a geographical notation and not a political judgment.
  4. COW Civil War is based on participating states. Instances of foreign intervention in another country's civil war also appear in that country's section with an 'internationalized' marker.

Part 4 How to Read Discrepancies Between Datasets

  1. Thresholds differ: COW requires 1,000 battle deaths per year, whereas UCDP requires 25 battle deaths per year. The same conflict may appear only in UCDP.
  2. Coup definitions are narrow: Powell & Thyne counts only attempts to unseat the sitting executive. Because events categorized by domestic courts or academia as 'military rebellions' may be excluded, they are presented alongside literature in the country sections.
  3. Reporting-dependent data inherits reporting disparities: Mass Mobilization relies on major international media, while ACLED relies on media and local partners. Low figures in countries with restricted press access may not indicate lower actual occurrence.
  4. Success bias: NAVCO notes in its codebook that nonviolent campaigns suppressed early and unmentioned in the press are omitted.
  5. Coverage start years: ACLED coverage begins in different years across regions. Gaps prior to coverage start dates indicate unindexed status.

Part 5 Structure of the Country Sections

  1. Basic Indicators — η and BSLI from the Country Analysis Series (linked) and data availability status for the country
  2. Pre-Industrial Revolution (~1759) — Narrative of uprisings and peasant revolts, in chronological order
  3. 1760~1815 — Literature
  4. Modern Data (1816~) — Civil wars, armed conflicts, coups, regime overthrows, uprising campaigns, protests
  5. Modern Events Through Literature — Events omitted or recorded differently by data, comparison of figures between official announcements and independent investigations
  6. Related Research — Academic papers and research reports
  7. Source

Part 6 Axioms and Assessment Formulas — Original Text from the Country Analysis Series

The Series of Uprisings introduces no new formulas. The framework for reading event records consists of Axioms A0–A12 and UI uprising ignition conditions from Volume 1 of the Country Analysis Series (2026-10-07, definitive edition). The following transcribes the relevant sections from that work verbatim, and the original text is located in Country Analysis Series Vol. 1. Concordance is verified programmatically (formula_check.py) against the original text upon each release (per-block SHA-256: axioms fd13589220da · elasticity 781f7557a834 · ui 2af33d12f4a7).

The country sections of this Series solely record events and do not compute F(t) or SE(t) on a per-country basis. Assessment remains within the purview of the Country Analysis Series and the UI engine reports.

1.3 Axiom System A0–A12

Λ¹² rests upon 13 axioms. Axioms are not points of departure for proof, but inductive principles derived from accumulated observations; in accordance with Popper's falsifiability criterion, each axiom explicitly specifies its conditions for rejection. The two tables below reproduce §3.2 of the combined paper verbatim.

AxiomNameDefinition
Axiom A0Independent ExistenceΛ¹² is the macro-state vector (⊥). BSLI is the external perturbation vector. The two axes measure independent dimensions.
Axiom A1Target InvarianceRegardless of which dimension reaches threshold, the final collapse target remains A2 (institutional trust) and A8 (governance).
Axiom A2Elasticity DualityA2 possesses elasticity in both vertical (restoration) and horizontal (checks and balances) directions simultaneously. Operates complementarily with A8.
Axiom A3Internal ConflictC_internal(A2, A8) --- When A8 attacks A2, internal conflict energy is generated.
Axiom A4Enforcement AttributionWhen enforcement power is entirely attributed to A8, the resilience of A2 is structurally weakened.

Axiom A6Neurobiological Constant λLayer −1 (mirror neurons, oxytocin). Automatically activated during in-person assemblies. λ is a species-specific constant.
Axiom A7Impedance MismatchΓ = (Z_L0 − Z_A8) / (Z_L0 + Z_A8). The impedance difference between institution and culture determines the reflection rate.

1.6 Institutional Elasticity and Internal Conflict

Formula 3.1 — Vulnerability Index
V_Λ(t) = 1 − [ Σᵢ wᵢ · Aᵢ(t) ] · (1 + γ · C_internal)
V_Λ(t)
Vulnerability index at time t (0 to 1, higher values indicate greater vulnerability)
wᵢ
Dimensional weights; theoretical parameter settings subject to empirical regression calibration
γ
Internal conflict amplification coefficient (0 to 2)
C_internal
Internal conflict energy; intensity with which A8 attacks A2
Axiom A3 — Internal Conflict Energy
C_internal = max(0, A8_exec − A2_trust) · δ_conflict
A8_exec
Governance enforcement power
A2_trust
Institutional Trust
δ_conflict
Conflict intensity coefficient
Formula 3.2 — Institutional Elasticity Coefficient
E_A2(t) = A2(t) · [ 1 − κ_L0 · (1 − A8_check(t)) ]
E_A2(t)
Institutional elasticity (0 to 1); brittle fracture initiates at E_A2 < 0.2 (Axiom A9)
κ_L0
Cultural-historical coefficient (Axiom A5); increases as democratization experience accumulates
A8_check(t)
Checks-and-balances operational intensity (effectiveness of the separation of powers)
Formula 3.3 — Impedance Reflection Rate
Γ = (Z_L0 − Z_A8) / (Z_L0 + Z_A8), Γ ∈ [−1, +1]
Z_L0
Social impedance of the cultural-historical layer (resistance strength of collective memory, traditions, and norms)
Z_A8
Impedance of incumbent governance (institutional coercive force)
Γ → +1
Culture is stronger than institutions; popular resistance capacity is high
Γ → −1
Institutions are stronger than culture. Short-term stability and long-term brittleness of oppressive regimes
Γ = 0
Balance between culture and institutions
