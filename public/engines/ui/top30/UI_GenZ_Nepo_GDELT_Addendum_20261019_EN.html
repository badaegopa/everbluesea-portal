Layer 1 Data — Before vs. After

Before(Supplementary Observation Table 3-1): Google News RSS queried by half-year intervals for publication dates within the interval; only articles with dates within the interval were counted. There was a limitation where recent articles mixed into past interval queries, leading to undercounting (in Sri Lanka's uprising, 1 of 17 articles in the preceding half-year fell within the interval).

After(This revised edition): GDELT GKG 2.1 original (English collection) was received as a daily sample of 8 time points (3-hour intervals) from 2021-09-01, aggregated at the article level. Country = country appearing in article location information. Cronyism = articles with corruption (CORRUPTION·WB_832_ANTI_CORRUPTION) in topic codes and expressions of cronyism, high-ranking officials' children, or dynasties in titles or URLs, displayed per 10,000 articles (parentheses = sample count). Because it is by month, the period immediately preceding the uprising (1–3 months prior, trigger phase) and 4–6 months prior (structure phase) can be distinguished.

Layer 3 Pattern — What Both Before and After Point to

In the previous edition (Supplementary Observation Table (Pilot) 3-1), due to Google News's undercounting, it was not possible to determine whether reports of cronyism and children preceded uprisings. Recalculated using GDELT original data, only one country, Indonesia (sample: 1 article), saw an increase in reports of cronyism and children's corruption immediately before the uprising. Conversely, these reports concentrate in the period when the uprising began — Nepal (65.7 per 10,000 vs. 1.1 in normal times, sample: 19 articles), Sri Lanka (6.9 vs. 1.4), Bangladesh (5.2 vs. 1.8), and Peru (4.8 vs. 0.5). The proportion of corruption reports is also higher in the trigger phase than in normal times in no country (0.45–0.99x).

The conclusions of the previous and subsequent editions point in the same direction — in international English-language reporting, issues of leaders' children and cronyism do not appear as signals preceding uprisings but emerge afterward as narratives explaining the uprising.

Layer 4 Perspective Shift — Where Does the Trigger Ignite First?

It was reported that Nepal's 'nepo kids' spread on local social media before international media (source in the case table of supplementary observation). If international English-language reporting only covered this story after the uprising, to capture this element earlier, the measurement medium must change — local-language media and social media. This revised edition is a record confirming that limitation.

Layer 5 Limitations

  1. GDELT uses only English collections — local-language reports are excluded. It is an 8-time-point sample out of 96 daily points.
  2. The expression dictionary (cronyism, children, dynasties) uses English regular expressions, so there are missed expressions and misclassified articles.
  3. Generalization to the 11 case countries is not made.
