1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

President Tshisekedi (second term ending in 2028) stated on May 6, 2026, that he would accept a third term if the people wanted it, and legislation setting conditions for a referendum passed the National Assembly on June 9 and the Senate on June 15; the Constitutional Court issued a conditional ruling of constitutionality in late July (dates estimated). The opposition coalition C64 and the Catholic Episcopal Conference of Congo (CENCO) oppose the constitutional amendment, and protests on June 12 and September 15 were dispersed with tear gas and arrests. On August 27, the president announced a three-month national dialogue excluding armed factions. In the east, AFC/M23 has controlled Goma and Bukavu since early 2025, and former President Joseph Kabila formed the Front for the Restoration of the Nation (FRN) alongside M23 leadership on October 12, 2026. Meanwhile, the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola spread, reaching 9,277 confirmed cases and 4,478 deaths according to ECDC tallies as of 2026-10-14.

President (Félix Tshisekedi) (2026-05-06) — At a press conference, he stated that he had never demanded a third term but would accept it if the people wanted it; he said that without ending the war, the 2028 election could not be held and that 'elections cannot be held when part of the territory is occupied.' He noted that changing the term limit would require a constitutional amendment approved by referendum. [Source]

The bill allows changes to term provisions through a referendum if there is a 'serious functional breakdown' of state institutions. The Constitutional Court issued a ruling of constitutionality in late July. [Source]

President (National Dialogue Address) (2026-08-27) — Announced a 'national dialogue for peace, unity, and national reconstruction' lasting up to three months. The structure involves consultations with the 26 provinces followed by a national conference in Kinshasa and adoption of a 'national pact.' Actors who fought against the constitutional order or occupied territory by force (practically AFC/M23) are excluded, and general amnesties for war crimes, etc., are excluded. It does not propose constitutional amendment as an objective. [Source]

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

2015-01 (Protest against Electoral Law Amendment)

Protests broke out from January 19 after the National Assembly passed an electoral law amendment requiring a census before the next election. HRW confirmed at least 36 deaths in Kinshasa (at least 21 killed by security forces) and 4 deaths in Goma, while the police chief stated 12. On January 23, the Senate adopted an amended version of the bill decoupling elections from the census.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Attempt to alter the schedule by changing institutions ahead of the end of a term → protests centered on the capital and lethal crackdowns → divergence between official and independent death tolls → partial retreat of certain provisions.

  1. Human Rights Watch, DR Congo: Deadly Crackdown on Protests, 2015-01-24 · Human rights NGO · Orientation: International human rights NGO — https://www.hrw.org/news/2015/01/24/dr-congo-deadly-crackdown-protests

2016-12 (End of Kabila's Term)

Kabila's term expired at midnight on December 19 but he remained in office until the election (court ruling). On December 20, at least 26 people were killed and dozens arrested in protests, with most social media blocked.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Judicial decision to extend tenure when constitutional term expiration coincides with election delay → mass protests → lethal crackdowns and communications shutdowns.

  1. Al Jazeera, More than two dozen people killed in DRC protests, 2016-12-21 · Independent media · Orientation: Qatari state-funded international broadcaster — https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2016/12/21/more-than-two-dozen-people-killed-in-drc-protests

2018-12 (Presidential Election)

Englebert analyzes that although most credible tallies pointed to Faryulu's victory, Tshisekedi took office under a power-sharing agreement and foreign powers accepted this.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Disputes over the legitimacy of election results carry over into subsequent governments' legitimacy debates and serve as grounds for opposition mobilization.

  1. Englebert, Journal of Democracy 30(3), 2019 · Academic · Orientation: NED-published academic journal — https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/aspirations-and-realities-in-africa-the-drcs-electoral-sideshow/

2012 / 2025 (M23 Seizure of Goma)

M23 originated from a military mutiny in 2012 (ICCT) and seized Goma again in January 2025. Congo, the UN, and the West allege Rwandan support, which Rwanda denies.

Recurring Structure (Observation): The eastern armed uprising and allegations of neighboring-country intervention recur, with a political crisis in the capital (term dispute) overlapping with the eastern front at the same time.

  1. ICCT, Baudon, M23: A Forgotten War and an Overshadowed Ceasefire, 2024-08-09 · Academic · Orientation: Dutch counterterrorism think tank — https://icct.nl/publication/m23-forgotten-war-and-overshadowed-ceasefire
