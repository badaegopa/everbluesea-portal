1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

The Democratic People's Republic of Korea amended its constitution in February 2026 after the 9th Congress of the Workers' Party of Korea and the March 15 election of the Supreme People's Assembly (all 687 seats won by single candidates), followed by a Supreme People's Assembly plenary session on March 22–23; Chairman Kim Jong Un declared an 'irreversible' consolidation of the country's status as a nuclear power and characterized South Korea as the 'most hostile state.' Domestic independent media and records of public protests are virtually nonexistent (Hermes collection: ACLED protest and unrest events, total 6 for 2018–2022), while foreign outlets report an increase in executions on account of the dissemination of foreign video content (UN Human Rights Office, 2025-09; Daily NK, 2026-06). Externally, the dispatch of approximately 11,000 troops to Kursk, Russia (National Intelligence Service, 2026-02), the opening of a road bridge across North–Russia in September, and the inauguration of the New Yalu River Bridge on October 15 expanded connections with both China and Russia; US President Trump mentioned '57 nuclear weapons' in August and that 'Kim Jong Un is my friend' in September.

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Mid-1990s (March of Tribulations)

A great famine occurred due to the collapse of the food rationing system, with an estimated 1 million deaths (Haggard & Noland). Thereafter, marketization from below spread.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Collapse of the rationing system → Response through individual survival strategies (jangmadangs, movement) rather than collective resistance → The state subsequently partially tolerated the market post hoc.

  1. Columbia University Press, Haggard & Noland, Famine in North Korea, 2007 · Academic · Affiliation: US university press — https://cup.columbia.edu/book/famine-in-north-korea/9780231140003

2009–2010 (Currency Reform)

Reports of savings losses, panic, and unrest followed the currency reform with a 150,000-won exchange limit and a 100:1 rate; merchants were hit, and food shortages worsened. In March 2010, reports emerged of the execution of former Party Planning and Finance Department Director Pak Nam Gi (unconfirmed by the South Korean government).

Recurring Structure (Observation): Attempt to reassert state control over the market → Reports of livelihood shocks and discontent → Concluded with punishment of those responsible (based on reporting).

  1. Al Jazeera, N Korea 'executes' top official, 2010-03-18 · Foreign media · Affiliation: Qatari state-funded international broadcaster — https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2010/3/18/n-korea-executes-top-official

2015–2026 (Legalization of Information Control)

Six laws allowing the death penalty were enacted since 2015; testimonies of increased executions for disseminating foreign content have emerged since 2020 (OHCHR). Reports of executions in Haeju in 2026 (Daily NK).

Recurring Structure (Observation): Increased inflow of external information → Escalation of punitive severity and deterrence through public and covert punishments → Followed by an absence of records of public collective action.

  1. UN News, DPR Korea report, 2025-09-12 · International organization · Affiliation: UN official news — https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/09/1165837
  2. Daily NK, Haeju execution, 2026-06-12 · Independent media · Affiliation: Based on defectors and internal sources; anti-government — https://www.dailynk.com/english/north-korea-public-execution-south-korean-videos/

2024–2026 (Two Enemy States Doctrine)

In 2024, the abandonment of peaceful unification was declared; in 2026, at the party congress and Supreme People's Assembly, South Korea was designated as the most hostile state and the constitution was amended.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A structure in which the institutionalization of external hostility and discourse on internal cohesion are simultaneously strengthened. Byman & Lind analyze that ideology, nationalism, and physical force deter revolution, while elite capture and coup prevention deter internal rebellion.
