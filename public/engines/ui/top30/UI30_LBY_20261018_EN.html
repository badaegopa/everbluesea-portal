1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

Libya is divided between the Government of National Unity (GNU) in Tripoli, led by Prime Minister Abdulhamid Dbeibeh, and a government in the east under Prime Minister Osama Hamad with influence from Commander Khalifa Haftar; elections have been indefinitely postponed since 2021. In late July 2026, amid extreme heat, power outages of over 14 hours per day in western Libya—including Tripoli, Zawiya, and Misrata—triggered protests and 'civil disobedience', with protesters occupying the Mellitah gas complex before it was retaken by the Ministry of Defense. In August, there were six drone strikes on energy facilities in Zawiya over five days, and on August 30 a UNSMIL-mediated 'small conference' yielded an agreement to reconstitute the HNEC board and hold elections within 24 months. A teachers' wage protest began on September 13 and escalated into a nationwide indefinite strike, delaying the start of school for approximately 1.6 million public school students.

2-3. Geopolitical Status

UN Security Council · UNSMIL (2025-10-31 / 2026-06-07) — Resolution 2796 (2025-10-31) extended the UNSMIL mandate for an additional 12 months (until 2026-10-31). The three-track roadmap in August 2025 (electoral framework, unified government, structured dialogue). The structured dialogue concluded on 2026-06-07 with approximately 600 recommendations (about 120 direct participants, more than 13,000 respondents via surveys, etc.). [Source]

United States (Special Envoy Marshall Bolten) (2026-06~07) — Reported proposal: retaining Prime Minister Dbeibah, making Khalifa Haftar a president with executive powers, followed by a transitional period and elections. Supporters argue it protects stability and oil production; critics claim it is an elite deal circumventing UN procedures. The United States described its efforts as complementary to UNSMIL. In late June, Khalifa Haftar met Secretary of State Pompeo. [Source]

Russia and China (2026) — China emphasized the centrality of UN mediation and the 'Libya-led, Libya-owned' principle. Russia viewed structured dialogue as an auxiliary means and preferred the 'three-party format' of the HoR, Presidential Council, and GNU, opposing externally imposed outcomes. Both countries demanded protection of frozen assets. [Source]

Joint Statement by 10 European and U.S. Countries (2026-08-26) — France, Italy, Germany, Greece, Malta, the Netherlands, Spain, Switzerland, the United Kingdom, and the United States welcomed the August 20 agreement in Tunis and called for final signing. [Source]
