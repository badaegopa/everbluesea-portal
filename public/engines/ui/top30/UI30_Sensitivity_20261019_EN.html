Layer 1 Data — Interval G and Lever Formulas

Uprising Index interval G = m·(−a) − (A2 + A8), m = A2·A8 (same as the formula for selecting the top 30 countries). Partial derivatives: ∂G/∂A2 = A8·(−a) − 1, ∂G/∂A8 = A2·(−a) − 1, ∂G/∂a = −A2·A8. In 196 countries, |m·a| ≤ 0.02, so if A2 or A8 rises by 0.1, G drops almost unchanged by 0.1, and the effect of BSLI acceleration a is two orders of magnitude smaller. In this approximation, the lever that moves the risk gap is in fact Institutional Trust (A2) and Governance (A8).

A2·A8 were recalculated by calling the Λ¹² v2 calculation function directly, and both the canonical CSV and all 196 countries have a difference of 0. a is the second-order central difference of BSLI 2022, 2023, and 2024. For the top 30 countries, these three years were either missing or carried forward in the canonical BSLI auxiliary time series v1.2, so a=0 at the time of selection. In this pilot, gaps were supplemented — the same BSLI v7.0 4-term formula and the same fixed normalization criteria as the canonical version were used, along with the mean, median, and poverty rate of World Bank PIP formula-linked estimates (extrapolated/interpolated) and the 10th percentile share of the most recent survey (PIP extrapolation under the same distribution-neutral assumption). 25 countries were supplemented, with the survey year differing from the canonical value by up to 0.02. Somalia, Afghanistan, Eritrea, Libya, and North Korea could not be supplemented because they lack PIP surveys. The supplementary interval G* is the value obtained by inserting the supplemented a into the same formula, without changing the selection of the top 30 countries. For countries missing one indicator (e.g., South Sudan without the Corruption Perceptions Index), the average of the remaining indicators becomes the dimension score, so the lever size changes.

Layer 2 Interests — How much do each source indicator move?

A2 = Mean (Corruption Perceptions Index, Control of Corruption), A8 = Mean (Government Effectiveness, Regulatory Quality, Rule of Law, Voice and Accountability, Political Stability and Absence of Violence, Control of Corruption) — each indicator is normalized across the entire country range using Min-Max normalization. Therefore, even a 0.1 improvement in the same normalization affects G differently for each indicator: Control of Corruptionenters both A2 and A8, making it the largest, followed by the Corruption Perceptions Index, and the remaining five indicators of A8 are at one-third of that level.

Layer 3 Patterns — Lever Representations by Country

For each country, for each indicator, the following were calculated: ① change in G with a 0.1 normalization improvement, ② change in G when raised to the median of countries in the same region (Country Analysis Series region), and ③ the amount of normalization improvement required to bring the gap down to that of the 31st-ranked country using that indicator alone. If ③ exceeds 1, it means that the indicator alone falls outside the range.

Layer 4 Perspective Shift — Leverage, Not an Alarm

This table does not predict when an uprising will occur. From the current position, it shows numerically how much the risk interval narrows when each source indicator moves. Calculations were done only through partial differentiation of the canonical formula and reproduction of the canonical output function; no new formulas or weights were created.

Layer 5 Knowledge Frame and Limitations

  1. G is a selection formula using the upper bound SE⁺ approximation of UI canonical SE(t) (approved by Cheonghae on 2026-10-17). When κ_L0·A8_check canonical values are available, the partial differentiation coefficients will change.
  2. Min-Max normalization changes its range depending on other countries' values — a calculation assuming improvement in only one country.
  3. Source indicators (WGI and CPI) are expert perception-based indicators. An indicator rising is not the same as an actual institution changing.
  4. Prior research: Goldstone et al. (2010, AJPS) showed that political institutions are the strongest predictor of instability — this lever collects institutional indicators in the same direction (Bolt Research Briefing prior research comparison document).
