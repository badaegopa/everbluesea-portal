1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

The Taliban (self-styled 'Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan') has been governing for five years since seizing Kabul on August 15, 2021, with Russia first recognizing the government in July 2025. The United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) assessed in its 2026-09-16 Security Council report that the authorities were not facing 'serious armed or political threats,' while reporting that risks stemming from government policies — including clashes in Badakhshan, arrests and protests over a dress code crackdown in Herat (2026-06-09, at least one death confirmed) — were accumulating. The border clashes with Pakistan that began in October 2025 escalated into renewed airstrikes following the bombing of a Kabul rehabilitation center on March 16, 2026 (over 400 deaths according to Afghan authorities), and a suicide attack in Pakistan on September 18 (31 deaths), and the Moscow talks on October 5 concluded without a joint statement. Approximately 1.2 million people returned from Iran and Pakistan in 2026 (cumulative 6.5 million since 2023), while only 30% of the 2026 humanitarian response plan was funded (UNAMA).

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

2022–2026 (Badakhshan after the opium ban)

Since the declaration of a ban on drugs in 2022, clashes and protests over eradication efforts in Badakhshan have recurred in May 2024 (Kash, Daraim, Arg), 2025 (Kash, Jurm), and May 2026 (Arg). Dozens of arrests have been reported since 2025 (claims by local and human rights groups).

Recurring structure (observation): Central ban enforcement → resistance in mountainous rural areas with insufficient alternative livelihoods → troop reinforcements and dispatch of negotiation delegations. The same pattern recurs annually during the planting and eradication seasons in the same region. Mansfield describes how previous bans weakened livelihoods in some areas and gave rise to rural rebellions.

2021 (Collapse of the Republic)

Following the announcement of US and NATO withdrawal, the Taliban seized Kabul almost without resistance on August 15, 2021.

Recurring structure (observation): A centralised regime dependent on external patronage disintegrated rapidly in the phase of patron withdrawal. Murtazashvili attributes the cause to the Kabul-centric institutional design.

2025-10 ~ 2026-10 (Border Conflict with Pakistan)

Large-scale attacks within Pakistan → Pakistani airstrikes on Afghan territory → Taliban retaliation and claims of sovereignty violation → mediation by third countries (China, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Türkiye, Russia) and temporary ceasefires → resumption of attacks recurring several times over a year.

Recurring structure (observation): A cycle of mediation → standoff → renewed conflict recurs while mutual claims over patronage (Pakistan: claim; Taliban: denial) remain unresolved.

2026 (Fissure with Northeastern Region Elites)

A Taliban regulation on gold mining led to a rift with Tajik regional commander Zama Khan Patheh, temporary suspension of mining, and resident protests, along with an increase in anti-Taliban armed activities (4 incidents in Badakhshan, June–December 2025 → 22 incidents, January–July 2026).

Recurring structure (observation): Expansion of central control over resources → reduction in the revenue base of local commanders and residents → armed resistance at the local level. A structure that appears as localized conflicts rather than a nationwide movement (ACLED, UNAMA refers to Badakhshan as a 'consequence of exclusion' case).
