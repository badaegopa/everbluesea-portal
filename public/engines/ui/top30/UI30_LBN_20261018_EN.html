1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

On March 2, 2026, Hezbollah attacked Israel, drawing Lebanon into the US-Israel-Iran war, and by late August, Lebanese authorities had recorded over 4,300 fatalities. The parliament extended its term by two years on March 9, postponing the May general election to 2028, and on June 26 in Washington, a 14-point framework agreement between Lebanon and Israel mediated by the United States was signed; Hezbollah declared it 'invalid,' and its supporters protested in Beirut. The World Bank projected that real GDP would contract by 6.4% in 2026 and inflation would reach 17.5%, and following a surge in fuel prices in September, public servants went on strike and the transport unions called a nationwide general strike for September 30. On October 12, the Constitutional Council rejected a challenge to the general amnesty law, ending a 30-day hunger strike by inmates.

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

2013–2014, 2017, 2026 (postponement of general elections)

The parliament extended its term twice in 2013–2014 citing divisions and unrest stemming from the Syrian Civil War, in 2017 over a dispute over the election law, and again in March 2026 for another two years citing war.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A structure recurs where parliamentary elections are postponed by parliamentary vote during periods of external war and institutional deadlock.

2006-08 (Resolution 1701)

Security Council Resolution 1701 adopted to end the 34-day war: cessation of hostilities, withdrawal of Israeli forces, simultaneous deployment of Lebanese armed forces and UNIFIL in the south, and stipulation that no armed force other than the state and UNIFIL would be present between the Blue Line and the Litani River.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Each time a war ends, 'the monopoly of state force in the south' enters the agreed wording, and its implementation remains an issue in the next phase (the 2026 French and Saudi statements also demanded full implementation of 1701).

2019-10 (October Revolution/Tishrin)

Nationwide protests erupted over reports of a WhatsApp tax plan; on October 29, the 13th day, Prime Minister Hariri announced his resignation. On the same day, Hezbollah supporters attacked a protest camp in Beirut's suburbs and burned tents (the first large-scale violence in an otherwise peaceful movement).

Recurring Structure (Observation): Fiscal burden measure → cross-sectarian popular protests → cabinet resignation, while counter-mobilization by sectarian organizations' base reduces protest space.

2020-08 ~ 2021-10 (Port Explosion and Tyre Clashes)

The Beirut port explosion on August 4, 2020 killed over 200 people. On October 14, 2021, Hezbollah and Amal held a rally demanding the dismissal of investigating judge Bitar; gunfire broke out in Tyre, an old frontline of the civil war, killing 6 and wounding about 30 (Minister of Interior and Red Cross).

Recurring Structure (Observation): The process of holding those responsible accountable (judicial investigation) clashes with collective mobilization by sectarian parties, and escalates into armed conflict along former civil war fault lines. The military acted as a buffer by setting up checkpoints.

Immediately after the signing of the framework agreement, Hezbollah supporters held a motorcycle parade and blocked airport roads; the military dispersed them on the same day with a statement that 'any harm to civil peace will not be tolerated.' A Hezbollah MP referred to an 'internal war.'

Recurring Structure (Observation): External agreement → short-term street mobilization by sectarian-based base → neutral intervention by the military to maintain order, as in 2019 and 2021, with the military acting as a buffer.
