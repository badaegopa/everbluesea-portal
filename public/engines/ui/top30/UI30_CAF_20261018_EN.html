1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

Following the 2023 constitutional amendment that abolished term limits for the presidency, President Touadéra was re-elected to a third term in the December 28, 2025 general election (confirmed by the Constitutional Court at 77.9%, turnout 52.4%), and the main opposition coalition BRDC rejected the election while second-place Dologrelle alleged fraud. Following 'dead city' protests against fuel price increases on October 12, police arrested four civil society leaders on October 15; civil society and the opposition are demanding their release. The UN peacekeeping mission MINUSCA was extended until November 15, 2026 (maximum troop ceiling 14,046, US abstention), and Russia has demanded that Wagner be replaced with an African Corps under the Ministry of Defense. The United States has been transferring third-country asylum seekers to Cameroon from June 2026 onward.

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

2013–2014 (Seleka and Anti-Balaka conflict)

Seleka ousted President Bozizé and the Bozizé faction armed Anti-Balaka, resulting in thousands of deaths. French military intervention under UN mandate in 2014. Relative stability since the 2019 peace agreement.

Recurring structure (observation): Armed coalition seizes capital → mobilization of rival militias → restoration of balance through external military intervention. Lombard analyzes that conflict has long been 'useful' in Central African politics and that international intervention has exacerbated it.
