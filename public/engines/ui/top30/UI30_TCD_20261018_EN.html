1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

Mahamat Idriss Déby was proclaimed interim president by the military following the death of his father Idriss Déby in April 2021 and elected in May 2024 with 61% confirmed by the Constitutional Council. In October 3, 2025, parliament adopted a constitutional amendment extending the presidential term from five to seven years and removing term limits, 236 to 0. Opposition leader Shukri Mahamat, former Prime Minister, arrested in May 2025 and sentenced to 20 years' imprisonment, received a presidential pardon on August 14, 2026, and was released the following day, but the conviction and disqualification from office remained. In the east, at least 42 people died in water disputes in April 2026, and Sudan's civil war has spilled over the border, including reported airstrikes on Ennedi-Ouest on August 20; Chad hosts 1.4 million refugees (more than 900,000 from Sudan).

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

2021-04 (Death of Idriss Déby and Military Succession)

When Idriss Déby Itno died in combat with rebels, military generals proclaimed his son Mahamat as transitional president. They promised an 18-month transition but extended it for two years; a new constitution was adopted through a referendum in December 2023, and he was elected in the May 2024 presidential election.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A pattern is observed where the leader's death → internal military succession → extension of the transitional period → leads to formalization of power through elections.

2022-10-20 (Protests against the extension of the transition)

Security forces fired live ammunition during protests against a two-year extension of the transition, and hundreds were transferred to the Koro Toro prison 600 km away. Death tolls differ: 50 officially, 128 by CNDH, and 218 by LTDH and OMCT.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A pattern is observed where extension of the power transfer schedule → urban protests → live ammunition crackdown and long-distance mass transfers → followed by a decrease in protest events (ACLED: 113 events in 2022 → 24 events in 2025).

  1. HRW, Koro Toro report, 2024-08-06 · Human Rights Organization · Affiliation: International human rights NGO — https://www.hrw.org/report/2024/08/06/worse-hell/death-and-torture-chads-koro-toro-prison

2024–2026 (Arrest and Pardon of Major Rivals)

Masra was appointed Prime Minister in January 2024, resigned after the presidential election, arrested in May 2025 for a 20-year sentence, and pardoned in August 2026. The conviction and restriction on running for office were maintained after the pardon.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A pattern is observed where rivals are brought into the system → competition → criminal punishment → pardon (conviction retained), moving rivals in and out of the system.

  1. HRW, Chad: Political Opponents Freed, 2026-08-21 · Human Rights Organization · Affiliation: International human rights NGO — https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/08/21/chad-political-opponents-freed

1975–76, 2024–26 (Breakdown and Restoration of Military Relations with France)

The military cooperation between Chad and France, which began in the mid-1960s, was severed from September 1975 to March 1976 before being restored; it was terminated in November 2024 and signals of a resumption have emerged since March 2026.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A pattern is observed where the country breaks ties with an external military patron while asserting sovereignty, and restores them when security pressures intensify, forming a back-and-forth structure.
