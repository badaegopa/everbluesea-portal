1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

Amid the ongoing civil war following the February 2021 coup, the military regime held a three-stage general election from December 28, 2025 to January 25, 2026 (excluding at least 65 townships from voting, NLD dissolved), and the military-backed USDP won approximately 90% of contested lower house seats (official results). Min Aung Hlaing transferred his position as Commander-in-Chief to Ye Win Oo on March 30 and was inaugurated as president in a pro-military parliament on April 10, and on April 24 he declared 90 days of military control over 60 townships in nine states and regions (including civilian trials by military courts). State television MRTV announced on April 30 that Aung San Suu Kyi had been transferred to house arrest at a designated residence, but her location was not disclosed. The UN recorded more than 2,592 airstrikes between June 2025 and May 2026 in an August 2026 report, and on September 28 local aid workers and the Arakan Army reported that 50 people were killed in an airstrike on Myawaddy Town, Rakhine State.

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

March–September 1988

Nationwide general strike and simultaneous protests on August 8, approximately 1 million people gathered at Shwedagon Pagoda on August 26, coup on September 18 establishing SLORC, and real gunfire from September 18–19 killing thousands.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A structure in which mass protests over economic and political grievances spread nationwide, the military then seized direct power and suppressed them with mass shootings and arrests, and many activists fled to neighboring countries.

  1. HRW — 1988 Massacre (2013) — https://www.hrw.org/news/2013/08/06/burma-justice-1988-massacres

August–October 2007

Protests began on August 15 over the abolition of fuel and gas subsidies (gas prices rose up to 500%), monks joined, and by September 24 there were approximately 150,000 in Yangon; a curfew was imposed on September 25, followed by shootings and raids on monasteries on September 26–27. State media announced the detention of 2,836 people.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A sequence similar to that of 1988 — livelihood shocks (price increases) trigger protests → religious and student groups join, spreading the protest → the protest ends with curfews, shootings, and mass detentions.

  1. HRW — Report on the Suppression of the 2007 Protests — https://www.hrw.org/report/2007/12/06/crackdown/repression-2007-popular-protests-burma

February 2021–present

Following the coup, nonviolent protests and the Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM) shifted to armed resistance by the People's Defense Force (PDF) after bloody crackdowns, with desertion by soldiers organized as a resistance strategy. ACLED protests + riots declined from 7,780 in 2021 to 704 in 2025, while political violence continued at 650–1,100 events per month.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A structure in which street protests decline after crackdowns and shift toward armed clashes — this time combining with ethnic armed organizations to turn into a protracted civil war.

July 2025–July 2026

More than 200 people were prosecuted under the law prohibiting election obstruction (maximum death penalty) after its enactment, including a 37-year prison sentence for a participant in the Mandalay flash protests.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A pattern of introducing an institutional procedure (elections) alongside a separate criminal law to block protests against that procedure — similar to the 'managed transition' of military rule since the 1990s.
