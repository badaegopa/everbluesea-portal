1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

President Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo has been in power since the 1979 coup, and Vice President is his son Teodorín Nguema Obiang Mangue. Following protests by residents of Anobón Island in July 2024, more than 30 people were arrested and internet and mobile communications on the island were cut off (AP also reported continued disruption in September 2025), and from November 2025 onward, 53 third-country nationals removed by the United States (tracked through July 2026) were transferred to Malabo. In January 2026 a decree was promulgated moving the capital to the mainland city of Ciudad de la Paz, and in September 2026 Jeune Afrique reported that a coup had been thwarted according to Russian military intelligence (the operation began in late June), but the vice president categorically denied this. In mid-October 2026, the government imposed fines on two distributors following the second fuel supply crisis in four months.

1-5. Government Announcements

President Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo (2026-01-02) — By presidential decree, Ciudad de la Paz was designated the new political capital and state institutions were ordered to relocate from Malabo within one year. The government cited unruly urban growth in Malabo and Bata and regional imbalances as reasons. [Source]

Vice President Teodorín Nguema Obiang Mangue · Government Statement (2026-09-05 (estimated)) — Denying reports by Jeune Afrique (September 4), the vice president stated that there had been no coup attempt, no foreign intelligence alerts, and no seizure of weapons at Bata port; he declared that 'the state is tranquil and institutions are functioning normally.' The statement demanded a retraction and reserved the right to legal action. [Source]

Fuel Crisis Meeting chaired by Vice President (Government) (2026-10-15) — Fines were imposed on fuel distributors Total GE and Tradex, described as 'not a temporary measure but a fundamental change.' The expansion of Gepetrol Servicios reserves and the construction of a new state refinery were announced. [Source]

Minister of Foreign Affairs Simón Oyono Esono Angue (2026-07-28) — Signed a joint agreement with Gabon to implement the ICJ ruling at the African Union headquarters in Addis Ababa, noting that the island dispute had been 'finally resolved.' [Source]

2-2. Neighboring Countries' Reactions

Gabon (2026-07-28) — One year after the ICJ ruled that sovereignty over Eno Niefi, Cocotie, and Conaga Island belonged to Equatorial Guinea (based on the 1900 France–Spain treaty, rejecting the 1974 Batanga Agreement), an implementation joint commitment agreement was signed at AU headquarters. The Gabonese side (signed by Constitutional Court President Diédonné Awa Ondo) characterized it as 'the beginning of a new era of cooperation.' Maritime boundary negotiations continue. [Source]

Liberia (2026-08-21) — Reuters reported that 5 of 20 third-country repatriates originally destined for Liberia (3 from Cuba, 1 from Brazil, and 1 from Cameroon) refused to board the flight and were instead transferred to Malabo. Gabon announced it had agreed to accept up to 1,200 individuals, and the Ministry of Information did not respond to requests for comment. [Source]

Cameroon (2017-11-22) — Business in Cameroon reported that Equatorial Guinea closed the Kie-Ossi border with Gabon about a month after implementing a 90-day visa-free regime for CEMAC citizens, and Malabo authorities did not explain the reason. Subsequent border developments were not verified from original sources in this investigation. [Source]

2-3. Geopolitical Status

Russia (African Corps) (2026-09-04) — According to reports by Fides and Jeune Afrique, since 2024 approximately 100–300 Russian Ministry of Defense-linked African Corps instructors and troops have been stationed in Malabo and Batá training the presidential security forces. Reports emerged that the government had thwarted a coup using information from this corps, but the vice president denied receiving any foreign intelligence alerts. [Source]

United States (Third-Country Repatriation Agreement) (2026-09-08) — 53 people were deported to Equatorial Guinea between November 2025 and July 2026 (tracked by Refugees International and Human Rights First). They originated from 11 African countries as well as Georgia, Jamaica, Brazil, and Cuba. The US Department of Homeland Security characterized the deportations as to a 'safe third country,' while the Equatoguinean government did not disclose the terms of the agreement. [Source]

United States·China (2026-09-04) — Fides reports that the Trump administration is moving closer to the Obiang family to prevent China's first Atlantic naval base construction. In December 2021, the WSJ reported that US intelligence authorities had stated that China wanted a permanent military presence in the port of Bata. [Source]

France·United Kingdom (Vice Presidency-related judicial and sanctions) (2025-09-14) — According to AP, the Vice President was convicted in France of money laundering and embezzlement and is subject to UK sanctions; the ICJ ruled that Equatorial Guinea had failed to demonstrate a 'plausible right to restitution' in its demand for return of the Paris mansion. The Trump administration is considering easing corruption sanctions against the Vice President. [Source]

Holy See (2026-04-22) — Pope Leo XIV visited Equatorial Guinea at the end of his April 2026 tour of four African countries, addressing a crowd of approximately 600 inmates at Bata Prison on the relationship between justice and human dignity, and referring to 'a wider space for freedom' in Mass at Mongomo (attended by Obiang). NGOs issued an open letter requesting that the treatment of deportees be addressed. [Source]

African Union·ECCAS (2026-10-17) — Gabon and Equatorial Guinea signed an agreement to implement the ICJ ruling under the chairmanship of AU Executive Commission Chair Mahamat Ali Yousouf (2026-07-28). Obiang referred in a message marking the 43rd anniversary of ECCAS's founding (published 2026-10-17) to 'the turbulence of war and political challenges' in the region. [Source]

UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention (WGAD) (2025-06) — In June 2025, it issued an opinion finding the arrest of 37 people in Annobón to be arbitrary detention; UNPO subsequently reported that the 37 had been released. [Source]
