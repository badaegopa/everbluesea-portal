1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

In the October 12, 2025 presidential election, the Constitutional Council confirmed Paul Biya's (then 92, in power since 1982) eighth term with 53.66% to Issa Tchiroma Bakary's 35.19% on October 27, while Tchiroma claimed victory with 54.8%. Before and after the announcement of results, protests left 16 deaths (as of 2025-12 according to the government) and 48 (according to a UN source on 2025-11-04, Reuters); opposition figure Anissé Etoundi died in detention on December 1 after 38 days, and Tchiroma has been residing in The Gambia since November 7. On April 4, 2026, the joint parliamentary session passed a constitutional amendment creating the vice presidency appointed by the president with automatic succession by a vote of 200 to 18, but no vice president was appointed even after Biya returned from Switzerland on June 7–August 20, having spent 73 days there. Crisis Group tallies over 7,000 conflict deaths in the Anglophone (Northwest and Southwest) regions (2026-04), while in the far north, Boko Haram-linked attacks forced more than 17,000 people to flee the Waza area in September 2026, and protests against rising femicide took place in Douala and Yaoundé in October.

Chairman Clément Atangana described the electoral process as 'peaceful' and criticized the opposition for announcing the results ahead of time. [Source]

Minister of Territorial Administration (Paul Atanga Nji) · President (X posts) (2025-10-28 / 2025-11-13–14) — Minister Atanga Nji warned that Tchiroma would be dealt with on charges of inciting post-election violence and the incoming president emphasized that only the Constitutional Council could declare victory. Biya posted on X on November 13 and 14 that he could not handle the post-election crisis and that maintaining order was his responsibility. Government (Minister of Posts and Telecommunications René Emmanuel Sadi) (2025-12-01) — Expressed regret over the death of opposition figure Anissé Etoundi (MANIDEM president, aged 74) while in detention. The government stated that a military doctor had treated him alongside his personal physician, and President Biya ordered an investigation into the circumstances of his death. Government tallies put post-election protest deaths at 16. [Source]

Joint Parliamentary Session (National Assembly · Senate) (2026-04-04) — Passed a constitutional amendment creating the vice presidency by 200 in favor, 18 against, and 4 abstentions. The vice president would be appointed and dismissed by the president, automatically succeed upon the president's death, resignation, or incapacity to serve out the remainder of the term, and be barred from proposing a constitutional amendment or running for office in the next election upon succession. The government presented it as a safeguard against sudden vacancies to protect institutional stability. The vice presidency had been abolished in 1972, and this was the first major constitutional amendment since the abolition of term limits in 2008. [Source]

Ministry of Women's Affairs and Family (Minister Marie-Thérèse Abena Ondoa) (2026-10-15) — Recorded approximately 70 women killed for gender-related reasons in 2026 (government estimate: at least 50 in 2023, 67 in 2024). The minister described the cases of femicide as 'brutal and concerning' and pledged an expanded government response. A bill criminalizing femicide separately is scheduled to be submitted to parliament in November. [Source]

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Pattern — Self-Similarity and Resonance

1991-06~ (Bill Motee · 'Ghost Towns' Movement)

The opposition demanded the convening of a National Conference and launched nationwide closures of shops and transport and a refusal to pay utility bills and taxes in June 1991. A government official stated that tax revenues fell by 30% in July–August. Police arrested opposition leaders, and reports emerged of civilian deaths in protests in Douala and elsewhere. Public servants did not participate, and oil revenues continued to fund the military. The movement was strong in Buea and Bamenda but ineffective in Yaoundé.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Economic non-cooperation (fiscal pressure) tactic → Government responds with arrests and existing fiscal sources (maintaining oil revenue and public servant salaries) → Regional mobilization disparities (strong in Anglophone and western areas, weak in the capital). Subsequent protests also repeatedly showed differences in mobilization intensity between the capital and the regions.

  1. Christian Science Monitor, Cameroon 'Ghost Towns' Rouse Public Resentment, 1991-10-01 · Independent media · Lean: Contemporary local reporting by an American daily newspaper — https://csmonitor.com/1991/1001/01051.html

2008-02-25~29 (Fuel Price Hike and Abolition of Term Limits Protest)

A transport strike protesting fuel price hikes coincided with Biya's push to amend the constitution to abolish term limits, leading to violent unrest in 31 cities. Government tallies reported 24 deaths → 40 deaths, opposition claimed over 100 deaths, and over 1,500 arrests (human rights groups reported over 2,000 arrests in Yaoundé alone). The National Assembly abolished term limits later that year.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Simultaneous occurrence of livelihood cost shock + constitutional amendment to extend power → Short-term explosion centered on urban youth → Concurrent crackdowns and mass arrests with concessions on prices and wages → Amendment passed. The 2026 vice presidential amendment is recorded as the first major amendment since 2008.

2016-10~ (Anglophone Crisis)

Following the violent crackdown on protests by Anglophone lawyers and teachers in 2016, separatist forces declared independence in October 2017. Crisis Group tallies reported over 7,000 deaths and approximately 1 million displacements (2026-04), with about 45% of Anglophone schools not operating (OCHA 2026-02). The UN Human Rights Office assessed in January 2026 that the intensity of hostilities had decreased but serious violations continued on both sides.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Protests by professional groups over language and institutional issues → harsh crackdown → transition to armed separatism → long-term low-intensity conflict. The Anglophone-Francophone institutional imbalance dating back to the formation of the federation in 1961 is repeatedly cited as an axis of conflict (Konings & Nyamnjoh 1997).

2025-10~12 (Post-Presidential Election)

Protests broke out in Douala and northern Garoua before and after the announcement of the incumbent's eighth-term victory, with government figures reporting 16 deaths versus 48 deaths reported by UN sources and over 55 deaths claimed by opposition. A prominent opposition figure died in custody, and the runner-up fled to The Gambia via Nigeria.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Disputing election results → urban protests (including the runner-up's base in the north this time) → real-steel crackdowns and arrests of leadership → weakening of mobilization as leaders go into exile. A similar pattern was observed, resembling the post-1992 and 2018 elections dynamics where disputes and arrests continued for the runner-up (in 2018, second-place Kameri was arrested in January 2019).
