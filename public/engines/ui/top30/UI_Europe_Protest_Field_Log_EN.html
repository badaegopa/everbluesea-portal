Summary of Main Text — Spread of European Protests

  1. Order of spread. In the fall of 2026, European protests began in Spain (August, housing) and then spread from France (ignited on 9/30) to Belgium (10/7), Italy, and Portugal. Belgium followed France almost identically a few days later (maximum correlation 0.89).
  2. The epicenters and countries of spread had different causes. The new austerity (54 billion euros), low trust (21%), and four prime ministerial changes were unique to France. What distinguished the places that caught fire was high debt and high interest rates (AUC 0.94) and a shared language zone (0.93). Greece, with more fuel, and Germany, with lower trust, did not catch fire by the third week of October (the first student and teacher cost-of-living marches in three Greek cities opened on 10/21).
  3. Who goes first, and when does it turn into riots? Among the 43 campaigns, all five that turned into riots were youth/student-led and met with repression. Union-led protests remained peaceful. France's riot conversion rate is 18.5 per 100 protest reports.
  4. Recurrence. In 40 European campaigns from 2010 to 2025, policy withdrawals led to recurrence within 24 months in 0/6 cases, while forcible implementation without concessions led to recurrence in 7/12 cases. The government response in 2026 involved repression (29) roughly twice as often as concessions (16).
  5. Common denominator. When the state's decisions descend onto the daily lives of ordinary people (in 2026, 63% targeted specific state decisions), and when the channels to voice opposition to those decisions are blocked, youth emerge first, and when met with repression, riots ensue.
  6. Read as an axiom. It was not the quantity of gunpowder (BSLI) but the absorber binary (A2 institutional trust · A8 governance) that divided. Where government trust dimmed below 20%, governance bypassed parliament to attack institutional trust, triggering brittle fracture (A9) — among 26 countries, only France at 9% and Greece at 19% fell below 20%, and only France, where internal conflict had commenced, broke.
  7. Difference from Southeast Asia. European conflicts remain within the institutions through protests and strikes, fighting over living costs. In Southeast Asia, armed conflicts fight over boundaries and belonging in state-formation periods. However, cost-of-living urban unrest (Indonesia, Philippines, Timor-Leste) resembles both regions.

The reporting indicators of this main text (surge ratio · self-reference n) are recalculated daily, and the self-reference n and lag are crude approximations that fluctuate whenever the data end is extended by one day.

Collection Limitations: Of 556 raw items (516 unique articles after duplicates removed), 279 were used as event sources, and 237 were excluded. Exclusion criteria: sports/military 'strikes', regions with the same name (e.g., Georgia, USA), events in other regions (Middle East, Ukraine), commentaries/columns/travel alerts/market articles, retrospectives of events before 10-20 (e.g., the Liège riots, retrials of Northern Ireland race riots), crowd clashes at football/basketball matches (Partizan–Crvena Zvezda), aggregators whose reliability could not be verified (shattered.io and tech-insider's reports of '16 arrests' in Belgium), and protests against Iranian executions abroad. Collection Limitations: Raw data consisted only of Google News RSS headlines and links for the most recent day, and because the links were news.google.com redirects, WebFetch could not open the articles; AP and Guardian originals were also blocked from access, so figures were confirmed only from headlines and the English Wikipedia (2026_European_unrest, 2026_French_student_protests collected on 10-23). Events lacking dates in headlines and unconfirmed in body text are marked date='Unknown' (mostly reports from 10-21–22). The lang of sources follows the collection query language notation (some Albanian outlets use English headlines). Queries for GBR and IRL have few relevant articles, and jurisdictional boundaries in Northern Ireland are unclear. The 2026_French_protests article is a homonym and contains no content.

Limitations

  1. News searches cover up to 1 day of recent results per country, with a maximum of 50 items per party; exposure to articles varies by search algorithm and language. Smaller local actions may be missed.
  2. Event bundling and summaries are based on collected article headlines and partial bodies. Events with only one source are marked 'Single Source.' Paywalls and blocked articles were not accessed.
  3. Participation estimates from organizers, police, and media differ widely; the stated figure was used.
