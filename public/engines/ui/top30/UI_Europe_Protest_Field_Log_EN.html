Summary of Main Text — Spread of European Protests

  1. Order of the spread. In the fall of 2026, European protests began in Spain (August, housing) and then spread from France (ignited on 9/30) to Belgium (10/7), Italy, and Portugal. Belgium followed France almost identically a few days later (maximum correlation 0.89).
  2. The epicenters and countries of transition had different causes. The new austerity (54 billion euros), low trust (21%), and four prime ministerial changes were unique to France. What separated the places where protests spread was high debt and high interest rates (AUC 0.94) and shared language zones (0.93). Greece, with more fuel, and Germany, with lower trust, did not spread by the third week of October (the first student and teacher cost-of-living marches in three Greek cities opened on 10/21).
  3. Who went first, and when did it turn into riots? Among the 43 campaigns, all five that turned into riots were youth/student-led and met with repression. Union-led protests remained peaceful. France's riot conversion rate is 18.5 per 100 protest reports.
  4. Recurrence. In 40 European campaigns from 2010 to 2025, policy withdrawals led to recurrence within 24 months in 0 of 6 cases, while forcible implementation without concessions led to recurrence in 7 of 12 cases. The government response in 2026 involved repression (29) roughly twice as often as concessions (16).
  5. Common denominator. When the state's decisions descend into the daily lives of ordinary people (in 2026, 63% targeted specific state decisions), and when the channels to voice opposition to those decisions are blocked, youth emerge first, and when met with repression, riots occur.
  6. Read as an axiom. It was not the quantity of gunpowder (BSLI) but the absorber binary (A2 institutional trust · A8 governance) that divided. Where government trust dimmed below 20%, governance bypassed parliament to attack institutional trust, triggering brittle fracture (A9) — among 26 countries, only France at 9% and Greece at 19% fell below 20%, and only France, where internal conflict had begun, broke.
  7. Difference from Southeast Asia. European conflicts remain within the institutions through protests and strikes, fighting over living costs. In Southeast Asia, armed conflicts fight over boundaries and belonging in state-formation periods. However, cost-of-living urban unrest (Indonesia, Philippines, Timor-Leste) resembles both regions.

The reporting indicators of this main text (surge ratio · self-here n) are recalculated daily, and the self-here n and lag are rough approximations that fluctuate whenever the data end is extended by one day.

Collection Limitations: Of 556 raw items (516 unique articles after duplicates removed), 279 were used as event sources and 237 were excluded. Exclusion criteria: sports/military 'strikes', regions with the same name (e.g., Georgia, USA), events in other regions (Middle East, Ukraine), commentaries, columns, travel alerts, market articles, retrospectives of events before 10-20 (e.g., the Liège riots, trials of the Northern Ireland race riots), clashes between football or basketball spectators (Partizan–Crvena Zvezda), aggregators whose reliability could not be verified (shattered.io, tech-insider's reports of '16 arrests' in Belgium), and protests against Iranian executions abroad (e.g., protests in Germany). Collection Limitations: Raw data consisted only of Google News RSS headlines and links for the most recent 1 day; because the links were news.google.com redirects, WebFetch could not open the articles, and AP and Guardian originals were also blocked from access, so figures were confirmed only from headlines and the English Wikipedia (2026_European_unrest, 2026_French_student_protests, collected editions of 10-23). Events lacking dates in headlines and unconfirmed in body text are marked date='unknown' (most reported on 10-21~22). The source lang follows the collection query language notation (some Albanian outlets use English headlines). Queries for GBR and IRL have few relevant articles, and jurisdictional boundaries within Northern Ireland are unclear. The 2026_French_protests article is a homonym and contains no content.

Limitations

  1. News searches retrieve up to 1 day's worth of recent results or up to 50 items per ruling party, and the exposed articles vary by search algorithm and language. Small local actions may be missed.
  2. Event bundling and summaries are based on collected article headlines and partial bodies. Events with a single source are marked 'single source.' Paywalls and blocked articles were not accessed.
  3. Participation estimates from organizers, police, and media differ widely, and the stated figure was adopted.
