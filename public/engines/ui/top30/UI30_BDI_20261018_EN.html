1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

In the June 5, 2025 general election, CENI announced that the ruling CNDD-FDD had won 96.5% and taken all directly elected seats, while major opposition candidate Agathon Rwasa and CNL candidates were barred from running. On October 6, 2026, the UN Human Rights Council extended the mandate of the Burundi Special Rapporteur by 22 to 7 (with 17 abstentions), and the government has refused cooperation; the next presidential election is scheduled for May 3, 2027. The IMF tallied foreign reserves at $214 million (1.6 months of imports) at year-end 2025, and FEWS NET reported a sharp decline in fuel imports and rising stock prices from March 2026 onward. The border with Rwanda has been closed since January 2024, and public mutual recriminations between the two heads of state have continued over the M23 conflict in eastern Congo.

2015

Recurring structure (observation): A case where questions of term limits and eligibility to run led to large-scale protests and crises during the electoral phase

2005~2016

Analysis holds that while the consociational constitution after the Arusha Accords reduced the political salience of ethnic cleavages, subsequent crises unfolded along lines of political division (Reyntjens 2016)

Recurring structure (observation): A structure is observed in which the main fault line of conflict shifts from ethnic to political lines between the ruling party and the opposition

  1. Reyntjens, Africa Spectrum 51(2) — https://journals.sub.uni-hamburg.de/giga/afsp/article/view/978

2020~2027

Rwasa, the runner-up in the 2020 presidential election, was dismissed as party leader in 2024 and barred from running in the 2025 general election and the 2027 presidential election

Recurring structure (observation): The eligibility of a major opposition figure to run is repeatedly restricted through administrative and judicial procedures in each electoral cycle
