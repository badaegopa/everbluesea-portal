1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

In the July 28, 2024 presidential election, the CNE announced a victory for Maduro (51.20%), but opposition tallies, the Carter Center, and UN experts said the results could not be verified, and on January 10, 2025, Maduro took office for his third term. On January 3, 2026, U.S. military operations led to the arrest of Maduro and his wife and their transfer to New York; Delcy Rodríguez has been leading the government as acting president (since January 5). Since then, a pardon law (February 2026), the release of political prisoners, general licenses for oil from the United States and the NABEP oil agreement, and U.S.-mediated talks between the government and the National Assembly of 2015 (3rd round in October) have been underway, but as of October 16, 2026, no presidential election schedule has been set. The Porló Penal tally lists 322 political prisoners (as of 2026-10-05), and the twin earthquakes of June 24 (M7.2 and M7.5) resulted in 5,069 deaths according to government announcements (as of July 17).

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

1989-02~03 (Caracazo)

Following an IMF-supported austerity reform package, protests by residents of poor neighborhoods in Caracas escalated into looting. President Pérez imposed a curfew and deployed tanks; the military fired on crowds. Initial official death tolls were approximately 300; a 2003 government statement put it at approximately 1,000. In its 1999 ruling, the Inter-American Court of Human Rights ordered the publication of a list of 276 victims.

Recurring structure (observation): Fiscal and price shock (austerity) → large-scale spontaneous unrest among urban poor → suppression by military intervention. Observed as the starting point of a structure in which the military appears as the ultimate means of maintaining domestic order in subsequent events.

  1. Al Jazeera, Relief for Venezuelan riot-hit families, 2003-11-12 · Independent media · Orientation: Qatari state-funded international broadcaster — https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2003/11/12/relief-for-venezuelan-riot-hit-families

1992-02-04 (Coup Attempt)

A coup attempt led by Chávez and other lieutenants failed. It was the first major coup attempt since 1962 in a democratic regime that had persisted since 1958.

Recurring structure (observation): Three years after the Caracazo, a faction within the military intervened directly in politics. A structure in which internal divisions within the military become channels for political change during a phase of rising social discontent.

2002-04 (Coup and Chávez's Return) and 2002-12–2003 Oil Strike

At least 13 people died in anti-government protests amid a conflict over the replacement of PDVSA management; on April 12, military commanders ousted Chávez. Acting President Carmona dissolved Congress after taking office and lost military support, resigning on April 13; Chávez returned on the 14th. In December of the same year, an opposition general strike hit oil production and exports.

Recurring structure (observation): Conflict over control of the state oil company → mobilization of the masses → the military's shift within 48 hours reversed the outcome. A case in which whether the military withdrew its support determined the survival of the regime.

2014-02 and 2017-04–09 (Anti-Government Protests)

Nationwide protests began on February 12, 2014; HRW documented 45 cases and over 150 victims. Following the Supreme Court's attempt to seize legislative power in April 2017, months of protests ensued, with dozens killed, hundreds injured, at least 5,400 detained, and at least 757 civilians prosecuted by military courts (HRW · Penal foro).

Recurring structure (observation): Weakening of institutional checks (judiciary replacing legislative power) → prolonged street protests → parallel crackdowns by security forces and pro-government armed groups (collectivos), use of military courts. A pattern in which protests ease while the military remains loyal.
