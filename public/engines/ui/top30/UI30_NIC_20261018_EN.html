3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

1979 (Sandinista Revolution)

The Sandinista Revolution overthrew the Somoza dictatorship, bringing Ortega to power for the first time. Ortega returned in 2007 after serving as president from 1985 to 1990.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A cycle is observed where long-term rule through a family dynasty (Somoza) → collapse through armed revolution → re-establishment of long-term rule by the revolutionary leadership. The current government refers to opposition figures as 'Sandinistas' in its speeches.

1999–2007 (Alarcón Pact and Return)

In 1999, the Alarcón pact lowered the threshold for a first-round victory to 35%, and Ortega returned to power in 2007 with 38% of the vote without a runoff.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A path is observed where elites reach power by changing electoral rules through an elite pact, followed by narrowing competitive conditions through subsequent institutional reforms.

2018-04~ (Suppression of Protests)

Large-scale anti-government protests were suppressed by police and pro-government armed groups; UN tallies reported over 300 deaths. ACLED protest events decreased from 977 in 2018 to 3 in 2024. The OAS mission's entry in August 2018 was denied.

Recurring Structure (Observation): A structure is observed where large-scale protests → harsh suppression → leads to the disappearance of protest space (sharp decline in event counts) and large-scale emigration. Responses rejecting external bodies' visit requests were repeated in 2018 and 2026.

In 2021, all seven opposition presidential pre-candidates were detained before the election, followed by Ortega's declaration of victory.
