1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

In the October 9, 2024 presidential election, the Constitutional Council declared Daniel Chande of the Frelimo party elected (65.17%), but the runner-up Venancio Mondlane's camp alleged fraud; civil society group Plataforma de Decisão tallied at least 303 protest-related deaths, 619 total injuries, and 4,228 arrests by January 15, 2025, Chande's inauguration date. In 2026, the new party Anamola led by Mondlane is holding separate consultations outside the 'inclusive national dialogue' organizing committee of the Chande government, and police used tear gas at an August 1 anniversary march for Chelimanee. In Cabo Delgado in the north, attacks on civilians by the Islamic State of Mozambique (ISM) increased to 9 in July, 11 in August, and 19 in September (ACLED), while Rwanda warned it would withdraw its troops if funding was not secured by March. On July 31, the Constitutional Council ruled unconstitutional provisions of the decree-law that had permitted the government's internet shutdown.

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

2024-10 ~ 2025-01 (Post-Election Crisis)

Discontent over disputed presidential election results spread through youth-led protests, with Decidimos tallies reporting over 303 deaths and 4,228 arrests. Police fired on protesters even on the inauguration day.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Dispute over election result reliability → prolonged street protests → police force response and mass arrests, with the outcome confirmed by a constitutional body (Constitutional Council).

  2. SWP Megatrends Afrika Spotlight 43, 2024-12-17 · Academic · Orientation: German institute policy analysis — https://www.swp-berlin.org/en/publication/mta-spot43-mozambique-after-the-elections-civil-protests-and-the-risk-of-escalation

2024-10 ~ 2026-07 (Telecommunications Control and Judicial Rulings)

Mobile internet and social media restrictions lasted for weeks during the post-election protest period, with CDD winning its lawsuit. Subsequently, Decree-Law No. 48/2025 of 2025 established the authority to block, but in 2026-07-31 the Constitutional Council ruled it unconstitutional for infringing on parliamentary powers.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Administrative control of telecommunications during a crisis phase → judicial response by civil society → constitutional bodies braking the process (on the basis of legislative authority).

  1. CPJ, Mozambique court nullifies decree allowing government to shut down internet, 2026-08-03 · Civil society organization · Orientation: International press freedom NGO — https://cpj.org/2026/08/mozambique-court-nullifies-decree-allowing-government-to-shut-down-internet/

2017 ~ 2026 (Cabo Delgado Armed Insurgency)

Jihadist insurgency began in Cabo Delgado in the north, the TotalEnergies LNG project was suspended following the 2021 attack on Palma, and Rwandan troops were deployed from 2021 onward. In 2026, Rwanda warned of withdrawal over funding issues, while ISM increased attacks on civilians and ransom kidnappings.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Armed insurgency in resource development areas → security dependent on foreign forces and external funding → direct link between fluctuations in external funding and concerns over local security vacuums.
