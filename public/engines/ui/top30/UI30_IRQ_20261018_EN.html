1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

Following the start of the US–Israeli war on Iran in February 2026, Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz led to a sharp decline in Iraqi crude oil exports from about 100 million barrels in February to approximately 32.1 million barrels in May–June (Ministry of Oil), and part of the July civil servant salaries went unpaid (The National, 2026-08-04). The government of Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi, endorsed by the Coordination Framework, was inaugurated on May 14 with only 14 out of 23 ministries approved; on June 28, anti-corruption arrests in the Green Zone took place, a push for state monopoly over arms (deadline September 30) was launched, and the US withdrawal was completed by September 30. On July 28–29, US-Saudi airstrikes killed at least 20 people at Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF) bases in seven governorates, which the Iraqi National Security Council characterized as a violation of sovereignty. Power shortage protests and crackdowns and arrests recurred around Kut in Wasit Province in June–August, and in early October, the first IS attack after the US withdrawal and a nighttime curfew in the Khasib sub-district of Kirkuk were reported.
