1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

Following the White Army's attack on the government base and the city of Nasir in March 4, 2025, First Vice President Riek Machar was placed under house arrest on March 26, and on September 11, 2025, the Minister of Justice indicted 21 people, including Machar, for treason, murder, and crimes against humanity; the trial has continued through the 114th hearing on September 7, 2026. The National Elections Commission (NEC) announced December 22, 2026 as election day on June 22, 2026, and on September 22, 2026, President Kiir dissolved the transitional unified government and the central and state legislatures immediately after amending the electoral law (removing prerequisites for a census and a permanent constitution) and appointed a caretaker administration. The Machar-aligned SPLM-IO faction (led by Acting Chairman Oyoti Nathaniel) rejected the election plan (2026-08-21), while the Juba-based Stephen Par Kuol faction supports the elections. IPC estimated in April–July 2026 that 78% of the population, or 7.8 million people, were at IPC Phase 3 or higher, and 73,300 were at Phase 5 (catastrophe) (joint FAO·WFP·UNICEF announcement on 2026-04-28).

1-5. Government Announcements

Presidential Republic Order — SSBC Reading (2026-09-22) — Dismissed the Vice President, ministers, and local administrative heads of the transitional unified government; dissolved the central and state legislative councils; appointed caretaker ministers for foreign affairs, defense, interior, justice, national security, finance, and petroleum, as well as caretaker governors for 10 states and 3 administrative areas. The president explicitly stated that he would remain in office 'until the inauguration of the next elected president.' The judiciary and security services were maintained. The previous day, he signed the amendment to the electoral law (removing prerequisites for a census and a permanent constitution). In mid-September, NEC Chairman Gabriel Bol Deng was replaced by Mawut. [Source]

National Elections Commission (NEC) (2026-06-22) — Announced election day as December 22, 2026. By the end of June, an electoral timetable was announced; voter registration is expected to take about one month after commencement, and only part of the approximately $250 million required has been secured. [Source]

Ministry of Information (Minister Athian Wek Athian) (2026-04-21) — No further extension of the transitional period; elections to be held in late 2026; it is appropriate for the next elected government to conduct the census and constitution-making. Security is generally calm, but parts of northern Jonglei are unstable due to armed groups linked to the opposition. [Source]

Ministry of Justice — Indictment Announcement (2025-09-11) — Indicted 21 people in connection with the Nasir incident (8 detained, including Machar). Charges include murder, treason, crimes against humanity, conspiracy, destruction of public property, and financing terrorism. Of the 83 persons investigated, 76 were released for lack of evidence, and 13 remain at large. (Cited in a HRW document as a government announcement) [Source]

Republic Order 36/10/2026 (2026-10-15) — Mandatory prior approval for overseas travel by former and current high-ranking public officials; mandatory written notification to the Ministry of Interior 48 hours before domestic travel. Previous general travel restrictions guidelines were repealed. [Source]

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

January 2012

Suspension of oil production amid conflict with Sudan → sharp decline in state revenue; austerity that largely preserved the military sector, accompanied by large-scale borrowing.

Recurring structure (observation): An oil revenue shock coincides with intensifying political competition within a structure where oil revenues sustain patronage and security spending (de Waal's analysis).

  1. African Affairs — de Waal 2014 · Academic journal · Orientation: Peer-reviewed — https://doi.org/10.1093/afraf/adu028

December 2013

President Kiir announced that he had thwarted a coup led by former Vice President Machar → mutiny, seizure of the capital, Machar's appeal to overthrow the government, and civil war.

Recurring structure (observation): The rivalry between Kiir and Machar escalated into armed conflict following the dismissal of the vice president.

  1. African Affairs — Johnson 2014 · Academic journal · Orientation: Peer-reviewed — https://doi.org/10.1093/afraf/adu020

July–August 2016

Clashes in Juba between government forces and Machar's faction; Machar withdrew and left the country (to the Democratic Republic of the Congo); Kiir dismissed Machar and appointed Taban Deng Gai, a breakaway faction leader, as vice president. UN Security Council approved an additional 4,000 peacekeepers.

Recurring structure (observation): A structure in which the ouster of the first vice president is accompanied by a breakaway faction within the opposition replacing that position.

Nasir attack → Machar's pension, indictment, and trial; SPLM-IO split into the Oyot faction (rejecting elections) and the Par Kuol faction (supporting elections).
