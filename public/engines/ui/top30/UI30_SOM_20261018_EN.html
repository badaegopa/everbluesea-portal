1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

After the parliament passed a constitutional amendment extending federal terms from 4 to 5 years, President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud announced that his term would run until May 15, 2027; opposition groups (the Somali Future Committee) and Puntland and Jubaland argue that his term expired on May 15, 2026 and do not recognize him as president. Following the collapse of the U.S.-UK-mediated Halane negotiations in mid-May, fighting in Mogadishu from June 3 to 5 left 13 dead, 189 wounded, and approximately 12,500 households displaced (Protection Cluster/UNHCR). After federal forces took Baidoa in late March, opposition forces from the former Southwest State of Raxowgaren seized Burhakaba on October 15, and the defense minister announced on October 13 that 260 armed personnel had been removed from an opposition leader's residence. The United States notified UNSOS of a funding cutoff for the end of 2026 in a letter dated July 1, and UN agencies announced on May 15 that 6 million people (31% of the population) were at IPC Phase 3 or higher in April–June 2026 and that famine risk existed in the Burhakaba area.

At the same time, it stated that it would maintain relations with the subnational governments of Jubaland and Somaliland 'for security cooperation and commercial interests' in consultation with the federal government. 2-3. Geopolitical Status

USA (2026-07-03) — In a letter to the AU on July 1, it notified the planned end of UNSOS (most of the AUSSOM redefinition) funding by the end of 2026 and the blocking of further UN logistical support in the Security Council. The letter described that the Somali government had maintained progress against al-Shabab, taken over security functions, but failed to reform the security sector. The United States has spent approximately $2 billion on the UN mission in Somalia since 2007 and more than $1.6 billion on African troops. [Source]

African Union (AUSSOM) (2026-07-03) — AUSSOM approx. 12,000. The AU Executive Council convened an emergency meeting on the mission's future. An anonymous senior African diplomat noted that the decision was 'irreversible' and that the mission was 'over' (AFP). [Source]

FAO·OCHA·UNICEF·WFP (2026-05-15) — In April–June 2026, 6 million people (31%) were in IPC Phase 3 or higher, including approx. 1.9 million in Phase 4 (tripling in one year). Famine risk in the Buurhakaba region of Bay; acute malnutrition in approximately 40% of children under 5. Approximately 1.9 million malnourished children (severely malnourished: 493,000). The 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan was 15.2% funded. [Source]

Israel (2025-12-26) — The first UN member state to recognize Somaliland as an independent state on December 26, 2025. Netanyahu's office referred to the 'spirit of the Abraham Accords.' Trump answered a question about US recognition with 'No.' [Source]

Türkiye, Egypt, AU (2025-12-27) — Türkiye characterized the recognition as 'flagrant interference in internal affairs'; AU Chairperson Yusuf noted that Somaliland is part of Somalia; Egypt jointly condemned it with Türkiye, Somalia, and Djibouti. [Source]

3-1. Recurrence in History — Self-Similarity and Resonance

Layer 3 Historical Patterns — Self-Similarity and Resonance

2009

The Transitional Federal Parliament (TFP) formed under the Djibouti Peace Process extended the original mandate of the Transitional Federal Charter by two years (until August 2011).

Recurring Structure (Observation): The method of extending the term of the governing body through parliamentary resolution rather than an electoral timetable first appeared.

  1. U.S. Department of State 2011 Human Rights Report (ecoi.net) · Government Report · Affiliation: U.S. Government — https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/217680

2011-02~2011-06

The TFP unilaterally extended its own mandate to three years (until August 2014) on February 3, 2011 → Civil society and regional political forces demanded parliamentary reform and elections → On June 9, the president and chairman signed the Kampala Agreement, extending the transition until August 20, 2012.

Recurring Structure (Observation): Unilateral extension by parliament → Backlash from local administrative bodies and external partners → Extension narrowed through an agreement mediated by external parties.

  1. U.S. Department of State 2011 Human Rights Report (ecoi.net entry) · Government report · Affiliation: U.S. government — https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/217680

2021-04

After the election failed, the House of Representatives voted to extend President Farmajo's term by two years (rejected by the Senate). In late April, anti-extension forces in Mogadishu took up positions and blocked roads, clashing with government forces, leaving at least 3 dead and residents displaced. On April 28, the president announced he was abandoning the extension attempt.

Recurring structure (observation): Term extension → Internal military fractures along clan and factional lines, leading to armed standoff in the capital → Withdrawal of extension following domestic and international pressure. The opposition figure who claimed an attack on his home in 2021 is current President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud.
