1-4. Current Situation and Chronology

Following the student-led uprising on August 5, 2024, Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina departed for India, and the transitional government under Muhammad Yunus governed for approximately 18 months; on May 12, 2025, amendments to the anti-terrorism law temporarily banned Awami League activities. On November 17, 2025, the International Criminal Tribunal-1 sentenced Hasina to a suspended death sentence, and India maintains that its request for extradition is 'under legal review.' In the February 12, 2026 general election (with the Awami League excluded; turnout 59.88%), the BNP alliance won 212 seats and the Jatam alliance 77, leading to the formation of the Tarique Rahman cabinet. Since its formation, human rights group ASK has recorded 66 politically related killings and 61 deaths in custody over six months, and protests continue: student protests in more than 13 districts in July 2026, a hunger strike against DP World's 15-year concession contract for the Chittagong port terminal on October 8, and garment workers' wage protests in Dhaka.

Turnout was 59.88%. The July Charter referendum held on the same day passed with 60.26% in favor. [Source]

Exams in five districts of the Chittagong Education Board were suspended. [Source]

The Country Analysis Series reads uprisings and collapses not as events to be prevented by repression, but as turning points in social evolution, dividing the post-ignition path into three branches (Series Vol. 1 §3.2; see original Box 1-1).
