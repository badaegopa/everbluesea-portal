I. Summary

In the first half of 2026, two authoritarian regimes on different continents exposed the same psychological vector. In a society where internal legitimacy has completely collapsed, pressure from external major powers does not cause the classical 'rally-round-the-flag effect' but instead becomes an object of hope projection for the populace. In Venezuela, immediately after the US military's operation to arrest Maduro (currently detained and undergoing trial in New York), 83% of the citizens expressed optimism (Gold Glove face-to-face survey, published by CSIS — US think tank [Considering Atlanticist leanings]), and in Iran, some protestors from the largest uprising since 1979 and the diaspora publicly appealed for US intervention. This phenomenon, in which pressure is absorbed as resilience rather than a repulsive force, is referred to in this report as 'hope-vector inversion'and evaluates its conditions for continuation and bankruptcy by contrasting them with historical patterns.

Key Assessment: ① The hope vector is not a constant but a credit with an expiration date, and Iran has already exposed its first default upon maturity (anti-MoU protests by the diaspora) due to the signing of the US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding (MoU). ② Venezuela is on the previous segment of the same curve, where the presence or absence of an election timetable determines repayment or default. ③ As a result of comparing 11 historical samples, disasters and interventions were not the causes of regime change, but rather functioned as measuring instruments that allow the entire population to simultaneously measure the state of legitimacy below the threshold, with changes occurring after a time lag of 1 to 7 years. ④ The historical end point of an externally propped state is predominantly hostiles' reversal (7 to 26 years) or rapid collapse after the withdrawal of props (Saigon/Kabul), and the common condition for the successful soft-landing group was early elections and rapid transfer of sovereigntywas.

II. Structure of the Two Cases — Radar Comparison

All six axes represent qualitative assessment scores (0–5). The two cases are similar on five axes, but diametrically opposed on two axes: in Iran, the suppression execution capacity (SE_endogenous) remains intact, succeeding in subduing protests through massacres, whereas in Venezuela, SE_endogenous was hollowed out by the external arrest of its leadership, resulting in an extreme value for external dependence. That is, Iran represents a bloody reaffirmation of F(t) < SE_endogenous, while Venezuela represents the outsourcing of SE itself — although the paths differ, the popular hope vector pointed in the same direction (external).

III. Case A — Venezuela: Externally Propped State

On January 3, 2026, Maduro was ousted due to a US military operation in Caracas, and the Delcy Rodríguez transitional government was established under US support. The June 24 M7.2+7.5 doublet earthquake (the strongest since 1900, with 4,333 deaths — government official, independent verification incomplete) was a measuring instrument that allowed the entire nation to witness the absence of the state: the military failed to deploy during the initial 72 hours, which the International Crisis Group evaluated as "nonexistent to completely inadequate" (cited in Foreign Policy [US foreign policy magazine]), disapproval rate of 63.3% (+5pt), 1,926 protests in the first quarter (a 2.4-fold increase year-on-year, OVCS), and entering an unconstitutional state after the expiration of the constitutional 180-day term (July 3). Nevertheless, the reason it has not ignited is that SE_exogenous — US political recognition, relief resources (airlifted 7 times a week to Amazonas state), mediation of debt restructuring, and physical deterrence forces (confirmed by USNI on July 7: landing platform dock LPD-28 docked at La Guaira, permanent deployment in the Caribbean, projected to stay until 2029) — fills this vacuum.

Assessment: F(t) > SE_endogenous established (endogenous collapse condition met) · F(t) < SE_endogenous + SE_exogenous maintained (ignition deferred) → forced oscillator system / metastable state. Naval deterrence is an asymmetric seal that blocks only the elite coup channel — the accumulation of F(t) in the public square continues.

IV. Case B — Iran: Compressed Cycle After Bloody Suppression

On December 28, 2025, protests ignited by economic hardship (rial collapse and inflation) spread to over 200 cities in 31 provinces — the largest uprising since 1979. On January 8–9, live-ammo massacres took place amid internet blackouts (7,015 confirmed by HRANA, up to 30,000+ cited by local health authorities, 3,117 announced by the government). Security forces remained loyal (House of Commons Library briefing), and the main protesting force was suppressed by mid-January. In this phase, Trump declared, "We are going to save the protesters, locked and loaded," and Crown Prince Pahlavi appealed for international intervention — an instance where the external projection of the popular hope vector was recorded in official documents(the Congressional Research Service [CRS] report directly categorized and analyzed this). On February 28, US-Israel airstrikes commenced, Khamenei died, a ceasefire was declared on April 8, and subsequently, a US-Iran MoU was signed (Chatham House analysis framework: seeking "strategic capitulation").

Empirical observation of the expectation–betrayal gap G(t). In the first week of July, the dissident diaspora held anti-MoU protests in front of US embassies worldwide. Banner in London: "Iranians did not risk their lives for corn and wheat" (The Jerusalem Post [Considering pro-Israel media and pro-Pahlavi coverage bias]). As the US reclaimed the credit granted by the hope vector as leverage for its own purposes (restraining the nuclear program), the first signal of the expectation-to-betrayal transition flashed after six months. Iran serves as a leading indicator of where Venezuela might land if it proceeds toward a managed transition without elections.

VIII. Geopolitical Integration — Two Theaters of the Donroe Doctrine

Integrating both cases into a single strategic map, the common medium of control is energy. In the Caribbean theater, seizing Venezuelan oil is in itself a dual-purpose tool — internally, it is the financial lifeline and extraction target of the transitional government, while externally, it is a means of strangling Cuba (Cuban crude oil imports: Mexico 44%, Venezuela 33%, Russia 10% → zero from Venezuela since January, Mexico suspended, NYT "first effective blockade since the missile crisis"). If two of the anti-US triangular axis (Venezuela-Cuba-Nicaragua) are neutralized, the remaining domino is Ortega's Nicaragua. Panama was already restructured in 2025 under canal pressure, and consequently, the entire Caribbean Sea, encompassing the shipping lanes from Panama to the US East Coast, is returning to being 'America's Mediterranean' — a Mahanian (A.T. Mahan) scheme that Al Jazeera termed the "Donroe Doctrine" [Al Jazeera: Qatari state-run, considering anti-US critical stance] — is in its final phase of completion. In the Middle East theater, the same energy leverage worked in the opposite direction: sanctions on Iran and the Strait of Hormuz risk raised the strategic value of Venezuelan oil through oil prices (March exports recovered to 1 million b/d, overlapping with the Hormuz blockade phase), which is a feedback loop that strengthens extraction incentives in the Caribbean theater.

The Russian Variable: Depleted by the war in Ukraine, Russia could not significantly reinforce either Cuba (its original 10% share) or Iran (its capacity for air defense support). A unipolar intervention environment devoid of sponsor competition — This is the condition of 2026, which is different from 1953 or 1963, and is also the background condition under which hope-vector inversion occurred simultaneously (absence of an alternative sponsor = the US is the only variable for both the people and the regime).

SYNTHESIS — Observer Standpoint 4-Stage Synthesis

Stage 1 · Structure — Both regimes met the endogenous conditions for exceeding the F(t) threshold, but their endings diverged: in Iran, the active SE_endogenous (security force loyalty) forced the release of F through massacre, while in Venezuela, SE_exogenous (the US) replaced the extinguished SE_endogenous. The former is a bloody reconfirmation, the latter is the outsourcing of deterrence.
Stage 2 · Dynamics — The psychological vectors of the public in both countries were equally directed outward (hope-vector inversion). This vector is not support but credit with an expiration date, and the expectation–betrayal gap G(t) determines the derivative of F(t). Iran's anti-MoU protests are the first actual measurement of G(t) default, while Venezuela's 83% optimism is a bond balance that has not yet expired.
Stage 3 · Pattern Comparison — In the 11 historical samples, disasters and interventions were measuring instruments and accelerators, not causes. The endpoints of the external prop are only of three types: hostile inversion (7–26 years), collapse after withdrawal (immediate–2 years), and soft landing (only in the case of early elections + rapid transition). Currently, Venezuela's observed path (extraction first, absence of election timetable, prolonged stationing) aligns with the grammar of the failure group.
Stage 4 · Outlook — Short-term (~7/28): Protest scale and the response of the chain of command to suppression orders are the primary observation points. Medium-term (~end of 2026): Processing the vacancy in the term of office and the Cuban cascade (power grid threshold) proceed in parallel. Long-term (~2029 stationing outlook horizon): As reconstruction was delayed due to the earthquake, the stabilization window has been pushed back, and without sovereign redemption before expiration (elections), the Caribbean's 'resilience absorption' phase-transitioning into an Iranian-style 'recoil inversion' is the baseline probability of history. This judgment is left open to falsification under the falsification conditions in §XII.
