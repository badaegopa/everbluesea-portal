4-2. Visible Unrest and Absorption Capacity Measure Different Things

ACLED records events. For an event to be reported and counted, a protest must take place, and there must be media to report it. The Uprising Index does not measure the number of events but rather the capacity of institutions to absorb pressure (A2 institutional trust + A8 governance) and the acceleration of pressure on everyday life (Country Analysis Series Vol. 1 §3.1–3.2). The two values do not move in the same direction because they measure different phenomena.

Among the top 30 countries by Uprising Index, 11 had fewer than 50 protest records in 2025: State of Eritrea, Democratic People's Republic of Korea, Nicaragua, Chad, Equatorial Guinea, Burundi, Turkmenistan, Zimbabwe, Congo, Guinea-Bissau, and the Republic of Mali. Conversely, among the top ranks of 2025 protest records, there are also countries with high absorption upper limits (Table 1-3). Whether a low number of records indicates no pressure or that pressure is unrecorded cannot be determined from this data — that distinction is addressed in the country-specific detailed reports through comparisons of media and government announcements.

Layer 5: Knowledge Frames — Interpretations in Prior Research

5-1. Related Research

  1. Raleigh, C., Linke, A., Hegre, H., & Karlsen, J. (2010). Introducing ACLED: An Armed Conflict Location and Event Dataset. Journal of Peace Research, 47(5), 651–660. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022343310378914
    ACLED's design — the starting point for recording events by unit and classifying actors and types.
  2. Raleigh, C., Kishi, R., & Linke, A. (2023). Political instability patterns are obscured by conflict dataset scope conditions, sources, and coding choices. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 10, 74. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-023-01559-4
    Comparisons showing how scope conditions, sources, and coding choices make unstable patterns appear differently — directly related to the limitations of 1-1 and 4-2.
  3. Davenport, C. (2007). State Repression and Political Order. Annual Review of Political Science, 10, 1–23. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.polisci.10.101405.143216
    Synthesis of research on state repression — a framework for reading the composition of interventions and excessive use of force in 2-2.
  4. Lichbach, M. I. (1987). Deterrence or Escalation? The Puzzle of Aggregate Studies of Repression and Dissent. Journal of Conflict Resolution, 31(2), 266–297. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022002787031002003
    Conflicting aggregate studies on whether repression reduces or increases resistance — grounds for not interpreting 2-2 and 3-2 causally.

5-2. Sources and Limitations

  1. ACLED public aggregation (five country-year types, country-month political violence, six regional weekly zones) — file list in 1-1. Armed Conflict Location & Event Data (ACLED), www.acleddata.com.
  2. Uprising Index ranking and absorption upper limit — calculated by the Institute's Country Analysis Series Λ¹² v2.0 and BSLI auxiliary time series v1.2, selecting the top 30 countries (an approximation of the SE upper bound of the UI canonical text).

Limitations: (1) Differences in years of inclusion — global comparison covers only 2021–2025. (2) Records depend on reporting — countries with restricted media access are undercounted. (3) Aggregates may be retroactively adjusted. (4) The weekly aggregation dates follow ACLED notation, and the 2025–2026 comparison was aligned to the same number of weeks. (5) This report uses only sums, shares, and ranks of raw values; it does not create new indices or formulas. It addresses only Layers 1 through 5 of the Six-Layer Discourse and makes no prescriptions, moral evaluations, or predictions.
