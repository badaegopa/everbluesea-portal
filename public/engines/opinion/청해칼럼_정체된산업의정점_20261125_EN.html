Opinion · Cheonghae Column

The Peak of a Stagnant Industry!

What Capital Points To

Cheonghae (Giseub Kim) · Everblue Sea Institute for Social Dynamics · 2026-11-25

1. What the Crack Revealed

On January 28, international gold prices hit $5,589 per ounce during trading.

Domestic Korean Exchange gold was 269,810 won per gram on January 29.

And on November 24, the same gold became 156,760 won.

42% vanished in ten months.

Nine out of eleven months ended with declines.

I also hold that gold.

The original purpose was not a 100% investment.

I wanted to read the global macro capital flows that move gold prices.

However, this year has diverged from the early-year expectations due to overlapping geopolitical factors.

Therefore, these numbers are not someone else's story to me.

But this article is not about complaining about my losses.

It is an article looking at where capital points in the place where gold fell.

2. The Corruption of the Name 'Safe Asset'

People called gold a safe asset.

They learned that it rises during wars and holds up when stock markets collapse.

This year, that formula broke three times.

While war in the Middle East has continued for nine months, gold declined.

While the US Federal Reserve raised interest rates again, gold fell further.

On November 20, when the US S&P 500 index fell by 3.7% in a single day and the fear index jumped by 26%, gold futures also fell by more than 4% together.

On the same day, 97 billion won worth of gold was traded on domestic exchanges.

It was three times the previous day's volume.

People did not try to protect gold; they sold gold to create cash.

This was to cover losses elsewhere and to replenish margin requirements.

Gold was no longer a precaution against crisis.

It was a cash box to be taken out and used when needed.

However, that box shrank by 42% in ten months.

During the same period, US money market funds yielded interest rates in the 3% range.

For individuals to entrust their reserve funds, gold was the most expensive choice.

3. Buyers Did Not Wait

The amount bought and sold in the market is always the same.

If 100g was sold, someone bought 100g.

Therefore, what should be of interest is not quantity but price.

The price at which transactions took place fell by 42% over ten months.

This means that fewer people wanted to buy at the same value.

Some say buyers are waiting for cheaper prices.

I do not see it that way.

If there had been a thick layer of buyers waiting, the decline would have stopped at a 20% or 30% drop.

On days when prices fall, trading surges; on days when prices rise, fewer follow to buy.

Gold prices rebounded on November 23, but almost erased that rebound the next day.

This is not a market waiting.

It is a market from which buyers have withdrawn.

4. The Only Thing Changed in the Trading Floor Is the Owner

Then, did the money sold for gold disappear?

No.

Money only changed positions within the trading floor.

Looking at data from the US futures market, speculative funds exited gold while commercial participants took it in.

Producers and refiners who mine and handle gold have put on the least amount of net short hedges since 2014 to prepare for future declines.

Central banks bought 321 tons in Q3 alone.

In the same week, $59.2 billion flowed into global equity funds, while $13.9 billion flowed out of global money market funds.

It appears that money from gold and cash moved into stocks.

Physics has conserved quantities.

Gold on the ground is approximately 224,000 tons, and trading does not add or subtract even a single gram.

What changed are the owners and the last transaction price.

From the January peak to now, the total gold on the ground has lost an estimated $1.5 trillion in valuation from the books.

However, that $1.5 trillion is not money that flowed elsewhere.

It is a number erased from the books as the last price changed.

In 1991, William Sharpe proved in his paper that if the total returns of all market participants are summed before subtracting costs, it equals the market average, and when costs are subtracted, it must be lower than that.

The exchange is a zero-sum game, and with tolls taken, it becomes a negative-sum game.

The trading floor may be closed, but there is something leaking at the boundaries.

There are tolls taken every time exchanges, brokerage firms, and the state move back and forth.

There is credit that disappears along with positions built on borrowed money when they are liquidated.

5. The Flow Has Been Blocked

Now it is time to broaden the view.

What has been broken this year is not gold alone.

Silver fell 64% from its January peak.

Bitcoin fell more than half from last year's high.

The Nasdaq fell 5.3% in a single day on November 20, giving back all of its gains for the year.

Bonds declined in value as real interest rates rose to their highest in 24 years.

On November 19, the real yield on US 10-year inflation-linked Treasury auction was 2.903%.

The highest since 2002.

European real estate is caught between rent caps and high interest rates.

Capital has no reason to exist if it cannot generate returns.

However, there is no stable return channel visible anywhere now.

I want to call this 'the flow has been blocked.'

In other words, capital has lost its place to invest.

6. Blocked Capital Changes Its Nature

Capital without a place to flow does not stop.

It is forced into narrow channels or turns toward the weaker side.

I want to call this the brutality of capital.

This brutality is already revealing itself.

First, it creates swamps in places where people should not be absent.

It is a leap, but so are housing, energy, and food.

According to Eurostat, in the European Union, between 2010 and the first quarter of 2025, house prices rose by 57.9% while rents rose by 27.8%.

Thomas Piketty argued that if the rate of return on capital remains higher than the rate of economic growth for a long time, the share of income from capital grows faster than the share of income from labor.

This is how a structure is created in which it is difficult to accumulate wealth through labor.

Second, it is harvested as interest.

The US government pays more than $1 trillion in interest to bondholders each year.

It is larger than defense spending.

The income of the indebted side moves to the side that holds cash through interest.

Third, it creates profits by reducing the cost of labor.

If profit cannot be made from sales, profit is made by reducing the number of people.

Restructuring and automation point in the same direction.

Fourth, it relies on the state.

It obtains subsidies, tax breaks, and regulatory easing.

This scene repeats itself in every region trying to attract a data center.

Electricity and water are provided by the region, but profits and jobs do not return in proportion.

The lives of ordinary people are stripped bare between them every day.

Taxes, installments, rent, and children's education expenses are the channels.

The institutional, political, and leadership circles have already outpaced the lives of ordinary people, so they do not feel their pain well.

I want to call this the cruelty of capital.

It seeks to penetrate the place with the lowest resistance.

7. The Pack and the Net

What is shaking the stock market now is artificial intelligence.

However, looking at the world economy as a whole, artificial intelligence is still an extremely limited industry.

More than 30% of the market capitalization of the US S&P 500 index is concentrated in a few large technology stocks, but the share of artificial intelligence in the real economy cannot be compared to it.

A publicly traded artificial intelligence company this year earned $16.3 billion in the first half and recorded net losses of $80.7 billion on its books.

It is an industry that absorbs capital rather than one that generates profits.

Why does such a small industry sway the stock market?

I see it in the pack.

Individuals and general companies rush in to hunt for prey.

Credit financing purchased stocks with borrowed money exceeded $1 trillion in the US.

Even when semiconductor stocks fell by more than 10% in a day, $7 billion flowed into technology funds.

The speed of rushing does not slow down even if signals that the prey is dwindling emerge.

Large capital does not step forward.

Outside the net, quietly watching fish rush into the net.

Berkshire Hathaway accumulated cash assets of approximately $382 billion in the third quarter last year.

More than $7 trillion is waiting in US money market funds.

History records the scene of the net being raised.

During the 1907 panic, J.P. Morgan rescued collapsing banks and acquired industrial assets on his own terms.

In 2008, Buffett invested in a struggling Goldman Sachs with a 10% annual dividend.

At the moment of the crash, those holding cash set the price and conditions.

They decide when to raise the net and when the pack of wolves takes its last breath.

8. The Dagger That Strikes from the Shadows

This net has a name.

It is shadow finance, the channel through which money flows outside regulation.

According to the Financial Stability Board, financial assets outside banks amounted to $25.68 trillion at the end of 2024, accounting for 51% of global financial assets.

They grew twice as fast as banks in 2024 alone.

Among them, private credit is a size that even regulators do not know accurately.

This year, cracks appeared in that private credit.

In the first half of the year alone, $12.7 billion in redemptions came from non-listed private debt funds, and redemption requests poured into some large funds at nearly twice the quarterly limit.

It was out of concern that the tech companies they had lent to were being shaken by artificial intelligence.

I believe that shadow capital exists.

It is not a single hand, but several hands that feed on the hopes and disappointments of the masses.

They make money when expectations swell, and they make money even when disappointment extinguishes them.

And behind those who succeed, they strike with a dagger when the time comes.

In 1929, individuals who had bought with debt held on to the end.

In 2000, as insiders of dot-com companies dumped their shares, the Nasdaq fell by 78%.

This cannot be stopped.

Hyman Minsky believed that when stability lasts long, people take on greater risks, and as a result, instability arises within the system itself.

The same picture exists in physics.

According to self-organized criticality revealed by physicists such as Per Bak in 1987, if sand is dropped grain by grain, the sandpile approaches an angle just before collapsing, and no one knows when or how big a collapse will occur.

If small collapses are continually prevented, sand accumulates until a larger collapse occurs.

In 2008 and 2020, the hands of central banks that supported the market did not remove the sand but postponed it.

9. Gold Will Be the Last Prey of Capital

Gold has been money since ancient times.

Therefore, I believe that gold will be the last prey of capital.

History shows this sequence.

After the stock market collapsed in 1929, the US government in 1933 collected private gold at $20.67 per ounce under Executive Order 6102.

The following year, the official price was raised to $35.

At the end of the collapse, gold was literally the prey that was taken away.

The silver denarius of Rome saw its silver content fall from 95% to below 5% over about 300 years.

After money was consumed by dilution, Constantine rebuilt it with the gold solidus.

After the dot-com bubble burst, gold rose sevenfold from its 2001 trough to its 2011 peak.

During the 2008 financial crisis, gold fell by 30% from its year's high and then rose 2.6 times by 2011.

The order was always the same.

The winner's assets collapse.

Gold is sold along with them.

In the end, capital rushes into gold.

Gold in November this year stands on that second step.

10. Technology Does Not Disappear but Continues Its Lineage

This does not mean that artificial intelligence is declining.

I remember the technology once called 'my computer'.

There was a time when the technology that reduced the functionality of PLCs to a single chip had an advantage.

That lineage continued through Arduino and Raspberry Pi.

Now, those chips are produced in the billions each year, becoming components costing less than $1, becoming parts of washing machines, cars, and toys.

Technology does not disappear.

It simply becomes invisible.

Artificial intelligence will follow that path as well.

It will be transplanted into some companies' products and consumed.

And from the minds of a few extraordinary developers, the next generation of success myths will emerge.

That signal is already visible.

Last January, when a single open model trained at low cost emerged, Nvidia's market capitalization vanished by $593 billion in a single day.

The cost of using artificial intelligence with the same performance has fallen by roughly one-tenth each year.

Artificial intelligence itself has already survived two winters.

In the 1970s and 1980s, research funding dried up, but the lineage continued among a few researchers.

In 2012, three researchers opened the era of deep learning, and in 2017, a single paper written by eight people gave birth to today's language models.

Carlota Perez summarized that in each of the past five technological revolutions, a frenzy of financial capital came first, and only after the bubble burst did productive capital spread the technology throughout society.

The bubble is not the end of technology but a sign that installation has ended.

However, there is a price to pay.

British railways in the 1840s survived, but most shareholders who funded the railway mania lost their money.

In the late 1990s, only about 3% of the capacity of optical cables laid by telecommunications companies was actually used in 2001, and WorldCom and Global Crossing went bankrupt.

Data centers could meet the same fate.

Over time, the remaining facilities will either find new owners or weigh down existing owners' management.

Electricity bills, water rates, and maintenance costs are paid every month regardless of utilization rate.

A 2007 study tracking dot-com era startups found that about half survived until 2004.

However, most of the value was captured by a tiny minority.

Only the strong survive.

The rest remain challengers.

11. The Hand Behind One Person's Start

The United States always produces a new innovator after a certain period of time.

IBM did, Microsoft and Apple did, and Google, SpaceX, and Tesla did.

It is not a household name, nor is it a Korean conglomerate that was built by pouring decades of time into it.

One person's start created massive success.

Their nationalities are also diverse.

According to the U.S. Chamber of Commerce Foundation's 2022 tally, about 55% of U.S. startups with a valuation of $1 billion or more were founded by immigrants.

However, no matter how advanced the technology, it is difficult to attract success and capital on one's own.

Someone evaluates them, and someone behind the scenes helps them legally and in terms of policy.

The records show this.

SpaceX received a $1.6 billion cargo transportation contract from NASA just before going bankrupt in 2008.

Tesla received a $465 million low-interest loan from the U.S. Department of Energy in 2010.

Funding from the National Science Foundation went into the Stanford Digital Library project, which was the starting point for Google.

Among Palantir's early investors was In-Q-Tel, a venture investment arm of the U.S. Central Intelligence Agency.

Maria A. Machucauto revealed that the roots of the internet, GPS, touch screens, and voice assistants that make up the iPhone were all research funded by the state at risk and with money.

Korea also helped the state.

The other is where that hand touched.

In Korea, the state's hand reached the chaebols, and in the United States, market capital took on the risks of early technology and chose eccentric geniuses and extraordinary individuals.

12. The Masses Learn Not to Believe but to Survive

When the AI craze began earlier this year and now, public perception has changed.

The initial wariness has shifted toward acceptance, and people are trying to learn how to use it.

According to CJ Mezo Media's 2026 survey, 88% of Koreans aged 15 to 59 in major cities had used generative AI, and 43% had subscribed by paying money within the past year.

However, only 38% believe the results.

In a Pew Research Center survey, the number of Americans who were more worried than hopeful increased from 37% in 2021 to 50% in 2025.

Those who use it are increasing, and those who worry are also increasing.

It is not used because they believe.

It is used so as not to fall behind.

Humans are animals that find it difficult to live when they are left behind.

To live within the boundaries of society, those who first mastered new technologies have always been ahead.

Looking at this lineage, the answer also becomes visible.

The construction of data centers around the world can be aggregated in total, and the production and development speed of chips can be controlled.

Only then can one avoid the shock when the bubble bursts.

That movement has already begun from below.

In July, New York State stopped reviewing environmental permits for new data centers with more than 50 megawatts of capacity for up to a year.

Amsterdam banned new data centers until 2030, and Denmark put data centers at the bottom of the queue for grid connections.

The limits of the power grid and local opposition brought regulation.

But there are gaps in regulation as well.

When the Netherlands bundled large data centers into two regions, a giant corporation raised three buildings to avoid that standard.

Bundling the total raises the value of already-built data centers, and those who have already established themselves become beneficiaries of regulation.

Even regulation can become new prey for shadow capital.

Capital always moves to places with lower resistance and fewer regulations.

13. Bubbles Are Not Created by Anyone

Bubbles are not created by anyone.

They are created by public expectations and disappointment.

Robert Shiller saw that bubbles begin with stories that spread like a contagion, and when those stories fade, the bubble dies.

Now, actual usage by the public is steadily rising, while financial market expectations are wavering at their peak.

Bubbles arise from the gap between those two curves.

If usage increases but expectations far outpace it, that difference is what will be extinguished.

And there exists shadow capital that feeds on that gap.

14. Seen from Another Position

I also record opposing views to my own thoughts.

First, risk-free assets now yield nearly 3% even after stripping out inflation.

This means there is a haven for capital.

The most violent surge of capital without a place to go was actually in the 2010s when interest rates were at 0.

In 2020, when negative-yielding bonds reached $18 trillion, money flowed everywhere.

Second, large capital is not one hand.

More than half of US fund assets are passive money that simply follows indices, and pension funds and large institutions automatically hold AI stocks in proportion to their index weight.

A significant number of those holding the net are already within it.

Lehman Brothers in 2008 was like this.

Third, AI may differ from microcomputers.

Because of its nature where usage explodes as prices drop, data centers could become under-supplied rather than surplus equipment.

Fourth, while it was the state that took gold away in 1933, today's states stand on the side buying gold.

After Russia's foreign exchange reserves of approximately $300 billion were frozen in 2022, central banks accumulated gold without anyone's debt.

Gold became the world's second-largest reserve asset after the euro based on market value in 2024, right behind the dollar.

Individuals sell gold while states buy it.

Whether gold will become prey at the end of the next collapse or whether states will stand against capital with gold in hand remains open.

15. What Capital Points To

Let's summarize.

Industry has stagnated, and only speculation remains.

Gold has lost its name as a safe asset and become a cash box, and those who wanted to buy have left.

Capital has lost where to flow, and its brutality flows through weak channels: swamps, interest, and labor cost compression.

The wolves rush toward narrow prey, and capital outside the net chooses its timing.

And at the end of that cycle, capital will point to gold again.

To that which has been money since ancient times.

Still, technology does not disappear.

A few extraordinary minds take up the next lineage where the bubble has burst.

Human technology and civilization have always evolved this way.

There are five things I will watch.

Does the US real interest rate peak and decline?

Does the Fed speak of its last hike?

Does the stock market's liquidation end?

Does the volume on a day when gold rises exceed that on a day when it falls?

And on that day, does gold stand alone when the assets of the winners collapse?

When that day comes, where capital points will be clear.

Still, I will track the movements of gold and global capital with the goal of 10 years.
